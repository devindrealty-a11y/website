## Post 2 — Week of October 20, 2026

**From:** the same October 10 article (deposits and the written agreement).

**Button:** Learn more

**URL:** `https://devindesaulniers.ca/blog/2026-10-10-how-to-rent-in-vancouver-before-you-move.html?utm_source=google&utm_medium=business-profile&utm_campaign=weekly-post&utm_content=02-deposits-and-agreement`

Sign a written tenancy agreement, and pay only the deposits British Columbia allows. The province says a tenancy agreement is a contract. Landlords must prepare one for every tenancy. Both of you sign and date it. It needs the Act's standard terms, your names, the address, the start, and whether it is month to month or fixed term, usually one year. A term that ignores the Act cannot be enforced. Photograph the pages. You must receive a copy within 21 days.

On deposits, the province says a security deposit can be at most half of one month's rent. In the province's example, rent of $1,500 means a security deposit of no more than $750. A pet damage deposit, if pets are allowed, is also capped at half a month. No monthly pet fee, and no fee just to process an application. Once the security deposit is paid, the tenancy is established. You have 30 days to pay it. Keep the receipt.

## Post 3 — Week of October 27, 2026

**From:** the same October 10 article (the scam section).

**Button:** Learn more

**URL:** `https://devindesaulniers.ca/blog/2026-10-10-how-to-rent-in-vancouver-before-you-move.html?utm_source=google&utm_medium=business-profile&utm_campaign=weekly-post&utm_content=03-rental-scams`

Newcomers get targeted by rental scams before the move, not only after they land. WelcomeBC says that plainly. The Royal Canadian Mounted Police describes a common version: someone advertises a home, says it is in high demand, and asks for a deposit without a viewing, sometimes by electronic transfer, with no meeting. After the money arrives, the messages stop.

Be suspicious, the RCMP says, if the rent is lower than similar places, if there is no formal agreement, if you are asked to send money outside the country, or if the landlord will not meet you and is not named on the papers. Walk through the unit, or join a live video call while someone is standing in it. Check that the photos are not copied from another ad. Do not send money or credit card details until you know what you are paying for.

**URL:** `https://devindesaulniers.ca/blog/2026-10-08-vancouver-september-asking-rents.html?utm_source=google&utm_medium=business-profile&utm_campaign=weekly-post&utm_content=04-september-asking-rents`

Rentals.ca and Urbanation released their October 2026 National Rent Report on October 7. Vancouver apartment and condo asking rents rose 1.4 percent from August, to $2,741, the largest monthly gain among Canada's six largest markets. Year over year, Vancouver was still down 1.3 percent, the 34th straight month of annual declines. The report called those September rents the lowest for that month since 2021.

Across Canada, average asking rent for all property types was $2,034 in September, down 4.2 percent from a year earlier. CBC News reported a 2.9 percent annual drop in B.C. asking rents. For apartments and condos, B.C. was the highest province, at $2,373.

One higher month is not the whole story if you are moving here. Asking rents are still below last September, and they are still high next to most of the country. These are asking rents on vacant listings, which is the number to budget for. They are not CMHC rents for homes people already occupy.

## Post 6 — Week of November 17, 2026

A $190,000 salary is not what you live on. I ran that income through 2026 federal and provincial tax, CPP and EI (QPP and QPIP in Quebec), and 12 months of average one-bedroom asking rent from the September 2026 Rentals.ca and Urbanation report.

On that math, Edmonton left $116,711. Calgary left $113,987. Vancouver left $102,376, within $101 of Ottawa at $102,477. Toronto left $99,465. Halifax was last at $95,336. Vancouver was 8th of the 11 cities. B.C.'s lower tax at this income nearly offsets the highest rent in the country, which is why Vancouver is not at the bottom. Halifax and Montreal finish low mostly because of provincial tax.

These are my figures, not a tax assessment. I used a single filer, basic credits only, a full year in the province, no dependants, and no RRSP. Rent is asking rent on listings, not CMHC in-place rent. Utilities and tenant insurance are not in the number. I would expect a commercial calculator to land within about $500.

If you are moving here, budget from what is left, not from $190,000. I am Devin Desaulniers, REALTOR®, Real Estate Associate with Axford Real Estate. This is not tax advice.

Greater Vancouver REALTORS® published its September 2026 report on October 2. Metro Vancouver had 1,717 sales, 8.4 percent fewer than September 2025 (1,875) and 25 percent below the 10-year average for the month.

The split matters. Apartments: 777 sales, down 18.6 percent. Detached homes: 575 sales, up 4.2 percent. Attached homes: 358 sales, up 0.6 percent. The composite benchmark was $1,075,900, down 5.5 percent from a year earlier. A benchmark is a typical home, not the price of one house.

In the Tri-Cities, BC, the benchmark was $971,700 in Coquitlam (down 6.3 percent), $835,500 in Port Coquitlam (down 8.2 percent), and $1,011,500 in Port Moody (down 5.8 percent). More homes were for sale than a typical September, so buyers have more to compare. I cannot tell you where prices go next.

If you are selling, price from recent sales near you. House and townhouse sales were slightly above last September. Condos were slower, so leave time. New here? Renting first is a sound way to learn the commute.
