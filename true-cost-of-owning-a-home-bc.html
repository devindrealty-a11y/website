The mortgage is the payment people plan for. The bills that keep arriving are property tax, strata fees, home insurance, maintenance and utilities. Property tax · 2026

Property tax in the Tri-Cities, BC

BC Assessment sets the value. The city sets rates per $1,000 of that value and also collects for the school district, TransLink, Metro Vancouver, BC Assessment and the Municipal Finance Authority. Water, sewer and garbage are usually a separate utility bill. In Port Coquitlam, the solid-waste levy is on the tax notice instead.

Port Moody

The city's 2026 tax-rate summary puts the residential grand total at $4.29125 per $1,000. That is $1.57807 for other governments and $2.71318 for the municipal levy, which includes storm drainage. The sheet lists a 3.95% increase in the municipal levy for 2026. School tax on that sheet is $1.1248 per $1,000, the same School District 43 rate as Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam.

The home owner grant

The grant reduces provincial property tax on your principal residence if you qualify and apply. In Metro Vancouver for 2026 the basic grant is up to $570 and the additional grant is up to $845, on an assessed value of $2,075,000 or less. Above that, the grant falls by $5 per $1,000. You must still pay at least $350, or $100 with the additional grant. The estimator applies those rules. It does not check citizenship, residency, or whether the home is your principal residence.

Strata

Strata fees are set by the building

A condo or townhouse in the Tri-Cities, BC is usually a strata. The monthly fee pays for things the owners share: building insurance, common repairs, management, and sometimes heat or water. The fee on one building is a poor guide to the fee on the next. Read the Form B, the budget, the minutes and the depreciation report before you rely on a number.

Relta publishes what it calls a typical monthly fee among listed strata homes that show a fee, drawn from BCRES MLS. Checked October 23, 2026. The page does not date the sample, and it does not split condos from townhouses.

Insurance

Home insurance is a quote, not a city rate

The Insurance Bureau of Canada publishes research on claims and the market. Its public industry-data page does not list an average premium for B.C. homeowners. Premiums move with the rebuild cost, the age and claims history of the home, the deductible, and whether you add earthquake or overland-water coverage.

MyChoice's Home Insurance Outlook for 2026, updated September 24, 2026, used quotes in its own database. It reports an average annual B.C. home-insurance quote of $2,253 for January 2026, down 1.20% from January 2025. The national figure in the same table is $1,343. Dividing $2,253 by 12 is $187.75 a month. That is the placeholder in the estimator. It is not a quote for Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Port Moody, or any other Greater Vancouver address, and the article does not say whether earthquake coverage is included.

Maintenance

A range for repairs, not a local average

CMHC's home-buying guide lists maintenance and repairs as an ongoing cost of owning, along with the mortgage, property tax, insurance, utilities and condominium fees. It says to set aside an emergency fund of about 5% of your income each year. It does not give a percent of the purchase price.

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada's 2012 workbook, Buying and Maintaining a Home, says: as a general guide, budget 1% to 3% of the value of the home a year, then divide by 12. On an $800,000 value, 1% is $8,000 a year, or $666.67 a month. 3% is $24,000 a year. That guide is national and more than a decade old. It is a planning range, not a measured cost for the Tri-Cities, BC.

For a detached house, that range is a way to save for the roof, the furnace, painting and the surprises. For a strata home, a large share of the building work is already in the strata fee. Putting 1% to 3% on the fee counts some of that work twice. The estimator therefore starts condos and townhouses at 0%, and you can raise it for repairs inside your own unit.

Utilities · 2026

Water, sewer, waste, gas and electricity

Metro Vancouver runs the regional water supply and the sewer-treatment plants. Homeowners in the Tri-Cities, BC do not get a separate Metro Vancouver utility bill. The cities collect a share and pass it on. Coquitlam says 53% of its water fee and 46% of its sewer fee go to Metro Vancouver. Port Coquitlam says most of the 2026 increase in water and sewer is from those regional charges. Port Moody itemizes the Metro Vancouver portion on the utility bill.

Natural gas

FortisBC's Mainland and Vancouver Island residential rates, effective July 1, 2026, are:

  • Basic charge: $0.4216 a day
  • Delivery: $8.469 per gigajoule
  • Storage and transport: $2.472 per gigajoule
  • Cost of gas: $1.660 per gigajoule

FortisBC's own example for 8 GJ in a 31-day month is $113.88 before other fees and taxes. On June 16, 2026, FortisBC described about 7.5 GJ a month as the use of an average residential customer. Using the same rates for a 365-day year, that month is $0.4216 × 365 ÷ 12 = $12.82, plus 7.5 × $12.601 = $94.51, or $107.33 before GST and any municipal operating fee. A share of that gas is the designated renewable blend already inside the storage and transport charge. The estimator uses this $107.33 figure when gas use is 7.5 GJ.

Electricity

BC Hydro Rate Schedule 1101, in the Electric Tariff dated September 22, 2026, and on BC Hydro's residential tiered-rate page:

  • Basic charge: 23.44 cents a day
  • Step 1: 11.87 cents per kWh for the first 675 kWh in a monthly bill
  • Step 2: 14.08 cents per kWh after that

The tariff says Step 1 is pro-rated by the day, and that rate riders can apply before tax. The customer page lists these three charges as what the bill shows. GST is extra. If someone used exactly 675 kWh in 30 days, the energy charge would be $80.12 and the basic charge $7.03, or $87.15 before GST. That 675 kWh is the Step 1 threshold, not a published average for the Tri-Cities, BC, so the estimator does not assume it.

Greater Vancouver

The same bills, different city rates

Burnaby, New Westminster, Vancouver and the rest of Greater Vancouver charge the same kinds of costs: property tax on the assessed value, utilities, insurance, maintenance, and a strata fee if the home is a strata. The municipal tax rate and the utility fees are set by each city. This page does not copy those rate sheets, so the estimator does not guess them.

School tax is set by school district. Order in Council 124/2026, made April 7, 2026, sets these 2026 residential rates per $1,000 of taxable value:

  • School District 43, Coquitlam (Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Port Moody, Anmore and Belcarra): $1.1248
  • School District 41, Burnaby: $1.1102
  • School District 40, New Westminster: $1.2349
  • School District 39, Vancouver: $0.9791
  • School District 42, Maple Ridge–Pitt Meadows: $1.2148

School tax is one line on the bill. The municipal rate, TransLink, Metro Vancouver and the home owner grant still have to be applied. BC Hydro and FortisBC rates above are regional, not limited to the Tri-Cities, BC. Strata fees for Burnaby, New Westminster and Vancouver are in the table higher up on this page.

Property transfer tax is a one-time closing cost, not a monthly bill. It is explained in BC property transfer tax and the first-time buyer exemption. First-time buyer programs are on the first-time buyer guide for the Tri-Cities, BC.

FAQ

Questions owners ask

What do I pay each month besides the mortgage?

The mortgage is only one bill. Owners also pay property tax, home insurance, maintenance and utilities. A strata home adds a monthly strata fee. A special levy, when owners vote for one, is extra and is not in the estimator. The figures on this page are published 2026 rates for Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam and Port Moody, or a number you type in. Where no public number exists, the line stays blank.

Is property tax based on the price I pay?

No. BC Assessment estimates market value as of July 1 of the year before the tax year, and the city applies its rates to that assessed value. For 2026, the valuation date is July 1, 2025. The price you pay can be higher or lower than the assessment.

How much is 2026 property tax in the Tri-Cities, BC?

Coquitlam's residential total is $3.8520 per $1,000 of assessed value. Port Moody's is $4.29125 per $1,000. Port Coquitlam's rate portion adds up to $3.7754 per $1,000, and a single-family home also pays a $333 solid-waste levy on the tax notice. On an $800,000 assessment, before any home owner grant, that is $3,081.60 in Coquitlam, $3,433.00 in Port Moody, and $3,020.32 in Port Coquitlam before the $333 levy.

Who can claim the home owner grant?

If the home is your principal residence and you qualify, the basic grant in Metro Vancouver, which includes the Tri-Cities, BC, is up to $570 in 2026 when the assessed value is $2,075,000 or less. It falls by $5 for each $1,000 over that threshold and is $0 at $2,189,000. The additional grant, for seniors, veterans and people with disabilities, is up to $845 and is $0 at $2,244,000. You still pay at least $350 in property tax, or $100 if you qualify for the additional grant. You apply with the Province every year.

What is a strata fee?

It is the monthly payment to the strata corporation for shared costs. The amount is different in every building. It is on the listing and on the Form B Information Certificate, with any special levy the owners have already approved. Among listed strata homes that publish a fee, Relta reports a typical monthly fee of $432 in Coquitlam (821 homes), $423 in Port Coquitlam (228 homes) and $450 in Port Moody (176 homes), as shown on that page when checked on October 23, 2026. The sample mixes condos and townhouses. It is not the fee for a home you are buying.

How much should I budget for maintenance?

CMHC tells owners to plan for maintenance and repairs, and to set aside about 5% of their income each year for emergencies. It does not publish a percent of the home's price. A 2012 guide from the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada says, as a general guide, budget 1% to 3% of the home's value a year and then divide by 12. In a strata, common-property repairs are mostly in the strata fee, so adding that full range as well as the fee can count the same work twice. The estimator starts strata homes at 0% for that reason.

What do utilities cost in the Tri-Cities, BC?

Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam and Port Moody bill flat annual water and sewer fees. Waste is billed differently in each city. The amounts are in the utilities section. BC Hydro's September 22, 2026 tariff charges a basic 23.44 cents a day, then 11.87 cents per kilowatt-hour for the first 675 kilowatt-hours in a month and 14.08 cents after that, before GST. FortisBC's Mainland residential gas rates, effective July 1, 2026, are a basic charge of $0.4216 a day, delivery of $8.469 per gigajoule, storage and transport of $2.472 per gigajoule, and a cost of gas of $1.660 per gigajoule, before tax and municipal operating fees. FortisBC described about 7.5 gigajoules a month as average residential use. This page does not assume an electricity amount.

How much is home insurance?

There is no single premium for the Tri-Cities, BC. The Insurance Bureau of Canada's public industry-data page does not list an average B.C. home premium. MyChoice, using quotes in its own database, reported an average annual B.C. home-insurance quote of $2,253 for January 2026, in an article updated September 24, 2026. That is a provincial quote average, not a quote for your home, and the article does not say whether earthquake coverage is included. Get a quote for the address.

Do these city rates apply across Greater Vancouver?

No. The estimator uses Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam and Port Moody only. Other cities set their own municipal rates. School tax also differs by school district. For 2026, School District 43, which includes the Tri-Cities, BC plus Anmore and Belcarra, is $1.1248 per $1,000. Burnaby (School District 41) is $1.1102, New Westminster (School District 40) is $1.2349, Vancouver (School District 39) is $0.9791, and Maple Ridge–Pitt Meadows (School District 42) is $1.2148. Those figures are school tax only.
