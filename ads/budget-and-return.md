# Budget, cap, and the 2:1 target

All figures are **CAD**. “Spend” means the media cost shown in Meta Ads Manager and Google Ads. It does not include GST or other sales tax the platforms may add to the card charge. Tax is a bookkeeping item, not part of the $1,000 cap and not part of the ratio.

Nothing in this folder spends money. The limits below are what to type in when a person chooses to launch.

## The numbers

| Rule | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly range | $200–$500 |
| **November default** | **$300** |
| Hard cap for the whole test | **$1,000** of platform spend |
| Return target | **2:1 or better** |
| First day | Sunday 1 November 2026 |
| Last day if it is still on | Saturday 31 January 2027, or the day the cap is hit, whichever comes first |

$300 is the default because it sits in the middle of the range and leaves two more monthly decisions before the cap. Starting at $500 would hit $1,000 in about eight weeks, before a relocation rental (a 4–6 week search) has time to finish.

### November (1–30 November 2026)

Thirty days. Set a **campaign spending limit** as well as the daily budget, so a missed Monday cannot blow the month.

| Campaign | Daily budget | November spending limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meta — relocating renters | $6 | $180 |
| Meta — relocating buyers | $2 | $60 |
| Google Search — both ad groups | $2 | $60 |
| **November total** | **$10** | **$300** |

Share of the month: renters 60%, buyers 20%, Google 20%. The renter campaign gets the largest share because one secured placement is $1,500 before GST, and that is the return this budget can actually reach. Buyer files pay more when they close, and they close slowly. Google is a search test, not the engine.

If Ads Manager or Google Ads rejects a daily budget, raise that campaign to the minimum the tool allows and **shorten the end date** so November still lands on the spending limit in the table. Do not raise the monthly total to satisfy a minimum.

### The $200 and $500 alternatives

Use these only by editing the spending limits. Do not run them on top of the $300 plan.

| | Renters | Buyers | Google | Month |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Floor | $120 | $40 | $40 | $200 |
| Default | $180 | $60 | $60 | $300 |
| Top of range | $300 | $100 | $100 | $500 |

The $500 row is for a **later** month, after a Monday check has already cleared the rules in [weekly-roi-check.md](weekly-roi-check.md). It is not the 1 November setting.

### December and January

On the Monday check that falls on or just before the 1st of the next month:

- **Continue at $300** only if the check says continue. Raise each campaign spending limit by another month’s amount ($180 / $60 / $60). Meta’s spending limit is a running total, so November’s $180 becomes $360 for two months, not a fresh $180 that replaces it.
- **Stop** if the check says stop. Leave the unspent limit unspent.
- **January** can take the remainder up to $1,000 ($400 if November and December each spent $300, less if they spent less). Do not schedule January on 1 November.

Maximum path if every check passes: $300 + $300 + $400 = **$1,000**. Stopping at $300 or $600 is a normal outcome.

## What counts as return

**Recognized return** = tenant fees Devin has invoiced, before GST + buyer-side commission Devin has actually received on a file that came from this test, after the brokerage split.

Count both halves of the tenant fee when they are invoiced: the $750 at the start and the $750 when a rental is secured. A completed placement is **$1,500** before GST.

Do not count:

- GST collected on the fee (it is not revenue)
- A buyer commission that is estimated, verbal, or still sitting with the brokerage
- A rental inquiry, a listings request, or a booked call (those are leads, not return)
- Axford’s share of a commission

The site does not publish Devin’s buyer-side split. The Monday sheet has a blank for the dollar amount he received. This plan does not invent one. A closed buyer file is upside. The 2:1 test is built so it can be met on tenant fees alone.

### Ratio

**Ratio = recognized return ÷ platform spend.**

The target is **2.0 or higher** at the end of the test, measured again **60 days after the last ad dollar**. A rental search runs 4–6 weeks, so a November retainer may finish in December. Give the last leads that window, then close the books. Do not keep the ads on during those 60 days unless the cap and the Monday rules still allow it.

| Spend so far | Return that equals 2:1 | A tenant-only way to clear it |
| ---: | ---: | --- |
| $300 | $600 | One $750 retainer invoiced |
| $600 | $1,200 | One completed $1,500 placement, or two $750 retainers |
| $1,000 | $2,000 | Two completed placements ($3,000). One placement alone is $1,500, which is 1.5:1 |

That last row is why the cap is a ceiling, not a goal. **Do not spend the full $1,000 just to finish the budget.** One completed placement does not clear 2:1 on $1,000. Two completed placements do (3:1). One placement plus a buyer commission he has received can also clear it, once that commission is filled in on the sheet.

## Stage gates

These match the Monday worksheet. Spend is cumulative platform spend.

1. **Monday 2 November** — launch check only (ads approved, limits in place, form emails arriving). No ratio decision. One day is not a result.
2. **Through $300 (end of November, or sooner if the limits were hit).** Keep going into a second $300 month only if there is at least one **qualified** renter lead or one **qualified** buyer lead. Definitions are in [weekly-roi-check.md](weekly-roi-check.md).
3. **At $600 with $0 invoiced.** Stop the test. Do not release the last $400.
4. **At $600 with some invoices but the pace under 2:1** (return under $1,200). Continue only if a second placement is already in progress (retainer paid, or a signed buyer agreement Devin expects to close) and the remaining spend still fits under $1,000.
5. **At $1,000.** Pause every campaign the same day, even if January is not over.
6. **60 days after the last dollar.** Record the final ratio. Under 2:1, leave the campaigns off.

## How the dollars map to leads

These are planning ranges for a cold Housing-category audience, not a forecast. Replace them with the first Monday’s real cost per landing-page view.

November’s $180 renter campaign, at a planning cost of $1.50–$4 per click, is about **45–120 clicks** to the relocating guide. If a few percent of those people finish the rental intake, November might produce a handful of intakes, or none. One intake that becomes a $750 retainer is already above 2:1 on that $180. Zero retainers after $300 of renter spend is the signal to change the creative once; zero qualified renter leads after that second creative and $300 more is the signal to stop the renter campaign.

The $60 buyer campaign may produce **zero or one** listings request in a month. Treat it as a test beside the renter campaign. Do not move the renter budget over to buyers to “give buyers a chance” unless the renter campaign has already failed its gate.

The $60 Google campaign may produce **10–30 clicks**. That is enough to see which queries draw a click and whether anyone writes. It is not enough to judge a keyword as a long-term channel.

## What to type for the dates

- Start: **1 November 2026**
- End on each campaign: **30 November 2026** for month 1
- When a later month is approved, extend the end date and raise the spending limit. Do not create a second campaign with the same ads (that splits the learning and double-charges the overlap).
