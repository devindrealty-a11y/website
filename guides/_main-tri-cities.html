Where I live and work

The Tri-Cities, plus two villages

I’m Devin Desaulniers, a Real Estate Associate with Axford Real Estate at 2326 Clarke Street in Port Moody. I’ve lived in Greater Vancouver for 28 years. I play soccer on a Port Coquitlam team. The Tri-Cities are the part of the region I can describe from a weekday, not from a listing.

“Tri-Cities” means three cities: Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam (PoCo) and Port Moody. Anmore and Belcarra are villages immediately north of Port Moody. People fold them into the same house search. They are not the same kind of place: the villages are larger lots, forest, and a car for most trips.

The wider region — Vancouver, Burnaby, New Westminster, Richmond, the North Shore, Surrey — is in the Greater Vancouver guide. This one stays local, and it still includes the tenancy rules, MSP, and the first 30 days, because those follow you no matter which city you pick.

Five places, not one

Neighbourhood snapshots

Coquitlam

The largest of the three

Coquitlam runs from older neighbourhoods near the Fraser up to Burke Mountain and Westwood Plateau. City Centre, around Coquitlam Centre, Lafarge Lake and Town Centre Park, is the hub the SkyTrain was built toward. September 2026 composite benchmark: $971,700 (down 6.3% from a year earlier). Detached $1,584,400, townhouse $976,600, apartment $640,100.

Names you will see on listings: Burke Mountain, Westwood Plateau, City Centre, Burquitlam, Austin Heights, Maillardville, Ranch Park, Eagle Ridge. Burquitlam is the SkyTrain stop on Clarke Road, shared in people’s minds with the Port Moody border. Maillardville is the older francophone neighbourhood in the southwest. Burke Mountain and Westwood Plateau are the hillside choice: more house, more of a drive, trails at the door. City Centre is the “I want the train and the mall” choice. The commute is usually what decides it.

Port Coquitlam

PoCo

Between the Coquitlam and Pitt rivers. A walkable downtown, the Traboulay PoCo Trail looping the city, and Terry Fox’s hometown. It is also where I play soccer, so it is part of my week. September 2026 composite benchmark: $835,500 (down 8.2%), the lowest of the three cities. Detached $1,240,600, townhouse $834,300, apartment $550,000.

Names on listings: Downtown PoCo, Citadel Heights, Mary Hill, Oxford Heights, Lincoln Park, Riverwood, Birchland Manor, Glenwood. There is no SkyTrain station. The West Coast Express stops at Port Coquitlam station on Kingsway, weekday rush hour only, and the R3 RapidBus runs along Lougheed Highway toward Coquitlam Central and toward Maple Ridge. If your job is downtown at 9 a.m., the train can work. If your hours move around, budget a bus plus SkyTrain, or a car.

Port Moody

At the end of the inlet

Port Moody calls itself the City of the Arts. It sits at the head of Burrard Inlet: a waterfront, then forested hills. Rocky Point Park, the Shoreline Trail, and the breweries along Murray Street — Brewers Row — are local favourites. Axford Real Estate’s office is on Clarke Street, which is this downtown. September 2026 composite benchmark: $1,011,500 (down 5.8%). Detached $1,903,500, townhouse $940,900, apartment $676,200. The detached number is a different market from the apartments at Inlet Centre. Do not average them in your head and go shopping.

Names on listings: Moody Centre, Inlet Centre and Suter Brook, Newport Village, Heritage Mountain, Heritage Woods, College Park, Glenayre, Ioco. Moody Centre and Inlet Centre have SkyTrain. Heritage Mountain, Heritage Woods and Ioco are up the hill. Ioco feels further from the stations than the map suggests once you are driving the switchbacks.

Anmore and Belcarra

The villages

Two small municipalities in the forest north of Port Moody. Homes tend to sit on larger, treed lots. Residents trade convenience for that. Buntzen Lake is in Anmore. təmtəmíxʷtən/Belcarra Regional Park and Sasamat Lake are the reason people from the rest of the region drive up on a hot day. GVR does not publish a separate benchmark for Anmore or Belcarra. I will not invent one. The nearest SkyTrain and West Coast Express are in Port Moody. Bus 182 runs from Moody Centre Station to Belcarra and stops at Anmore Grocery.

I don’t treat these villages as a quiet corner of Port Moody. They are a different errand pattern. Map the grocery trip and the school run before you fall for a lot.

What you would pay now

Rents in the Tri-Cities

Rentals.ca’s October 2026 report (September listings) publishes an apartment-and-condo asking average for Coquitlam: $2,497. It does not publish a Port Moody or Port Coquitlam figure, or a bedroom split for Coquitlam. For scale, Vancouver’s apartment-and-condo asking average the same month was $2,741, Burnaby $2,548, Richmond $2,572. Those are vacant listings, not the rent a neighbour who moved in three years ago is paying.

Evergreen, buses, one commuter train

Transit in the Tri-Cities

The diagram is mine. It is not a TransLink map and it is not to scale. The useful correction for newcomers: the Evergreen Extension is not a separate line you transfer onto. It is the east end of the Millennium Line, from Burquitlam to Lafarge Lake–Douglas.

Evergreen stations, in order: Burquitlam (Clarke Road, between Smith and Como Lake, Coquitlam) → Moody Centre (between Moody and Williams streets, Port Moody; also West Coast Express) → Inlet Centre (Ioco Road and Barnet Highway, Port Moody) → Coquitlam Central (east of Johnson Street at the Mariner Way overpass; also West Coast Express) → Lincoln (Pinetree Way, by Coquitlam Centre) → Lafarge Lake–Douglas (Pinetree Way and Guildford Way, the terminus, beside Town Centre Park).

Fares, as of October 2026

Three zones are still the system. Buses are 1-zone all the time. SkyTrain is 1, 2 or 3 zones on weekday daytimes, and 1-zone after 6:30 p.m. and all weekend and statutory holidays. A Compass Card is a $6 refundable deposit. Adult stored value is $2.85 / $4.20 / $5.40. Cash, a Compass ticket or contactless tap is $3.50 / $5.10 / $6.70. Monthly passes are $117.20 / $156.70 / $211.65. Children 12 and under ride free. A fare lasts 90 minutes, or 120 if the West Coast Express is included.

A weekday daytime ride from these stations into downtown crosses zone boundaries. Count the zones on TransLink’s map for your exact pair of stations before you buy a monthly pass. I am not labelling that trip “3-zone” in this guide unless you have checked the zone map for the stations you will actually use. Evening and weekend trips collapse to 1-zone either way.

The West Coast Express has its own 1-to-5 zone prices. Moody Centre, Coquitlam Central and Port Coquitlam are 3 West Coast Express zones from Waterfront. A SkyTrain monthly pass does not replace a West Coast Express pass. A West Coast Express return fare or monthly pass does include bus, SkyTrain and SeaBus the same day. Station parking was listed at $3 a day or $60 a month.

The train that only runs at rush hour

West Coast Express: Waterfront, Moody Centre, Coquitlam Central, Port Coquitlam, Pitt Meadows, Maple Meadows, Port Haney, Mission City. Weekdays only. Five trains westbound in the morning, five eastbound in the late afternoon and evening. Mission City’s departures are 5:25, 5:55, 6:25, 6:55 and 7:25 a.m., arriving Waterfront at 6:40, 7:10, 7:40, 8:10 and 8:40 a.m. — 75 minutes end to end. Tri-Cities stations are closer; use the current timetable for those minutes rather than a number I would be guessing.

Buses

Local routes feed Coquitlam Central, Moody Centre and Port Coquitlam station. The R3 Lougheed Hwy RapidBus is the limited-stop service between Coquitlam Central and Haney Place in Maple Ridge, via Port Coquitlam. The 182 is how you reach Belcarra, and the stop at Anmore Grocery is the one BC Hydro mentions for Buntzen Lake. The 160 runs from Port Coquitlam Station through Coquitlam Central and Moody Centre to Kootenay Loop in Vancouver along Hastings. I am not quoting a minutes-between-buses figure for the 160.

Groceries and everything else

Shopping

Coquitlam Centre is the enclosed mall: about 930,000 square feet, nearly 200 stores, on Barnet Highway at Lincoln station. The mall’s own site lists Walmart, Best Buy, London Drugs, Sport Chek, T&T Supermarket, UNIQLO, H&M, Sephora and lululemon among the anchors, and about 4,000 parking stalls with a complimentary parking window. It is the errand that SkyTrain was pointed at.

Suter Brook Village in Port Moody is a mixed-use street around a plaza, near Inlet Centre, with homes over shops. The developer’s leasing page has included Thrifty Foods, TD and Starbucks. Newport Village, also in Port Moody, is the other village-style retail street, on Newport Drive across from City Hall and the library, managed by Bosa. Neither is a substitute for Coquitlam Centre if you want a department-store run. Both are where a lot of Port Moody actually buys groceries.

Local favourites that are not malls: the breweries on Murray Street’s Brewers Row, and Rocky Point Ice Cream inside Rocky Point Park at 2800 Murray Street, still operating when this page was checked in October 2026. PoCo’s downtown, along Shaughnessy and the streets around city hall, is the smaller main street. I am naming places that exist, not ranking them.

The reason people pick this side of the region

Parks and hikes

Rocky Point Park

2800 Murray Street, Port Moody. The city’s waterfront park: pier, playground, Shoreline Trail, kayak and paddleboard rental, and food at the park. A master plan endorsed by council in 2024 sets the next stretch of upgrades. This is the after-work walk, not a hike.

Mundy Park

641 Hillcrest Street, Coquitlam. A 178-hectare urban forest, dedicated as an urban forest park in 1993, with Mundy Lake and Lost Lake. The 5.5 km multi-use loop around it is about an hour on foot. It is the large park inside the city, as opposed to the mountain parks on the edge.

Town Centre Park

1299 Pinetree Way. Forty-two hectares on Lafarge Lake, opened in 1989, with the Community Plaza on the lake (2016) next to Lafarge Lake–Douglas station. If you live at that end of the line, this is the park at the bottom of the escalator.

Buntzen Lake — read this before a sunny Saturday

BC Hydro runs the recreation area in Anmore. Parking is free. In recent summers, roughly mid-May to early September, you still need a free online parking pass, released in the morning for the next day. Cars without one are turned away. Dates change; check bchydro.com/buntzen for the current year. Outside that window the lot can still fill and the gate can close. There is no public parking in the Village of Anmore. Walking, cycling and the 182 bus do not need a pass. The 182 stops at Anmore Grocery, about a 30-minute walk to the South Beach lot.

Belcarra and Admiralty Point

Metro Vancouver’s təmtəmíxʷtən/Belcarra Regional Park: the beach, Sasamat Lake (White Pine Beach is the swim spot; the photo is February, not July), Woodhaven Swamp, and the Admiralty Point Trail. The park brochure lists that trail at about 6 km and 1.5 hours, a hardened path with rocky shoreline and inlet views. The 182 is the bus. On a hot weekend, locals go early or they don’t go.

Burke Mountain

Coquitlam’s trail system includes Burke Mountain loop 1 (about 2 km) and loop 2 (about 3.2 km), mixed surfaces, some steep sections. Harper Park is the main trailhead. Those are the maintained city trails. Beyond them, Pinecone Burke Provincial Park is backcountry: BC Parks says much of the hiking infrastructure is in poor condition and the signage is incomplete. Pack for that, or stay on the city loops.

PoCo’s equivalent of a long walk is the Traboulay PoCo Trail, plus the dikes on the Coquitlam and Pitt rivers. It is flat. Burke Mountain is not.

School District 43

One district, five communities

School District No. 43 (Coquitlam) serves Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Port Moody, Anmore and Belcarra. The district describes a neighbourhood catchment school based on the student’s permanent address, and it says boundaries are reviewed and can change. Use the district’s School Locator for the address on the lease or the contract. A listing that names a school is not the assignment.

The district’s 2025–26 contact list includes these secondary schools, grades 9–12, as examples only: Centennial and Dr. Charles Best and Gleneagle and Pinetree in Coquitlam, Heritage Woods and Port Moody Secondary in Port Moody, Riverside and Terry Fox in Port Coquitlam. That is not a ranking and it is not a promise your address falls in one of them.

If you are comparing a place in Burnaby or Vancouver, you are comparing a different district: SD41 and SD39. Crossing a city boundary to “stay near a school” does not keep you in SD43.

Fraser Health

MSP, a doctor, and the local hospital

Apply for MSP as soon as you arrive. Coverage waits the rest of the month you become a B.C. resident, plus two months. The Province’s example: resident on 16 August, covered 1 November. The wait is waived for returning Canadians, permanent residents, people arriving under CUEAT, and discharged Canadian Armed Forces members and their families. Buy private insurance, or keep your old province’s coverage, for the gap. Emergency rooms will see you. They will also bill you if you are uninsured.

Most adults then visit an ICBC office for a photo BC Services Card, which is the health card.

Register for a family doctor or nurse practitioner on the Health Connect Registry. The Province will not estimate the wait. Do not plan around a number you saw on social media.

Eagle Ridge Hospital, 475 Guildford Way, Port Moody, 604-461-2022, is the Tri-Cities community hospital: 24/7 emergency, general medicine, rehab and surgery. Royal Columbian Hospital in New Westminster is Fraser Health’s Level 1 trauma centre, the regional referral hospital when Eagle Ridge is not the right place. Know which is which before you need either.

Admin

Licence, utilities, internet, rain

ICBC. You have 90 days to exchange an out-of-province licence. Bring the current licence, government ID, and proof of driving history. ICBC credits up to 15 years when the abstract qualifies. Book a licensing office. I am not quoting a fee the pages I read did not list. Graduated-licensing rules were flagged to change on 19 October 2026 for some new drivers; check the live page if that is you.

BC Hydro is electricity. You can open the account up to 60 days before move-in. The first bill includes a $13.50 + GST new-account charge, and some people also pay a deposit. FortisBC is natural gas, including in Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Port Moody and Anmore. Confirm the address has gas before you assume a furnace or a gas range is theirs to turn on.

Internet. TELUS has PureFibre in Coquitlam and offers it in Port Moody and Port Coquitlam. Rogers serves the region on the former Shaw network. Novus is in some buildings in Coquitlam and Port Moody, not all of them. Look up the civic address. Order it before the week you arrive.

Rain. Vancouver Harbour’s 1991–2020 normals are the closest official set I am willing to quote: 1,537.4 mm a year, November the wettest month at 245.2 mm, January 236.6 mm, July 43.5 mm. Daily average temperature 5.3°C in January and 19.0°C in July at that station. Burke Mountain and the villages are higher and further from the water. Expect them to be cooler, and to see snow the inlet doesn’t. I don’t have a Port Moody normals table, so I won’t fake one. Waterproof shoes you can wear on a platform matter more than a nice umbrella.

Before you send anyone money

Renting rules and scams

  • Deposit: security deposit at most half a month’s rent. Pet damage deposit, if charged, at most another half month. Not first-and-last.
  • No application fee. Section 15 of the Residential Tenancy Act forbids charging someone to apply, to be screened, or to be accepted. A “refundable credit-check fee” is still a charge.
  • Increases: 2.3% maximum in 2026, 2.2% in 2027, once every 12 months, three full months’ written notice on the official form.
  • RTB-27 condition inspection at move-in and move-out. Copy within 7 days of move-in and 15 days of move-out. That report is how you get the deposit back.
  • Disputes go to the Residential Tenancy Branch. Filing fee $100, with waivers. Phone 1-800-665-8779.

See the suite, in person or on a live video from inside it. Match the person to the right to rent it. Prices far under Coquitlam’s $2,497 asking average, pressure to pay today, and requests for wire, gift cards or crypto are the pattern. Photos get stolen; reverse-image them. If you are still in another province, my search is the alternative to wiring a deposit to a stranger.

Listings here usually appear a few weeks before the suite is free. Plan the active search for about 4–6 weeks before you need keys.

Property transfer tax

What a purchase costs before the moving truck

Ordinary property transfer tax, on fair market value at registration:

  • 1% up to $200,000
  • 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000
  • 3% above $2,000,000
  • a further 2% on the residential portion above $3,000,000

Example using the September 2026 PoCo apartment benchmark of $550,000. Tax is $2,000 plus 2% of $350,000, which is $7,000, total $9,000. A first-time buyer who qualifies, on a registration on or after 1 April 2024, gets an $8,000 exemption on a home over $500,000 and up to $835,000, so the tax after the exemption would be $1,000 if every other condition is met. Full exemption applies at $500,000 or less. It phases out from $835,000 to $860,000 and is gone at $860,000. Port Moody’s apartment benchmark ($676,200) is inside the $8,000-exemption band. Port Moody’s detached benchmark ($1,903,500) is not: the first-time exemption is zero at that price.

You have to be a citizen or permanent resident, meet the residence and filing tests, never have owned a principal residence anywhere, never have used this exemption, and live in the home. The property generally has to be 0.5 hectares or smaller for the full amount. Anmore lots can bump into that size limit. Check the hectare figure before you count on the exemption.

A qualifying newly built principal residence can be fully exempt up to $1,100,000, and partly exempt up to $1,150,000. Coquitlam’s townhouse benchmark ($976,600) is under $1,100,000. Ordinary tax on that figure is $17,532 ($2,000 plus 2% of $776,600), which is what the exemption is worth if the home and the buyer both qualify. There is no end date on the page I checked; it applies to qualifying registrations after 16 February 2016.

An additional 20% tax can apply to a foreign buyer’s share of residential property in Metro Vancouver. Exemptions exist, including some for confirmed provincial nominees. This is not tax advice. Legal fees, a possible inspection, and GST on many new homes sit on top of property transfer tax. Try the mortgage calculator for a payment estimate, and treat it as an estimate.

Order of operations

First 30 days in the Tri-Cities

Before you arrive

  • Pick the city from the station you will use, not from a drone photo of the inlet.
  • Budget from current listings. Coquitlam’s $2,497 is an average, not a ceiling or a floor.
  • Prepare ID and income documents. Refuse any application fee.
  • Start a rental search about 4–6 weeks out if you want me in it.
  • Book BC Hydro, and FortisBC if the home has gas.
  • Check Novus by address; otherwise TELUS or Rogers. Order early.

Week one

  • File MSP. Buy coverage for the wait.
  • Walk the suite with the landlord. Sign RTB-27. Keep the copy.
  • Get a Compass Card. Ride your real commute once on a weekday morning before you buy a monthly pass.
  • Join the Health Connect Registry.
  • Book ICBC. Ninety days is the rule, not a suggestion.
  • Find Eagle Ridge on a map so you are not searching for it later.

By day 30

  • Run the address through SD43’s School Locator if school is part of the decision.
  • Learn your city’s collection day. Coquitlam, PoCo and Port Moody do not share one calendar.
  • Walk Mundy Park or the Shoreline Trail in the rain. That is the climate you signed up for.
  • If Buntzen is your Saturday plan, learn the parking-pass rule before summer.
  • Update bank, CRA and ICBC addresses.
  • If the neighbourhood is wrong, say so early. A lease term is a long time to dislike a hill.
From a weekday here

Things locals know that newcomers don’t

Three cities, two villages

Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam and Port Moody have different mayors, different tax bills and different stations. Anmore and Belcarra are not “north Port Moody.”

PoCo has no SkyTrain

The West Coast Express and the R3 are the rapid options. If someone says “the train” in PoCo, ask which one. They may mean a service that does not run at noon.

You do not transfer to the Evergreen Line

Stay on the Millennium Line. Lafarge Lake–Douglas is the end.

Lincoln is the mall. Lafarge Lake is the park.

They are one stop apart. People mix them up when they are giving directions to Coquitlam Centre.

Inlet Centre and Moody Centre are both Port Moody

Suter Brook is at Inlet Centre. Clarke Street and the brewery row are at Moody Centre. They are not the same walk.

The hill is a lifestyle

Heritage Mountain, Westwood Plateau and Burke Mountain look close to a station on a regional map. Add the drive down to the train on a dark January morning before you decide you are a SkyTrain household.

Buntzen’s pass is the reservation

The parking is free and, in summer, still unavailable if you did not book it. That surprises almost every newcomer once.

Half a month’s rent

That is the legal ceiling on a security deposit. Last month’s rent as a deposit is a phrase from somewhere else.

Asking rent is the number you pay

CMHC’s Coquitlam 1-bedroom average of $1,692 is October 2025 and includes people who already live there. It is a poor budget for a listing you will see this month.

SD43 does not follow a realtor’s sentence

Catchment is the address. Confirm it on the district’s locator after you have an address, not before you have fallen for a kitchen.

Eagle Ridge is the local ER

Royal Columbian, in New Westminster, is where the major trauma goes. Both are Fraser Health. They are not interchangeable.

November is the wet month

Not a long weekend of rain. At the harbour station, November averages 245 mm. The mountain gets more weather than Clarke Street.
