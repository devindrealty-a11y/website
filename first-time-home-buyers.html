First home · British Columbia

The taxes on a first home in BC

Two amounts can change the cash you need up front: the federal GST on a new home, and B.C. property transfer tax. This page uses the government rules as of November 28, 2026. Devin Desaulniers is a Real Estate Associate with Axford Real Estate in the Tri-Cities, BC.

$50,000Maximum federal GST rebate on a new home with a price of $1 million or less.
$8,000First-time property transfer tax exemption on a resale home over $500,000 and up to $835,000.
$0Property transfer tax on a qualifying newly built home of $1.1 million or less.

Three programs, three different tests

A resale home and a new home are taxed differently. The GST rebate is federal and only for a new home. Property transfer tax is provincial and has two exemptions that do not work the same way.

I work with buyers in Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Port Moody, and the rest of Greater Vancouver. The thresholds below are province-wide and federal. They are not a quote for a specific home.

GST on a new home

Up to $50,000

In force. A full rebate of the GST, capped at $50,000, when the price is $1 million or less. It shrinks after that and ends at $1.5 million. Resale homes do not qualify.

First-time transfer tax

$8,000 off

On a resale home over $500,000 and up to $835,000, the exemption is $8,000. That is the tax on the first $500,000. You still pay tax on the rest of the price.

New-home transfer tax

Often $0

The newly built home exemption can remove the whole property transfer tax at $1.1 million or less. You do not have to be a first-time buyer.

Federal · in force

GST relief for a first home that is new

Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 12, 2026. The Canada Revenue Agency said on March 17, 2026 that it is accepting the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST rebate. This is law, not a proposal.

In British Columbia a new home is charged 5% GST. B.C. does not add provincial HST on that purchase. The rebate gives back the GST (or, in HST provinces, the federal part of the HST). The CRA's published example: a new home priced at $1.25 million is the midpoint between $1 million and $1.5 million, so the rebate is 50% of the $50,000 maximum, which is $25,000.

$1 million or lessUp to 100%

Of the GST you paid, to a maximum rebate of $50,000.

$1.25 million$25,000

The CRA's midpoint example. Half of the $50,000 maximum.

$1.5 million or more$0

No First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST rebate.

Who can claim it

  • You are 18 or older, and a Canadian citizen or permanent resident.
  • The home is newly built or substantially renovated, and it will be your primary residence. You are the first person to live in it after that work.
  • You, and your spouse or common-law partner if you have one, did not live in a home that either of you owned in the year you buy it, or in the four calendar years before that.
  • For a home bought from a builder, the purchase agreement is dated on or after March 20, 2025, and before 2031. The date you both agreed is the one that counts, not the later date when conditions are waived.
  • If more than one person is on title, only one person claims the rebate, and that person has to be a first-time buyer under this test. A parent on title who already owns a home does not block the claim by itself.

There is also the older GST/HST new housing rebate. It is 36% of the GST, to a maximum of $6,300, when the home is $350,000 or less. It shrinks between $350,000 and $450,000, and it is $0 at $450,000 or more. On a home of $450,000 or less, the two rebates together can return all of the GST. You do not get the $6,300 as well as a full GST refund. Most new homes in the Tri-Cities, BC are over $450,000, so the older rebate is often $0 and the new first-time rebate is the one that matters.

An assignment of a contract that was signed before March 20, 2025 does not create a new agreement date for this rebate. The CRA memorandum walks through that case. If your contract date is close to the cutoff, ask your lawyer to read it before you count on the rebate.

Sources, checked November 28, 2026: Finance Canada, Royal Assent, March 12, 2026; CRA, what the rebate is; GST/HST Memorandum 19-3-10 (October 2026).

British Columbia

Property transfer tax, in cards

This is a one-time tax when the home is registered. It is not the yearly property tax bill from the city. The general rates, from the Province, are:

1%On the first $200,000 of fair market value.
2%On the portion above $200,000, up to $2 million.
3%On the portion above $2 million.
+2%A further 2% on the residential value above $3 million.

A resale home at $800,000

This price is in the Province's own exemption table. The home is under $2 million, so only the 1% and 2% bands apply.

1% of the first $200,000$2,000

$200,000 × 1%.

2% of the next $600,000$12,000

$600,000 × 2%.

Tax before any exemption$14,000

$2,000 + $12,000. The Province's table shows the same $14,000.

If you qualify for the First Time Home Buyers' Program, the exemption at $800,000 is $8,000. You pay $6,000. The same table lists that $6,000 as the tax payable.

If that $800,000 home is newly built and you qualify for the newly built home exemption instead, the tax payable is $0. The new-home exemption removes the whole tax at this price. The first-time program would have left $6,000.

Fair market value is the price a willing buyer would pay a willing seller on the registration date. Two exemptions

First-time buyers, and newly built homes

First Time Home Buyers' Program

This can apply to a resale home or a new home. To qualify when the home is registered you generally must:

  • Be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident.
  • Have lived in B.C. for at least a year immediately before registration, or have filed at least 2 income tax returns as a B.C. resident in the last 6 tax years.
  • Never have owned a principal residence anywhere in the world, and never have received this exemption or a refund of it before.
  • Use the property only as your principal residence. It must be 0.5 hectares (1.24 acres) or smaller and contain only a home.

You move in within 92 days and keep it as your principal residence through the first year, or you can lose part of the exemption.

Amounts for a registration on or after April 1, 2024, when you own 100% and the lot is under 0.5 hectares:

  • $500,000 or less: the full tax is exempt.
  • Over $500,000 up to $835,000: the exemption is $8,000.
  • Over $835,000 and under $860,000: the $8,000 shrinks in proportion to how close the price is to $860,000.
  • $860,000: no exemption.

Newly built home exemption

This is separate. You do not have to be buying for the first time. The home must be newly built and not yet lived in, registered after February 16, 2016, and this must be the first registration after the home was completed. You must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and the home must be your principal residence, in B.C., on 0.5 hectares or less.

For a registration on or after April 1, 2024:

  • $1,100,000 or less: the full property transfer tax is exempt. At $1,100,000 that tax is $20,000, so the exemption is $20,000 and the tax payable is $0.
  • Over $1,100,000 and under $1,150,000: the exemption shrinks. The Province's formula is the tax, multiplied by ($1,150,000 minus the price) divided by $50,000.
  • $1,150,000: no exemption. The tax at that price is $21,000.

You move in within 92 days and occupy the home for the rest of the first year.

On a new home under $1.1 million, this exemption removes more tax than the first-time program. The estimate on this page uses it when you say the home is a new build and the price still qualifies.

If only some of the buyers qualify, or the lot is larger than 0.5 hectares, the exemption is smaller. Foreign buyers can also owe an additional property transfer tax in specified areas of B.C. The rate and the map are on the Province's additional-tax page. This page does not add that tax. Saving the down payment

FHSA and the RRSP Home Buyers' Plan

First Home Savings Account

In the year you open your first FHSA, your participation room is $8,000. That room covers contributions and transfers from an RRSP, combined. Over your lifetime, contributions are capped at $40,000. Money you put in can be deducted on your tax return. A qualifying withdrawal to buy your first home is tax-free. Unused room can carry forward. The exact room for a later year is in your CRA account, because the formula also looks at excess amounts.

Home Buyers' Plan

You can withdraw up to $60,000 from your RRSPs. You pay it back over 15 years. For a first withdrawal between January 1, 2026 and December 31, 2028, the CRA says repayment starts in the fifth year after the year of the withdrawal. A 2026 withdrawal starts repayment in 2031. You can use an FHSA withdrawal and a Home Buyers' Plan withdrawal for the same home if you qualify for both.

The mortgage stress test, in short

For an uninsured mortgage from a federally regulated lender, OSFI's minimum qualifying rate is the greater of your contract rate plus 2 percentage points, or 5.25%. OSFI reviews that floor and that buffer at least once a year. The page stating the current rate does not set a different number. OSFI does not require that set rate when you switch an uninsured mortgage to another federally regulated lender at renewal, if the loan amount and the amortization do not go up. Insured mortgages follow the federal mortgage-insurance rules. Your lender applies the test that fits your loan. I am not a mortgage broker, and this is not an approval.

FAQ

Questions first-time buyers ask

Is the federal GST rebate for first-time buyers law?

Yes. Bill C-4, the Making Life More Affordable for Canadians Act, received Royal Assent on March 12, 2026. The Canada Revenue Agency said on March 17, 2026 that it is accepting applications. On a new home with a price of $1 million or less, an eligible buyer can get back up to 100% of the GST, to a maximum of $50,000. The rebate shrinks between $1 million and $1.5 million, and it is $0 at $1.5 million or more.

Does the GST rebate apply to a resale home?

No. The First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST rebate is for a newly built or substantially renovated home that will be your primary residence. A resale home is not charged GST in the usual sale, so there is no GST rebate to claim.

How much property transfer tax is charged on an $800,000 resale home?

Using the general rates, the tax is $14,000: 1% of the first $200,000 is $2,000, and 2% of the remaining $600,000 is $12,000. A qualifying first-time buyer gets an $8,000 exemption at that price and pays $6,000. Those figures match the Province's exemption table.

Does a first-time buyer pay no property transfer tax on a home up to $835,000?

Not always. If the fair market value is $500,000 or less, a qualifying buyer can be exempt from the full tax. From above $500,000 up to $835,000, the exemption is $8,000, which is the tax on the first $500,000. You still pay the tax on the rest. Between $835,000 and $860,000 the $8,000 shrinks, and at $860,000 there is no exemption.

What if the home is newly built?

A separate newly built home exemption can remove the property transfer tax when the fair market value is $1,100,000 or less. It shrinks between $1,100,000 and $1,150,000, and it is gone at $1,150,000. You do not have to be a first-time buyer. At $1,100,000 the tax before the exemption is $20,000, and a full exemption brings it to $0.

Can I use a First Home Savings Account and the RRSP Home Buyers' Plan for the same home?

Yes, if you meet the rules for each one. An FHSA lets you contribute up to $8,000 in the year you open it, with a $40,000 lifetime limit. A qualifying withdrawal is tax-free. The Home Buyers' Plan lets you withdraw up to $60,000 from your RRSPs. For a first withdrawal in 2026, 2027, or 2028, repayment starts in the fifth year after the withdrawal.

Is the estimator on this page my tax bill?

No. It is an estimate of B.C. property transfer tax from the published rates and exemptions, using the price and the two yes-or-no answers you enter. It assumes a fully residential property of 0.5 hectares or smaller and a buyer who meets the other legal tests. Your lawyer or notary calculates the tax when the home is registered.
