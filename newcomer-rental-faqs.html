I don't have a Canadian credit history yet. Can I still rent?

Often, yes, but your application has to work harder. Landlords mainly want to know you can pay the rent and will look after the home. Without a Canadian credit report, the things that help are a Canadian job offer or employment letter, recent bank statements, references from previous landlords (even outside Canada), and a short renter profile that explains your situation. I help you put that package together so it's ready the day a good rental comes up.

How much deposit can a landlord ask for in B.C.?

In B.C., a security deposit can be no more than half of one month's rent, and a pet damage deposit (if you have a pet) is also capped at half a month's rent. Landlords in B.C. can't ask for "first and last month's rent" or "key money". If a landlord asks for more than the rules allow, treat it as a red flag.

See Tenancy deposits and fees on the Province of British Columbia site, and Residential Tenancy Act, section 19.

How do I avoid rental scams when I'm moving from another country?

Don't send money for a place nobody has seen in person or on a live video call, and be wary of listings priced well below similar homes, landlords who can't show it right now, or anyone asking you to wire a deposit to hold a unit. Insist on a written tenancy agreement and a move-in condition inspection. I can view the home for you, live on video, before you commit.

What languages do you work in?

I work in English, and I use a translation app when a client is more comfortable in another language. Live video walkthroughs, floor plans and written lease summaries help a lot when you're reading everything in a second language.
