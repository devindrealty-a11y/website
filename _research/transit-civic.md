### 1.5 Compass Card and current (2026) fare/zone system

**Where to buy / load:** Compass Cards cost a $6 refundable deposit and can be purchased online at compasscard.ca, by phone (604-398-2042), at Compass Vending Machines (CVMs) in every SkyTrain/SeaBus/WCE station, at the TransLink Customer Service Centre (Waterfront Station), or at authorized Compass Retailers and select London Drugs stores. Concession cards can be purchased at all of these except CVMs.

- Source: https://www.translink.ca/transit-fares/where-to-buy. Accessed 11 Oct 2026.

**Stored Value vs. Monthly Pass vs. Compass Ticket (official, current):**
- *Stored Value* — pay-as-you-go, loaded in $5+ increments; cheaper per-trip than cash.
- *Monthly Pass* — unlimited SkyTrain/SeaBus travel within the zones purchased, plus unlimited bus/HandyDART travel at any time, and unlimited all-zone/all-mode travel on weekends, statutory holidays, and after 6:30 p.m. weekdays.
- *DayPass* — unlimited all-zone bus/SkyTrain/SeaBus/HandyDART travel for one day (not valid on WCE).
- *Compass Ticket* — single-use, cash-rate, bought only at CVMs.
- *Concession fares* — available to HandyCard holders, seniors 65+, and youth 13–18 (photo ID required); CNIB pass holders who live in Metro Vancouver ride free; children 12 and under ride free on bus, SkyTrain, SeaBus, HandyDART, and WCE.

**Current zone/fare structure (as published live, 11 Oct 2026 — this is the CURRENT system, not eliminated):**
> "There are three fare zones across Metro Vancouver... All bus and HandyDART trips are a 1-Zone fare at all times. SkyTrain requires a 1, 2, or 3-Zone fare, depending on the time and day and number of zone boundaries you cross during your trip. SeaBus requires a 1 or 2-Zone fare... All trips across the system starting after 6:30 p.m. (Monday to Friday) and all travel on weekends... are a 1-Zone fare. All trips... during provincial statutory holidays are a 1-Zone fare."

So, as of October 2026: **buses are flat 1-zone fare at all times** (this is not a new change — buses have long been flat-rate; it is simply the current confirmed state), and **SkyTrain/SeaBus retain the legacy 1/2/3-zone system** depending on time of day and how many zone boundaries are crossed (evenings after 6:30 p.m. weekdays and all weekend/holiday travel collapse to 1-zone). TransLink has **not** moved to a distance/time-based fare system as of this date — the zone system described above is the current, official system.

### 1.6 SkyTrain travel time, Tri-Cities → Downtown Waterfront (official published segment times — not invented)

TransLink publishes official **station-to-station travel-time charts** (in minutes) for each line. These are quoted directly, not estimated:

- **Coquitlam Central → Commercial–Broadway (on the Millennium Line): 32 minutes** (official chart value).
- **Lafarge Lake–Douglas → Commercial–Broadway (on the Millennium Line): 35 minutes** (official chart value).
- **Waterfront → Commercial–Broadway (on the Expo Line): 10 minutes** (official chart value).

Because Millennium Line trains run VCC-Clark ↔ Lafarge Lake-Douglas and do **not** continue to Waterfront, reaching downtown requires one transfer — most commonly at **Commercial–Broadway** onto an Expo Line train toward Waterfront (other transfer points exist at Lougheed Town Centre and Production Way–University, with slightly different combined times).

Summing TransLink's own published segment times (in-vehicle time only, **excluding** any wait time for the connecting train):
- **Coquitlam Central → Waterfront: approximately 32 + 10 = 42 minutes** of in-vehicle travel time, plus transfer wait.
- **Lafarge Lake–Douglas → Waterfront: approximately 35 + 10 = 45 minutes** of in-vehicle travel time, plus transfer wait.

These are TransLink's own published minute figures, summed across the one required transfer — they are **not** Google-estimate planning ranges. Actual door-to-door time will be a few minutes longer once you add the wait for the connecting train (TransLink publishes peak Expo-Line frequency as 2–5 minutes and peak Millennium-Line frequency as 3–4 minutes, so a realistic total including transfer wait is roughly **45–50 minutes** at peak). TransLink does not appear to publish a single combined "Coquitlam Central to Waterfront" number anywhere else, so this sum (clearly labelled as a sum of official segments, with the transfer-wait caveat) is the most accurate non-invented figure available.

- Source: https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/schedules-and-maps/skytrain/skytrain_station_travel_times.pdf — official TransLink "SkyTrain station to station travel times" PDF (Millennium Line and Expo Line charts). Accessed 11 Oct 2026.
- Peak frequency figures: https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/skytrain. Accessed 11 Oct 2026.

## 3. HEALTH

### 3.1 MSP (BC Medical Services Plan) registration

**How to apply:** Apply online (fastest), by mail (form HLTH 101), or in person at any Service BC location. You need ID proving legal name and Canadian citizenship/immigration status for everyone on the application.

> "Apply for MSP coverage as soon as you arrive in B.C. Do not wait until the end of your wait period."

- Source: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/health/health-drug-coverage/msp/bc-residents/eligibility-and-enrolment/apply-for-msp — "Apply for MSP," last updated 7 Jul 2026. Accessed 11 Oct 2026.

**Coverage wait period rule — VERIFIED, and it has NOT changed from the historical "3-month" structure:**
> "The wait period is: The rest of the month you become a resident of British Columbia, Plus two more months."

So the rule is still: **balance of the arrival month + 2 full months** (commonly summarized as "about 3 months," matching the historical rule — it has **not** been shortened or eliminated as of 11 Oct 2026). Example given on an official MSP Direct user guide: arriving/becoming a resident Aug 16 → coverage begins Nov 1 (balance of August, plus September and October).

**What HAS changed / is new (confirm this nuance, since the user flagged it):** there is now an explicit **waiver of the 3-month wait** for specific groups:
> "The 3-month wait period for MSP coverage is waived for returning Canadians, permanent residents and people arriving in Canada through CUEAT [Canada-Ukraine Authorization for Emergency Travel]."
Discharged Canadian Armed Forces members (and their spouses/children) are also exempt from the wait period. A formal "wait period waiver" request process exists for exceptional/hardship cases but is only granted "in the most serious and exceptional cases."

- Sources: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/health/health-drug-coverage/msp/bc-residents/eligibility-and-enrolment/apply-for-msp (last updated 7 Jul 2026) and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/health/health-drug-coverage/msp/bc-residents/eligibility-and-enrolment/how-to-enrol/coverage-wait-period (last updated 4 Jun 2026). Both accessed 11 Oct 2026.

**During the wait period:** get private health insurance (for arrivals from outside Canada) or keep your former province's coverage (for interprovincial movers). Emergency care is available before MSP starts, but you must pay full cost unless privately insured.

**After enrolling:** most adults must visit an ICBC driver licensing office in person to get a photo BC Services Card (the MSP/health card); children and most temporary-document holders are mailed a non-photo card.

### 3.2 Finding a family doctor / nurse practitioner — Health Connect Registry

> "If you live in British Columbia and need a family doctor or nurse practitioner, register for the Health Connect Registry... Registration takes less [than] five minutes."

Official URL: https://hcr.healthlinkbc.ca/primarycarepatientregistration/s/ (registration portal); overview at https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/find-care/health-connect-registry (last updated 2 Apr 2026).

The Health Connect Registry is part of B.C.'s three-part **Provincial Attachment System**. As of Spring 2026, the Province states **"more than 77% of British Columbians are connected to a primary care provider"** and roughly 4,000 more are being connected each week.

**Wait time: explicitly NOT estimated by the government.** Official text: *"We recognize you may have been waiting for some time to find a family doctor or nurse practitioner. While we are unable to estimate how long it will take to be matched, health care services are available while you wait."* Treat any specific number of weeks/months you see elsewhere as **UNVERIFIED** — the official source deliberately does not commit to a wait-time figure.

- Sources: https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/find-care/health-connect-registry, https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/find-care/health-connect-registry/health-connect-registry-after-you-register, and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/health/accessing-health-care/bcs-primary-care-system/provincial-attachment-system. ## 4. ICBC — out-of-province driver's licence exchange

Confirmed, official, current:

> "Welcome to B.C.! After moving here, you have 90 days to switch over your licence. You can drive with an existing, valid licence in the meantime."

This 90-day rule applies whether moving from within Canada or from outside Canada. Exceptions to the 90-day rule exist for tourists visiting up to 6 months, full-time students at a designated institution with a valid student exemption, people "ordinarily resident outside of B.C.," and SAWP seasonal agricultural workers (12 months).

**What to bring / requirements (official):**
- Your current, valid out-of-province/out-of-country driver's licence (to be surrendered once your B.C. licence is issued — "B.C. law requires that you only have one driver's licence").
- Proof of driving experience/driving history — e.g., a current licence showing your original issue date, a driver's licence history/abstract document, or a letter from your original licence issuer. ICBC states it "will credit you with up to 15 years of driving experience" upon receipt of a qualifying abstract.
- Accepted government ID.
- If your home jurisdiction has no licence-exchange agreement with ICBC, or you can't prove driving experience, you'll need a mandatory vision screening and possibly a knowledge test and road test.
- You must book an appointment (or use walk-in service) at an ICBC driver licensing office.

ICBC's current page notes new B.C. residents are **no longer required to provide proof of insurance** (dropped as a requirement), though "their premiums will be adjusted for the first three years of driving in B.C."

**No fee amounts were stated on the pages I fetched for the basic licence exchange itself**, so I have not quoted a dollar figure — treat any specific exchange fee as **UNVERIFIED** unless confirmed on icbc.com at time of use.

- Sources: https://icbc.com/driver-licensing/moving-bc/Moving-from-within-canada; https://icbc.com/driver-licensing/moving-bc/moving-from-another-country; https://icbc.com/getting-started-with-icbc; https://www.icbc.com/driver-licensing/moving-bc. All accessed 11 Oct 2026.

**Note:** ICBC's own site flags Graduated Licensing Program (GLP) changes "effective October 19, 2026" for certain new-driver scenarios when moving to B.C. — this is close to (just after) the research date, so double-check icbc.com for the very latest rule if applying near that date.

## 5. UTILITIES

### 5.1 Electricity — BC Hydro

BC Hydro is the electricity utility. To set up service when moving:
1. Create a MyHydro profile.
2. Select "Open a new residential account" (or "Move your service" if transferring).
3. Provide new address, phone number, move-in date, and landlord's name/contact (if renting).
4. Choose a rate plan.
5. **"A charge of $13.50 + GST for new accounts will appear on your first BC Hydro bill. Some accounts may also require a security deposit."**

BC Hydro recommends contacting them "well before your move-in date" — service can be set up up to 60 days ahead.

- Source: https://app.bchydro.com/accounts-billing/moving-electrical-connections/new-customer.html and https://app.bchydro.com/accounts-billing/moving-electrical-connections/move-in-out.html. Accessed 11 Oct 2026.

### 5.2 Natural gas — FortisBC

**FortisBC is confirmed as the natural gas utility for Metro Vancouver, including Coquitlam and Port Moody** (and Anmore, Port Coquitlam) — FortisBC's own business-account-manager contact list explicitly groups "Abbotsford, Agassiz, Anmore, Chilliwack, Coquitlam, Delta, Hope, Langley... Port Coquitlam, Port Moody, Surrey, White Rock" under one FortisBC energy-solutions-manager territory. ## 7. RENTING LAW — Residential Tenancy Branch (RTB) / gov.bc.ca

All of the following is drawn from the B.C. **Residential Tenancy Act (RTA)** and the Province's official Residential Tenancy Branch pages, current as of access date.

### 7.1 Deposits

> "19 (1) A landlord must not require or accept either a security deposit or a pet damage deposit that is greater than the equivalent of 1/2 of one month's rent payable under the tenancy agreement."

So: **security deposit max = ½ month's rent; pet damage deposit max = ½ month's rent (regardless of number of pets); combined max if a landlord charges both = 1 full month's rent.** Only one of each deposit type may be charged per tenancy, regardless of number of tenants/pets. If overcharged, the tenant may deduct the overpayment from rent (RTA s.19(2)).

- Source: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/02078_01 — Residential Tenancy Act, ss. 17, 19–21 (official B.C. laws site). Accessed 11 Oct 2026. Corroborated by https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/deposits.pdf (official info sheet).

### 7.2 Application fees — CONFIRMED PROHIBITED

> "15 A landlord must not charge a person anything for (a) accepting an application for a tenancy, (b) processing the application, (c) investigating the applicant's suitability as a tenant, or (d) accepting the person as a tenant."

This is section 15 of the Residential Tenancy Act — application/processing/credit-check fees are illegal in B.C., even if a landlord later refunds the fee or applies it to a deposit. "First and last month's rent" and "key money" demands are also explicitly prohibited (landlords cannot require more than the half-month deposit caps above).

- Source: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/02078_01 (RTA s.15). Accessed 11 Oct 2026.

### 7.3 Rent increases, 2026/2027

> "The 2026 rent increase limit for residential tenancies is 2.3%. The 2027 rent increase limit for residential tenancies is 2.2%."

Confirmed, officially: once per 12 months, and a landlord must give **at least 3 full months' written notice** using the proper Notice of Rent Increase form (RTB-7). Rent cannot be increased above the posted annual limit (no rounding up); landlords cannot "bank" an unused portion of a prior year's allowable increase. The limit is tied to the 12-month average change in the B.C. Consumer Price Index.

- Sources: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/rent-rtb/rent-increases (official rent-increase page, confirms 2026 = 2.3%, 2027 = 2.2%); https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025HMA0067-000786 (official B.C. Government news release announcing the 2026 rate and reconfirming the 3-month-notice / once-per-12-months rules); https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/forms/rtb7.pdf (official Notice of Rent Increase form, RTB-7). All accessed 11 Oct 2026.

### 7.4 Residential Tenancy Branch (RTB) role

The RTB administers the Residential Tenancy Act and provides dispute-resolution/arbitration services between landlords and tenants (e.g., over deposit returns, illegal rent increases, improper eviction notices). Filing fee for dispute resolution: $100 (fee waivers available for low-income applicants). Support available in 200+ languages by phone (1-800-665-8779).

- Source: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/solving-problems/tenancy-dispute-resolution/participatory-process/fees-and-fee-waivers and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/resources_for_renters.pdf. Accessed 11 Oct 2026.

### 7.5 Condition Inspection Reports

Official RTB Form **RTB-27 ("Condition Inspection Report")** is used at move-in and move-out. Landlord and tenant (or representatives) walk through the unit together, document condition, and both sign. The landlord must give the tenant **two opportunities** to attend the inspection (written notice required for the second). The landlord must provide the tenant a copy of the completed report **within 7 days of the move-in inspection** and **within 15 days of the move-out inspection** (or of receiving the tenant's forwarding address). These reports are the key evidence for any dispute over the deposit.

- Sources: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/starting-a-tenancy/move-in-condition-inspection; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/starting-a-tenancy/move-in-condition-inspection/complete-condition-inspection-report; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/information-sheets/inspecting_the_rental_unit.pdf. All accessed 11 Oct 2026.

### 7.6 Scam avoidance

I did not find a single, dedicated gov.bc.ca "how to avoid rental scams" page, but the official RTA provisions above (no application fees; deposit caps; the requirement for a written tenancy agreement) are the legal backbone of the standard, widely repeated consumer-protection advice: **do not pay any money (deposit, "application fee," "credit check fee") before you have personally viewed the unit, confirmed the identity/authority of the person renting it to you, and have a signed written tenancy agreement.** Avoid wire transfers, e-transfers to unverified parties, cryptocurrency, or cash sent before viewing. This guidance is consistent across the general consumer-fraud sources I reviewed (BCIT Student Association housing-fraud resource sheet; general newcomer housing guides), but **mark the specific wording and any "official government scam checklist" as UNVERIFIED** since I did not locate one single authoritative gov.bc.ca scam-specific page — the authoritative/verified part is simply that application fees are illegal under RTA s.15 and that a landlord cannot lawfully demand money before a tenancy agreement exists.

## 10. GST / PST (context for the $1,500 + GST service fee)

Confirmed via the Canada Revenue Agency's own official GST/HST rate table and the Province of B.C.'s official PST page:

- **GST (federal) in British Columbia: 5%.** (Confirmed on the CRA's "GST/HST calculator (and rates)" page — B.C. is listed at 5% GST with no HST, since B.C. is not an HST-participating province.)
- **PST (B.C. provincial sales tax): general rate 7%** on most goods and services, with some different rates for specific categories (e.g., liquor 10%, accommodation 8%, vapour products 20%) — not relevant to a flat service fee. GST and PST are calculated separately (PST is applied to the pre-GST price).

So, for a flat **$1,500 + GST** professional/service fee (assuming it is a type of service not subject to PST — most professional services are GST-only, not PST-taxable, though I did not verify the specific service category against the PST exemption list), the GST component would be 5% of $1,500 = $75, for a total of $1,575. **I have not verified whether PST applies to this specific service** — B.C.'s PST generally applies to goods and only specific listed services (e.g., legal services, telecommunication services, software) are PST-taxable, so whether PST additionally applies depends on exactly what the service is; treat any PST applicability claim beyond the general 7% rate as **UNVERIFIED** without knowing the specific service category.

- Sources: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst/publications/small-business-guide; https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst (last updated 21 Sept 2026). All accessed 11 Oct 2026.
