A $275 rent hike is going viral on X right now.

This started as a post on my social channels. Here's that version.

## The story everyone's arguing about

The tenant: 4 years at $1,750, never late, never trashed the place.

The landlord: "taxes, insurance and repairs all went up. Rent's going up $275."

The comments: absolute chaos.

## My take as a B.C. realtor who works with both tenants and landlords

In B.C., that renewal email couldn't happen that way.

- For existing tenants, the 2026 cap is 2.3% (so about $40 on $1,750, not $275)
- Rent can only go up once every 12 months
- Landlords need to give 3 full months' written notice on the official Notice of Rent Increase form (RTB-7)

## To be fair to landlords

Rising costs are real. B.C. has a separate application process for certain additional increases, and a tenant can agree in writing to more than the cap. It just has to go through the proper channels, not a surprise email.

Honestly, the healthiest landlord-tenant relationships I see are the ones where both sides know the rules up front. Fewer viral fights, more 4-year tenancies.

## What this means for you

If you're renting in Greater Vancouver or the Tri-Cities, BC, and you get a rent increase notice, check it against the Residential Tenancy Branch rules and its rent increase calculator (linked in the sources below).
