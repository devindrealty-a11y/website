Vancouver: 4th in Canada for pay. Dead last for pay vs. rent.

This started as a post on my social channels. Here's the full version, with the method and sources behind it.

## Canada's best cities for work: the podium

You might have seen that "America's Best Cities for Work in 2026" map going around. I couldn't find a Canadian version, so I built one: our 23 biggest metros scored on job opportunity, pay, job security, career mobility, pay vs. rent and economic opportunity. All public StatCan and CMHC data.

The podium:

1. **Calgary**
2. **Regina**
3. **Québec City**

Calgary didn't win on vibes. It topped pay, career mobility AND economic opportunity.

And Vancouver? 11th out of 23.

## The Vancouver twist: pay vs. rent

Here's the twist: we're top 5 for pay and for economic opportunity. Take rent out of the math and Vancouver jumps to 5th. Leave it in and rent vs. pay ranks us 23rd of 23.

Here's how Vancouver placed in each category (rank out of 23):

- Job opportunity: 9th
- Compensation: 4th
- Job quality and security: 17th
- Career mobility: 9th
- Pay vs. rent: 23rd
- Economic opportunity: 3rd

For pay vs. rent, I compared a year of average two-bedroom rent in purpose-built apartments (CMHC, October 2025: $2,363 a month in Metro Vancouver) with average employment income from 2023 tax data ($64,106). That rent works out to about 44% of average employment income, the highest share of the 23 metros.

## How I built the ranking

The 23 metros are the census metropolitan areas with 200,000+ residents that the Labour Force Survey publishes. I used 12 indicators across six equally weighted categories, such as the unemployment rate, prime-age employment rate, average employment income, full-time share of jobs, employment growth, population growth, businesses per 1,000 residents and rent compared with pay. Each indicator was scaled 0 to 100 across the 23 metros, then averaged. The composite score is my own calculation from these tables, not an official ranking.

A few honest limits: income is an average, not a median, and comes from the 2023 tax year. CMHC rents cover purpose-built rentals only (not condos or basement suites), and they reflect what sitting tenants pay, not today's asking rents. Small metros also have more sampling error in the jobs data.

Full ranking, 1 to 23: Calgary, Regina, Québec City, St. John's, Saskatoon, Halifax, Ottawa-Gatineau, Toronto, Edmonton, Kitchener-Cambridge-Waterloo, Vancouver, Winnipeg, Montréal, Hamilton, Victoria, Sherbrooke, Oshawa, Abbotsford-Mission, London, Barrie, Kelowna, Windsor and St. Catharines-Niagara.

## What this means for you if you're moving here

My take? After 28 years in Greater Vancouver, this matches what I hear from people relocating here. The jobs are here, the paycheques are solid, and rent is the boss fight. The moves that go smoothly are the ones with a housing plan before the moving truck shows up.
