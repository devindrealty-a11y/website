$190K sounds like a lot. Here's what it actually leaves you in Canada's biggest cities.

This started as a post on my social channels. Here's that version, with the ranking and a short note on how I calculated it.

## What $190K leaves after tax and rent

I ran a $190,000 salary through 2026 federal + provincial income tax, CPP/EI (QPP/QPIP in Quebec) and 12 months of average 1-bedroom rent:

1. **Edmonton:** $116,711 left/yr ($9,726/mo)
2. **Calgary:** $113,987
3. **Saskatoon:** $112,071
4. **Regina:** $112,071
5. **Victoria:** $107,008
6. **Winnipeg:** $105,972
7. **Ottawa:** $102,477
8. **Vancouver:** $102,376
9. **Toronto:** $99,465
10. **Montreal:** $97,337
11. **Halifax:** $95,336

## The Vancouver surprise

The surprise: Vancouver isn't last. B.C.'s lower tax at this income almost cancels out the highest rent in the country, and Vancouver lands within $101 of Ottawa. Halifax and Montreal finish at the bottom, mostly because of provincial tax.

Vancouver has the priciest rent, but lower B.C. tax keeps it out of the basement.

## How I calculated it

These figures are my own calculation. They are approximations, not tax advice.

I used a single filer with $190,000 of employment income and basic credits only. That is one person, a full-year resident of the province, with no dependants and no RRSP or other deductions. Rent is 12 times the September 2026 average 1-bedroom asking rent from the Rentals.ca and Urbanation National Rent Report. Those are asking rents on listings, not CMHC in-place rents. Utilities and renter's insurance are not included.

I would expect the results within about $500 of a commercial calculator.

## What this means if you're moving here

Where would you rather earn $190K?

If you're a newcomer relocating to Greater Vancouver, budget from what's left after tax and rent, not from the $190,000 alone. On this list Vancouver is 8th, at $102,376 left for the year, within $101 of Ottawa.
