Friendly reminder: your mortgage rate hold has an expiry date. Kind of like the milk in your fridge. Go check both.

I posted that on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:share:7528957918432468992/), Facebook and X on November 18. Here is the same note, with the sources and the payment math filled in. Every figure below is as of November 18, 2026. The extra 0.25% is a scenario, not a forecast.

## Why it matters right now

The [Bank of Canada raised its target for the overnight rate by 25 basis points to 2.5%](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2026/10/fad-press-release-2026-10-28/) on October 28, with the Bank Rate at 2.75% and the deposit rate at 2.45%. It was the first increase since July 2023. In the press release, the Bank said: "There could be a need to increase the policy rate further, but given uncertainties, Governing Council agreed to proceed carefully."

The [next announcement is December 9, 2026](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/core-functions/monetary-policy/key-interest-rate/), and the following Monetary Policy Report is January 27, 2027. Economists and markets were split, as of mid-November, on whether it moves again. [CBC reported October inflation flat, unchanged at 3.1%](https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/cpi-inflation-october-9.7423293), and said a December hold was the likely call. A [Globe and Mail roundup](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/inside-the-market/article-how-todays-inflation-data-has-moved-markets-and-bets-for-future-boc/) of market pricing and economists had a hold as the modest favourite. That split is why I am not calling the next move. If rates rise another 0.25%, the payment change is real money, but how much depends on your mortgage size.

## What another 0.25% can cost

A simple rule of thumb at these rates: about $14 more a month for every $100,000 borrowed, if the rate rises 0.25%. On these two rates the exact step is $13.87 per $100,000, so the examples below are the real difference, rounded to the nearest dollar.

Each example uses a 25-year amortization, monthly payments and Canadian semi-annual compounding, comparing 4.34% with 4.59%:

- $500,000: about $69 more a month
- $800,000: about $111 more a month, or about $6,660 over a 5-year term
- $1 million: about $139 more a month

On the $800,000 example, a rate held at 4.34% is $4,357 a month. If that rate rises to 4.59%, it is $4,468 a month.

[WOWA.ca listed 4.34% as the lowest insured 5-year fixed in Canada](https://wowa.ca/mortgage-rates) as of November 18, 2026, at 10:17 a.m. ET, and the lowest 5-year fixed conventional, or insurable, rate around 4.39%. The 4.59% figure is 4.34% plus 0.25 percentage points: a labelled scenario, not a December 9 prediction, and not a rate a lender has offered you. Try your own size in the [mortgage affordability calculator](https://devindrealty-a11y.github.io/mortgage-calculator/), then ask a broker what you would actually be offered.

## Most holds last 90 to 120 days

Most lenders hold a pre-approved rate for 90 to 120 days. [WOWA's rate lock-in guide](https://wowa.ca/mortgage-rate-lock-in) says lenders commonly offer 30, 60, 90 or 120 days, with some longer. On its lender table, RBC and TD are at 120 days and BMO is at 130. [RBC's pre-approval page](https://www.rbcroyalbank.com/mortgages/getting-preapproved.html) says you can lock a rate for 120 days, and [TD advertises a 120-day rate hold](https://www.td.com/ca/en/personal-banking/products/mortgages/first-time-home-buyer/pre-approval). Confirm the BMO window on [BMO's pre-approval page](https://www.bmo.com/en-ca/main/personal/mortgages/pre-approval/). Yours is the date on your own approval. These are typical windows, not one client's expiry.

If you locked in before the October 28 hike, many of those holds run out between now and February. A 120-day hold from October 27 lands around February 24, 2027. A 120-day hold from mid-August lands around mid-December.

In the [Greater Vancouver REALTORS® October 2026 market report](https://www.gvrealtors.ca/market-watch/monthly-market-reports), Andrew Lis said: "With one quarter-point rate hike now behind us, and financial markets pricing in a slight chance of another quarter-point hike in December, it's possible some buyers may step in to take advantage of rate holds that will soon be expiring."

## A tool, not a reason

Buyers: a held rate is real money. If you were already close to buying, now is the time to get serious, and to confirm the exact expiry date with your broker.

Sellers: a few motivated buyers with a deadline could make the next few weeks busier than the headlines suggest. That is a read of the calendar, not a promise about prices.

My take: a rate hold is a tool, not a reason. Don't buy the wrong home just because a date is coming up. But do call your broker this week, find out exactly when yours ends, and ask if it can be extended.
