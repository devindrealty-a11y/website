## Day 1 to Day 3: health, licence and power

**Day 1: Apply for MSP.** All B.C. residents enrol in the [Medical Services Plan](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/health/health-drug-coverage/msp/bc-residents/eligibility-and-enrolment/how-to-enrol). The [Province of B.C.](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/health/health-drug-coverage/msp/bc-residents/eligibility-and-enrolment/how-to-enrol/coverage-wait-period) says coverage starts after a wait: the rest of the month you become a resident, plus two more months. Apply right away. If you're moving from another province, keep your old health plan during the wait.

**Day 2: Visit ICBC.** [ICBC](https://www.icbc.com/insurance/moving-travelling/moving-BC) gives you **90 days** to switch to a B.C. driver's licence, and **30 days** to register, license and insure your vehicle (commercial vehicles right away). Book an appointment at a driver licensing office. On the same visit, many people have the photo taken for a BC Services Card, which helps finish MSP enrolment.

**Day 3: Turn on the utilities.** Open a [BC Hydro](https://app.bchydro.com/accounts-billing/moving-electrical-connections/new-customer.html) account for electricity (new residential accounts carry a **$13.50 + GST** setup charge on the first bill, and some need a security deposit). If your home has natural gas, open a [FortisBC](https://www.fortisbc.com/accounts/open-close-or-move-your-account) account. While you're at it, book your internet installation. Coming from Alberta? See my [Alberta to BC Tri-Cities, BC checklist](/blog/2026-11-05-moving-from-alberta-to-bc-tri-cities-checklist.html).

## Day 4 to Day 7: mail, transit, school and a walk

**Day 4: Protect your stuff and your mail.** Ask your landlord whether they require tenant insurance, and get a policy that fits. Set up [Canada Post Mail Forwarding](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/personal/mail-forwarding.page) from your old Canadian address (the service needs a Canadian original address). Lettermail, Registered Mail and magazines are forwarded; parcels are not, so tell parcel senders your new address yourself. Buy the service a few days before you need it.

**Day 5: Get a Compass Card.** [TransLink](https://www.translink.ca/transit-fares/compass-card) uses a reloadable Compass Card to tap on for SkyTrain, buses and SeaBus. Buy one online, at a station vending machine or at select retailers, load Stored Value or a pass, and tap in (and out on SkyTrain and SeaBus). In the Tri-Cities, BC, the Millennium Line (the Evergreen Extension) gets you downtown without driving. For Coquitlam detail, see my [first 30 days in Coquitlam checklist](/blog/2026-11-06-relocating-to-coquitlam-bc-first-30-days.html).

**Day 6: Sort out school and a doctor.** Families in Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Port Moody, Anmore and Belcarra register with [School District No. 43 (Coquitlam)](https://www.sd43.bc.ca/registration-information). Eligible Canadian citizens, permanent residents and refugees who are ordinarily resident in B.C. usually register at their catchment school; work-permit and study-permit situations go through International Education. Once you have your Personal Health Number, sign up for a family doctor or nurse practitioner through the [Health Connect Registry](https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/health-connect-registry), or call 8-1-1.

**Day 7: Buy a real rain jacket.** Then go explore. Try [Rocky Point Park](https://www.portmoody.ca/parks-recreation-and-environment/parks/rocky-point-park/) in Port Moody, [Lafarge Lake](https://www.coquitlam.ca/facilities/facility/details/Town-Centre-Park-84) at Coquitlam Town Centre Park, or the [Traboulay PoCo Trail](https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/recreation-parks/parks-trails/traboulay-poco-trail) in Port Coquitlam. You live here now.
