Relocating to Coquitlam, BC is exciting, and the first month goes better when you know which tasks to knock off right away. Between power, transit, health coverage and (if you have kids) school registration, there is a short list of official steps that start the day you arrive.

I'm Devin Desaulniers, a Real Estate Associate with Axford Real Estate in Port Moody. I'm a lifelong Coquitlam local, I've lived in Greater Vancouver for 28 years, and I've been licensed since 2020. This checklist is for newcomers settling into Coquitlam in their first 30 days. For the wider region, start with my [guide to relocating to Greater Vancouver](/relocating-to-greater-vancouver.html), and peek at my [Coquitlam area page](/coquitlam.html) for neighbourhood context.

## Week one: power, water and your Compass Card

Open your electricity account before you unpack. [BC Hydro](https://app.bchydro.com/accounts-billing/moving-electrical-connections/new-customer.html) says new residential customers create a MyHydro profile, then open a new account with the address, phone number and (if you're renting) the landlord's name and contact information. There's a **$13.50 + GST** setup charge on the first bill, and some accounts need a security deposit. BC Hydro also warns that if nobody applies for service after the previous tenant leaves, power can be disconnected, so [apply before move-in day](https://app.bchydro.com/accounts-billing/bill-payment/customer-service-rules.html).

Ask your landlord what else is in your name. The [City of Coquitlam](https://www.coquitlam.ca/538/Utility-Fee) bills residential **property owners** each year for water, sewer and (for homes that get City collection) garbage and green waste. Most apartments and townhomes do not pay the City garbage fee because they use private collection. If you rent a condo or townhome, clarify what is included in your rent versus what you set up yourself (internet, gas if applicable).

Then get around. [TransLink](https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/skytrain/) lists the Millennium Line from VCC–Clark through Port Moody into Coquitlam, ending at **Lafarge Lake–Douglas**, with Coquitlam stops at **Coquitlam Central**, **Lincoln** and Lafarge Lake–Douglas (plus Burquitlam on the Burnaby/Coquitlam edge). Peak trains run about every 3 to 4 minutes. Buy a reloadable [Compass Card](https://www.translink.ca/translink/transit-fares/compass-card) online or at a station, load Stored Value or a pass, and tap in and out at SkyTrain gates. My post on [commuting from Port Moody to Vancouver](/blog/2026-11-04-commuting-from-port-moody-to-vancouver.html) covers the same line one stop west if you're comparing neighbourhoods.

## Health, licence and school in the first month

Apply for MSP as soon as you arrive. The [Province of BC](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/health/health-drug-coverage/msp/bc-residents/eligibility-and-enrolment/how-to-enrol/coverage-wait-period) says new residents wait the rest of the month they become residents **plus two more months** before coverage starts, and tells people moving from elsewhere in Canada to keep their former plan during that wait. Don't wait until month three to apply.

If you drive, [ICBC](https://www.icbc.com/insurance/moving-travelling/moving-BC) gives you **90 days** to switch to a BC driver's licence and **30 days** to register, license and insure your vehicle (commercial vehicles right away). Book the licence appointment early; many people finish MSP enrolment and get a BC Services Card at the same ICBC office. Coming from Alberta? My [Alberta to BC Tri-Cities, BC checklist](/blog/2026-11-05-moving-from-alberta-to-bc-tri-cities-checklist.html) goes deeper on those deadlines.

Families with school-age kids should register with [School District No. 43 (Coquitlam)](https://www.sd43.bc.ca/registration-information), which serves Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Port Moody, Anmore and Belcarra. Eligible Canadian citizens, permanent residents and refugees who are ordinarily resident in BC usually register at their catchment school (use the district's School Locator for your address). Work-permit, study-permit and some other situations go through International Education instead. SD43 notes that 2026–2027 registration is open; bring the new-student form and residency documents the school asks for.

## Relocating to Coquitlam, BC: parks and a 30-day checklist

When the paperwork is underway, get outside. The City describes [Mundy Park](https://www.coquitlam.ca/facilities/facility/details/Mundy-Park-59) (641 Hillcrest Street) as a 178-hectare urban forest with a **5.5 kilometre** multi-use path you can walk in about an hour, plus sports fields and an outdoor pool. [Como Lake Park](https://www.coquitlam.ca/Facilities/Facility/Details/Como-Lake-Park-26) (700 Gatensbury Street) has about a one-kilometre lakeside loop, a playground and seasonal fishing. Both are easy early wins for feeling at home.

### What this means for you: first 30 days

1. Open BC Hydro (and confirm water, garbage and internet with your landlord) before or on move-in day.
2. Get a Compass Card and do a test trip from Coquitlam Central or Lafarge Lake–Douglas at your real commute time.
3. Apply for MSP immediately; keep your old coverage through the wait.
4. Book ICBC for your licence within 90 days and handle the vehicle within 30 days.
5. Register kids at the SD43 catchment school as soon as you have a local address.
6. Walk Mundy Park or Como Lake once — it helps Coquitlam feel like yours.
