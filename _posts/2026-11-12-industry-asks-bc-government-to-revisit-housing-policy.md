B.C.'s real estate and development industry has a wish list for the incoming provincial government, and it's mostly about cost. According to a Nov. 12 report from BIV, published in the Richmond News, groups representing builders and REALTORS® want the new government to review the housing rules of the last few years and decide which to keep, change or drop.

I wrote about [what the new government ran on for housing](/blog/2026-10-26-bc-election-what-it-means-for-housing.html) after the election. This is the other side of it: what the people who build and sell homes are now asking for.

## What builders are asking for

Michael Drummond, CEO of the Urban Development Institute (UDI), summed it up this way: "We have a cost of delivery crisis. It costs more to build than what people can afford."

The article points to CMHC data showing new condo construction in Metro Vancouver hit a 15-year low in the first half of 2026, which it links to weak presales and high construction costs. Drummond's worry is the pipeline: once today's housing is sold, "there is no next new project."

UDI's asks, as reported:

- Pause the scheduled increases to the BC Energy Step Code and Zero Carbon Step Code, holding large buildings at Step 2 (roughly 20% more energy efficient than the older standard).
- Cancel the planned PST expansion to professional services.
- Bring in a development charge reduction program modelled on Ontario's recent approach.

## What the BC Real Estate Association wants

BCREA is also asking the government to review the Step Code and home-energy policies, stop the PST expansion, and reconsider housing-related taxes, including the speculation and vacancy tax. Its argument is that these add to the cost of buying a home.

Jasroop Gosal, BCREA's government relations manager, noted, "we haven't exactly had a shortage of housing policy," pointing to Bills 44 and 47, the flipping tax, the foreign buyer tax and housing targets, among others. As a first step, BCREA wants a permanent Provincial Housing Policy Roundtable that brings different parts of the industry together.

Jill Atkey of the BC Non-Profit Housing Association added a useful reality check: "One year is not very long when it comes to housing because of the development pipeline." What the sector mostly wants within a year is clarity on direction.

## What this means for you

These are requests, not decisions. Nothing has changed yet, and anything that does change will take time to show up in prices or supply. Here's how I'd think about it:

**Buyers:** today's market is quiet. Greater Vancouver REALTORS® reported 2,024 sales in October, down 10.2% from a year earlier and 22.5% below the 10-year seasonal average. That gives buyers time and room to negotiate now, whatever happens in Victoria. If you're weighing a presale in Coquitlam or Port Moody, the slowdown in new projects is worth keeping in mind. My [buyer's page](/buyers.html) explains how I help you work through it.

**Sellers:** watch for news on the speculation and vacancy tax and other housing taxes, but don't price your home on a policy that hasn't happened. Price for the market you're actually selling in.

**Renters and newcomers:** fewer new condo projects today can mean fewer new rentals a few years from now, because many investor-owned condos end up on the rental market.
