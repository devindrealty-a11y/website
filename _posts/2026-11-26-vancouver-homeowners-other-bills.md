Equifax Canada’s third-quarter credit report, out November 24, puts a number on something I hear from people who already own. The mortgage gets paid. Other bills are where the strain shows up. The [Vancouver Sun](https://vancouversun.com/news/robbing-peter-to-pay-paul-vancouver-mortgage-holders-facing-growing-strains-making-payments) asked Equifax for a Vancouver cut the next day. Missed payments on other loans rose 35.2% in a year among Vancouver mortgage holders.

## What Equifax counted

The national 90-day-plus mortgage balance delinquency rate was 0.32% in the third quarter of 2026, [Equifax reports](https://www.equifax.ca/about-equifax/newsroom/-/intlpress/rising-interest-rates-could-put-nearly-1-in-4-mortgage-holders-at-risk-of-added-financial-stress-as-mortgage-delinquencies-reach-13-year-high/). That is up 31.1% from a year earlier, the highest since Equifax started this measure in 2013, and the sixteenth quarterly rise in a row. Ontario and British Columbia account for 91% of the growth in those severely late accounts. A rate of 0.32% is still a small share of balances. The direction is what I am watching.

Rebecca Oakes, Equifax Canada’s vice-president of advanced analytics, said delinquencies “have been climbing for four years, and Ontario and B.C. continue to drive much of that increase.” The dollar value of late mortgages is rising faster than the number of them, which she said “signals that borrowers with larger mortgages are increasingly struggling to keep up with their payments.”

Negative equity means you owe more than the home is worth. Equifax still calls it uncommon: 1.85% of mortgages in British Columbia, against 5% in Ontario. Ontario holds an estimated 70% of Canada’s negative-equity mortgages. On the Sun’s pull, Vancouver accounts for 4.7% of those mortgages nationwide and Toronto for just over 12%, and Vancouver has three times fewer of them than Toronto.

Condos and 2022 purchases carry more of that risk. Equifax says about 7.2% of condominium mortgages are likely in negative equity, compared with 2.7% of freehold houses. For homeowners who bought in 2022, the share is 8.4%.

## Where the missed payments actually are

The local number is not the mortgage itself. The Sun’s Equifax comparison:

- Vancouver mortgage holders: missed payments on other loans, up 35.2% in a year.
- Mortgage holders across Canada: non-mortgage delinquency, up 9.8%, the same figure in [Equifax’s release](https://www.equifax.ca/about-equifax/newsroom/-/intlpress/rising-interest-rates-could-put-nearly-1-in-4-mortgage-holders-at-risk-of-added-financial-stress-as-mortgage-delinquencies-reach-13-year-high/).
- Vancouver residents with no mortgage: those other-loan misses, down 4.2%.

The Sun says that Vancouver rise is still 35% lower than Toronto’s, and below the Canadian average.

Broker Eitan Pinsky told the Sun people protect the house payment. “They feel their home is sacrosanct,” he said. “We see people robbing Peter to pay Paul when it comes to making their mortgage payments.” He has also seen clients who could not refinance because a lower value left them at 80% of the appraised price, the cap the Sun says most banks usually use. That is one broker’s recent files.

On Equifax’s city table, which covers everyone’s non-mortgage debt, Vancouver averaged $24,960 in the quarter, up 3.33% in a year. Toronto’s average on the same table was $22,512.

The [Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2026/10/fad-press-release-2026-10-28/) raised its policy rate by a quarter point, to 2.5%, on October 28, and set the next announcement for December 9. Equifax counts 9.1 million mortgage holders in Canada, about 1.4 million of them on a variable rate, plus 1.3 million people with a home equity line over $50,000. Those balances can move before a renewal. I am not calling December.

## What this means for you

If you own in Greater Vancouver, including the Tri-Cities, BC, do not paste 35.2% onto a Port Moody or Coquitlam street. Those figures are Vancouver and national. Oakes said negative equity “isn’t an immediate problem as long as they can keep up with their payments. The challenge comes if they need to sell or refinance.” Ask your lender what today’s appraisal does to the credit you can still draw. I don’t know your file, and I am not guessing a sale price.

If you are buying, a seller who is tight on equity may care about price and the completion date. Your approval still follows the lender’s appraisal, not the asking price. I walk through that on my [page for home buyers](/buyers.html), including in Coquitlam, Port Moody and Port Coquitlam.

If you are a newcomer renting first, this report is about owners’ credit, not your lease. It does not set what a landlord can ask. While you choose a neighbourhood, use the [guide to relocating to Greater Vancouver](/relocating-to-greater-vancouver.html) and my [tenant rental search](/tenants.html). Keep a payment you can carry without leaning on other credit.
