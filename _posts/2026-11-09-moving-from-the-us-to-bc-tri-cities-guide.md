If you're moving from the US to BC and planning to land in Coquitlam, Port Moody or Port Coquitlam, a few steps start before you even reach the border. Health coverage, your list of belongings and your car all have rules that are easier to handle from home than after you arrive.

I'm Devin Desaulniers, a Real Estate Associate with Axford Real Estate in Port Moody. I'm a lifelong Coquitlam local, I've lived in Greater Vancouver for 28 years, and I've been licensed since 2020. Here's the order I'd tackle things in as a newcomer from the States. For the bigger regional picture, start with my [guide to relocating to Greater Vancouver](/relocating-to-greater-vancouver.html).

## Moving from the US to BC: health coverage comes first

BC's Medical Services Plan is for residents. The [Province](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/health/health-drug-coverage/msp/bc-residents/eligibility-and-enrolment/are-you-eligible) defines a resident as a Canadian citizen or permanent resident who makes their home in BC and is here at least six months a year. Some study and work permit holders with permits valid for six months or more can also qualify.

Even when you qualify, coverage doesn't start right away. The [MSP wait period](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/health/health-drug-coverage/msp/bc-residents/eligibility-and-enrolment/how-to-enrol/coverage-wait-period) is the rest of the month you become a resident plus two more months. If you're arriving from outside Canada, the Province says to arrange private health insurance before you arrive and keep it until MSP starts, and notes most private plans don't cover pre-existing conditions, including pregnancy. Apply for MSP as soon as you land, not at the end of the wait.

## Your belongings and your car at the border

The [Canada Border Services Agency](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/travel-voyage/mrc-drc-eng.html) asks people settling in Canada to prepare two copies of a list of everything they're bringing, with value, make, model and serial numbers where they apply. Split it into goods coming with you and goods to follow. This matters: things that arrive later only qualify for duty- and tax-free entry if they were on your original list, and you hand that list to the officer at your first point of entry even if your truck is still weeks behind you. To qualify, the CBSA says you must have owned, possessed and used the goods before you arrived, and leased goods don't count.

Bringing a US vehicle takes more planning. [Transport Canada](https://tc.canada.ca/en/road-transportation/importing-vehicle/importing-vehicle-united-states-mexico) says you declare it at the border on the Vehicle Import Form (Form 1), it must be clear of recalls, and it must pass a Registrar of Imported Vehicles inspection within **45 days**. Some vehicles need modifications first, and one that fails the inspection can't stay in Canada. Check the compatibility list before you decide to drive it up. After that, [ICBC](https://www.icbc.com/insurance/moving-travelling/moving-BC) gives new residents 30 days to register, license and insure a vehicle in BC.

## Swapping your US driver's licence

You can drive on your valid US licence for up to 90 days after moving, then you need a BC licence. [ICBC](https://www.icbc.com/driver-licensing/moving-bc/moving-from-another-country) waives knowledge and road tests for passenger and motorcycle licences from most US states, and you'll surrender your US licence when you apply. Bring proof of your driving history: you need at least two years of non-learner experience to skip graduated licensing, and ICBC credits up to 15 years of experience for insurance.

## Renting in the Tri-Cities, BC from the US

If you want a place lined up before moving day, a few BC basics help you spot a fair deal:

- **Deposits.** A landlord can ask for up to half a month's rent as a security deposit ([Province of BC](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/starting-a-tenancy/deposits-fees)). My post on [BC rental deposits and scams](/blog/2026-10-31-bc-rental-deposits-and-scams.html) covers the red flags.
- **Credit history.** Your US credit file doesn't follow you here. My post on [renting in BC with no Canadian credit history](/blog/2026-10-24-renting-in-bc-with-no-canadian-credit-history.html) explains what to offer a landlord instead.
- **Sales tax.** BC's general PST rate is 7%, with some exceptions ([Province of BC](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst)). Budget for it when you furnish your new place.

## What this means for you: a US-to-BC checklist

1. Confirm your immigration status and whether it makes you eligible for MSP.
2. Buy private health insurance that starts the day you arrive.
3. Make two copies of your goods list, split into "with me" and "to follow."
4. Check your vehicle on the import compatibility list before deciding to bring it.
5. Book the RIV inspection within 45 days, then register and insure with ICBC within 30 days of arriving.
6. Gather your driving record and switch your licence within 90 days.
7. Pick your city by commute and lifestyle.
