Relocating to Port Coquitlam, BC puts you in one of the three Tri-Cities, BC communities, alongside Coquitlam and Port Moody, with a weekday commuter train straight to downtown Vancouver. It also comes with a few local habits, from how the West Coast Express works to how the City wants your garbage carts locked, that newcomers usually learn the hard way.

## Relocating to Port Coquitlam, BC: how the commute really works

The first surprise for many newcomers: the West Coast Express is the only train that stops inside Port Coquitlam. [TransLink](https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/west-coast-express/) describes it as a commuter rail line between downtown Vancouver and Mission City that runs on weekdays only, westbound in the morning and eastbound in the afternoon and evening. There's no weekend service.

From Port Coquitlam Station (2125 Kingsway, at the foot of Wilson Avenue), TransLink's weekday schedule shows five morning trains, from **6:04 a.m. to 8:04 a.m.**, each arriving at Waterfront Station **36 minutes** later. Five evening trains leave Waterfront between 3:50 p.m. and 6:20 p.m. If you miss the last one home, you'll be taking the Millennium Line to Coquitlam Central and a bus or a ride from there. That's why I suggest a test trip at your real commute time before you sign anything.

If your job isn't on a nine-to-five downtown schedule, SkyTrain access matters more. My post on [commuting from Port Moody to Vancouver](/blog/2026-11-04-commuting-from-port-moody-to-vancouver.html) explains the Millennium Line side of the Tri-Cities, BC.

## City services: carts, wildlife locks and recreation

Port Coquitlam runs its own curbside collection. According to the [City](https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/services/waste-collection), green carts (food scraps and yard waste) are picked up weekly, while blue recycling carts and grey garbage carts go out on alternating weeks based on your collection zone. Glass goes in a separate grey bin collected monthly. The City's [sorting guide](https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/media/file/sort-it-right-guide) says single-family homes get this service through their property taxes, while multi-family complexes arrange their own. So if you rent a condo or townhome, ask the landlord or strata how garbage works in that building.

If you rent a house or suite with City carts, know the wildlife rules. The City asks residents to secure garbage and food scraps in a garage, shed or wildlife-resistant cart lock, put carts out between **5:30 and 7:30 a.m.** on collection day, and secure them again by 7 p.m. The free Sort & Report app sends pickup reminders.

For staying active, the [Port Coquitlam Community Centre](https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/services/city-facilities/port-coquitlam-community-centre) at 2150 Wilson Avenue is a 205,000-square-foot hub downtown with three arenas, a leisure pool, a fitness centre and a gym. The [Terry Fox Library](https://www.fvrl.bc.ca/locations/terry-fox-library), run by Fraser Valley Regional Library, is in the same building. A library card is a cheap, easy way to find programs and free Wi-Fi while you settle in. On the north side, [Hyde Creek Recreation Centre](https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/services/city-facilities/hyde-creek-recreation-centre) (1379 Laurier Avenue) has a lap pool, leisure pool, waterslide, gym and fitness room.

## Schools, trails and settling in

Port Coquitlam is part of [School District No. 43 (Coquitlam)](https://www.sd43.bc.ca/registration-information), which also serves Coquitlam, Port Moody, Anmore and Belcarra. Families usually register at their catchment school using the district's School Locator, so confirm the catchment for any address before you apply for a rental.

Then get outside. The City calls the [Traboulay PoCo Trail](https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/recreation-parks/parks-trails/traboulay-poco-trail) a **25-kilometre** loop of gravel and paved paths that circles the whole community, through forest, marsh and riverfront, open to walkers, cyclists and even horseback riders. You don't have to do it all at once. Picking up one section near home is a great way to learn the city.

For a sense of size, [Statistics Canada's 2021 Census](https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2021/dp-pd/prof/details/page.cfm?DGUIDlist=2021A00055915039&GENDERlist=1&HEADERlist=0&Lang=E&STATISTIClist=1) counted **61,498** people in Port Coquitlam, up 4.9% from 2016.

### What this means for you: a PoCo newcomer checklist

1. Match your commute to the West Coast Express schedule (weekdays only), or plan your route to a SkyTrain station.
2. Ask whether your rental uses City carts or private building collection, and who handles them.
3. Get a wildlife-resistant setup for your carts and download the Sort & Report app.
4. Sign up for a Terry Fox Library card and check the community centre's drop-in schedules.
5. Confirm the SD43 catchment school before you commit to an address.
6. Walk one stretch of the Traboulay PoCo Trail in your first week.
