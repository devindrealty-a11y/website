Port Coquitlam council meets tonight with a recommendation to pause new applications that rely on the province's multiplex rules. I'm writing this the morning of the meeting, so this is the agenda, not a decision. If you own a house there, or you buy or rent in the Tri-Cities, BC, the rules themselves have not changed yet.

## What the multiplex rules actually are

A multiplex is more than one home on a lot that used to hold a single house: a suite, a duplex, a triplex or a few townhomes. It is not a tower.

The province calls this small-scale multi-unit housing, shortened to SSMUH. It comes from Bill 44, a 2023 law from the previous NDP government. The [Province's page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/local-governments-and-housing/housing-initiatives/smale-scale-multi-unit-housing), last updated January 20, 2026, still lists the requirements. In municipalities over 5,000 people, a lot zoned only for a house or a duplex generally has to allow at least three homes on 280 square metres or less, and four on a larger lot. Near frequent bus service the minimum can be six. A suite or backyard cottage is covered where that minimum does not apply, and cities had to update zoning by June 30, 2024. [CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/metro-vancouver-mayor-pushing-to-suspend-housing-applications-relying-on-ndps-bill-44/) reported Monday that the B.C. Conservatives have said Bill 44 is "on the outs." That page has not been rewritten to say the rules are gone.

## What is on tonight's agenda

Council meets Tuesday, November 24, 2026, at 5 p.m. at City Hall, 2580 Shaughnessy Street. The [agenda](https://pub-portcoquitlam.escribemeetings.com/Meeting.aspx?Agenda=Agenda&Id=36e464c8-053c-4e36-9535-ce237ca82b3a&lang=English) recommends two directions for staff. Nothing had been voted on when I published this. Check the city afterward for the result.

First, staff would suspend consideration of new applications that specifically rely on those provincial density rules, until the province clarifies the changes it intends. The wording is about new files. It does not say what happens to an application already in.

Second, staff would review the city's zoning and bring ideas back on duplexes, townhomes and other ground-oriented homes, lot sizes, whether a project is realistic, city services, floodplain and flood protection, and parking and access.

Mayor Brad West told CTV News he is not waiting for a repeal, or for the spring legislative session. "The government didn't listen; they rammed it through," he said. "We saw the consequence of Bill 44; I don't think it's been successful."

## The provincial rules have not been repealed

A new cabinet was [sworn in on November 20](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026PREM0069-001142). Cathie Ounsted, the minister whose portfolio includes housing, told CTV News the government will review "any legislative requirements that may be required" and put decisions "closer to the communities they affect." That is still ahead of us. A Port Coquitlam motion, even if it passes tonight, does not scrap Bill 44.

I wrote about [what the new government ran on for housing](/blog/2026-10-26-bc-election-what-it-means-for-housing.html) and about [what builders and REALTORS® asked Victoria to revisit](/blog/2026-11-12-industry-asks-bc-government-to-revisit-housing-policy.html). This week is the local step. The Vancouver Sun reported November 2 that Doerkson had promised to repeal Bill 44, and that West has long called the province's approach "one-size-fits-all." He was one of 16 Metro Vancouver mayors who asked in December 2025 for Bills 43 and 44 to be rolled back. That is background, not tonight's vote.

## What this means for you

**Homeowners in Port Coquitlam who want to add homes, or sell a lot a buyer might want for a multiplex:** the recommendation targets new applications that rely on the provincial density rules. If you already have a file in, or you are only adding a suite, ask the city whether a pause would cover you before you pay for drawings. I would not price a sale or an offer on multiplex potential while this sits in front of council. Developer Luke Mari told CTV News that turning away a housing type that is still legal could lead someone who has already spent money to sue, and West said he is not concerned. I am not predicting either result. My [page for home buyers](/buyers.html) is how I help people work out what a property can be used for.

**Buyers:** a listing that leans on several homes on one lot is using rules both city hall and the legislature may change. Ask what today's zoning allows, and treat the rest as uncertain.

**Renters and newcomers:** multiplexes and suites are one way more rentals show up on existing streets. A pause, if council passes one, could slow that future supply in Port Coquitlam. It does not take homes already for rent off the market.
