Where could Canada's population grow most by 2050?

This started as a map I shared on my social channels yesterday. Here's that version, with a bit more room to explain what the numbers are, and what they aren't.

## The big picture: a very uneven map

Statistics Canada's medium-growth M1 scenario points to a very uneven map between July 1, 2025 and July 1, 2050:

- **Canada:** 41.65M to 48.69M, up 7.03M (+16.9%).
- **Alberta:** up 2.19M (+43.6%), the fastest percentage growth among provinces.
- **Ontario:** up 2.92M (+18.0%), the largest numerical increase.
- **B.C.:** up 892,700 (+15.7%).
- **Quebec:** down 11,100 (−0.1%). **Newfoundland and Labrador:** down 5,600 (−1.0%).

A few other provinces on the map: Prince Edward Island +28.0%, Saskatchewan +25.3%, New Brunswick +21.7%, Manitoba +20.5% and Nova Scotia +13.2%.

## Why 2050 rather than 2100?

That's the furthest year in StatCan's latest provincial and territorial projections. The Canada-only series goes to 2075, but this map keeps every region on the same timeline.

These are conditional projections, not predictions. Changes in migration, fertility and other assumptions can change the picture.

## What this means for the Tri-Cities, BC

For Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam and Port Moody, this is a useful backdrop for thinking about housing supply, transportation and services — not a forecast for any individual neighbourhood or for home prices. The projection is province-wide; it doesn't break out the Tri-Cities, BC, and I'm not going to pretend it does.

What it does tell me is that B.C. is projected to keep growing, by close to 900,000 people in this scenario. A lot of that growth comes from people moving here, so newcomers will keep being a big part of the story for rentals and first purchases in Greater Vancouver.
