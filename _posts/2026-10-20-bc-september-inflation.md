Statistics Canada released the September Consumer Price Index on October 19. Canada's all-items CPI rose 3.1% from a year earlier, after a 3.0% increase in August. In British Columbia, the 12-month change stayed at 3.0%.

Gasoline did most of the lifting. The line I watch for clients in Greater Vancouver is shelter. That is the one that sits next to a mortgage payment or a rent cheque in the Tri-Cities, BC.

## What moved, and what did not

Gasoline prices were 27.8% higher than a year earlier. In August that yearly increase had been 22.8%. Statistics Canada says crude oil reached its highest level since May, amid ongoing uncertainty about the wars in the Middle East. Energy prices rose 19.0% year over year, after a 15.4% gain in August.

Take gasoline out, and the CPI rose 2.2%, after 2.4% in August. Month to month, the national CPI rose 0.1%. On a seasonally adjusted basis it rose 0.4%.

British Columbia did not follow that national month. From August to September, B.C.'s CPI fell 0.2%. The 12-month rate was 3.0% in August and 3.0% again in September. Statistics Canada says prices rose at a faster yearly pace in five provinces, mainly because of gasoline and fuel oil. B.C. was not one of those five.

This release does not publish a separate rate for Vancouver, Coquitlam, Port Moody or Port Coquitlam. I will not invent one. B.C.'s 3.0% is the closest official reading we have for Greater Vancouver this month.

## Shelter, rent and mortgage interest

Shelter prices rose 1.8% year over year, up from 1.5% in August. Statistics Canada points to faster growth in homeowners' home and mortgage insurance (4.9%) and homeowners' maintenance and repairs (1.2%), plus fuel oil and other fuels, up 57.8% year over year after 43.7% in August. The agency says that fuel-oil increase had a greater impact in Atlantic Canada, where fuel oil is more widely used.

Two owned-home measures fell at the same yearly rate as in August. The homeowners' replacement cost index was down 1.9%. The mortgage interest cost index was down 0.2%. That figure is a national price index, not a quoted mortgage rate.

Rent rose 2.8% year over year, matching August.

Food purchased from stores rose 1.7% year over year, after 2.8% in August. Statistics Canada says that is the smallest yearly grocery increase since May 2024 (1.5%). Grocery prices are still up 33.0% since September 2020.

## The rate decision on October 28

The Bank of Canada's target for the overnight rate is 2.25%. It has been 2.25% since October 29, 2025, when the Bank lowered it by 0.25 percentage points from 2.50%. The Bank left it unchanged on September 2, 2026. The next announcement is Wednesday, October 28, with a Monetary Policy Report.

September's CPI is the latest inflation reading before that date. The October CPI comes out Monday, November 16.

I am not going to predict October 28. If you have a variable-rate mortgage, the overnight target is the number to watch that day. This release does not quote a five-year fixed rate, and I will not invent one.
