Two families who'd never met teamed up on a $1.85M house in Vancouver. Each family gets its own suite and kitchen. They share the basement, the yard... and a freezer.

I shared this story on my social channels yesterday. Here's the blog version, with a little more room for the details and what I'd tell you if you're thinking about it.

## Two families, one house

According to BBC News, the two families bought a six-bedroom detached house together in 2023 for C$1.85M. They split the C$250K deposit and the roughly C$3,900 a month mortgage 50/50, along with bills and repairs. The Vancouver Sun reports each family has its own 3-bed, 2-bath suite, and they share spaces like a playroom, a workshop and the yards.

Three years in, they say it's still working and has saved both families a lot of money.

## Here's why I like it

- It turns "one family can't afford a house" into "two families can"
- You get the yard and the space instead of a small condo
- Costs like the mortgage, bills and repairs get split down the middle

## What I'd tell any client thinking about it

- **Get a proper co-ownership agreement drawn up by a lawyer before you buy.** The BBC piece describes formal agreements with things like notice periods and a first right to buy the other owner's share.
- **Plan the exit on day one:** what happens if one family wants to sell?
- **Talk mortgage, title and who pays for the new roof BEFORE you fall in love with a house.**

## What this means for you

My take: it's not for everyone, but it's one of the most creative ways I've seen people get into a detached home around here. Pick your co-owners like a business partner, not a roommate.
