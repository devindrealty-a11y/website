If you need temporary housing in the Tri-Cities, BC while you look for a rental, plan for a hotel or a room in someone's home rather than a whole vacation apartment. In Port Moody, Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam, the city rules mean most legal short-term rentals are rooms in a host's own house, so the more of your rental search you finish before you arrive, the shorter that stay can be.

I'm Devin Desaulniers, a Real Estate Associate with Axford Real Estate in Port Moody. I grew up in Coquitlam and I've lived in Greater Vancouver for 28 years. A lot of the newcomers I work with ask me where to stay for the first couple of weeks, so here's what I tell them.

## How short-term rentals work in the Tri-Cities, BC

The [Province of BC](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/short-term-rentals/information/visitors) defines a short-term rental as accommodation offered to the public for money for **less than 90 consecutive days**. Hotels and motels don't count. Every host has to register with the provincial registry each year and show a provincial registration number on their online listings, plus a local business licence number where the city requires one.

Port Moody, Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Anmore and Belcarra are all on the Province's [list of communities with the principal residence requirement](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/short-term-rentals/principal-residence-requirement). That limits short-term rentals to the host's own home plus one secondary suite or accessory dwelling unit (such as a laneway home) on the same property. Local bylaws can be stricter, and in the Tri-Cities, BC they are:

- **Port Moody:** since February 4, 2025, short-term rental hosts can only operate out of a bed and breakfast or boarding business, with a City licence ([City of Port Moody](https://www.portmoody.ca/home-and-property/housing/rental-housing/)).
- **Coquitlam:** a permanent resident has to live in the home and run the rental, with a maximum of two guests or one family at a time and no separate kitchen in the rental space ([City of Coquitlam](https://coquitlam.ca/602/Business-Licences)).
- **Port Coquitlam:** up to two bedrooms without cooking facilities and up to four guests, and the operator has to live in the home as their principal residence ([City of Port Coquitlam](https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/media/file/accesory-home-business-zoning-bylaw-3630-str-update-002)).

In plain terms: if you're picturing a whole furnished condo with its own kitchen for a few weeks, you'll find fewer legal options in the Tri-Cities, BC than you might expect. A private room in a host's home or a hotel is the more realistic plan, especially for a family.

## What temporary housing in the Tri-Cities, BC does (and doesn't) give you

Two things surprise newcomers here.

First, BC's [Residential Tenancy Act](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/02078_01) doesn't apply to living accommodation occupied as vacation or travel accommodation. A nightly or weekly stay is a booking, not a tenancy, so refunds and cancellations follow the host's and platform's policy, as the [Province](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/short-term-rentals/information/visitors) points out. Read those terms before you pay.

Second, the registration number is your quick check. The Province says only registered short-term rentals can be advertised and booked, and guests aren't fined for staying somewhere that turns out to be non-compliant. The responsibility sits with the host and platform. Still, a listing with no registration number is a reason to move on, and I'd be cautious with anyone who asks you to pay outside the platform.

## What this means for you: a temporary housing checklist

1. **Shrink the gap first.** Start your rental search 4 to 6 weeks before your move so you need days in temporary housing, not weeks.
2. **Book refundable if you can.** Move-in dates can shift, so flexible cancellation terms are worth a lot.
3. **Check every listing for a provincial registration number** and, where the city requires one, a business licence number.
4. **Stay near transit if you'll be viewing homes.** A base near a SkyTrain station makes it easier to see places across Port Moody and Coquitlam. My post on [living near Inlet Centre Station](/blog/2026-11-11-living-near-inlet-centre-station-port-moody.html) shows what's within walking distance of one.
5. **Know the deposit rules for your real rental.** Once you sign a regular tenancy, a BC landlord can ask for a security deposit of no more than half a month's rent, plus up to half a month as a pet damage deposit if pets are allowed ([Province of BC](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/starting-a-tenancy/deposits-fees)).
6. **Pick your area before you land.** My [Port Moody](/port-moody.html), [Coquitlam](/coquitlam.html) and [Port Coquitlam](/port-coquitlam.html) guides, plus my [newcomer's guide to relocating to Port Moody](/blog/2026-11-02-relocating-to-port-moody-bc-newcomers-guide.html), can help you narrow it down.
