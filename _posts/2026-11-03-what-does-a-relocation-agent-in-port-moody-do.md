A relocation agent in Port Moody helps a newcomer find and secure a place to live before they arrive, by searching listings, viewing homes on their behalf and reviewing the lease with them. In BC, someone who does this for a fee needs to be a licensed real estate professional, so the first thing to ask any relocation agent is whether they're licensed.

## Why the licence matters in BC

The BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) regulates real estate professionals in the province. In its [guide to rental licence categories](https://www.bcfsa.ca/about-us/news/blog/know-your-licence-trading-services-vs-rental-property-management), BCFSA says that helping "a prospective tenant to find a property to rent" is a trading services activity, and that only trading services licensees can enter into a contract with someone looking for a place to rent.

Anyone can call themselves a relocation helper, but you can check a licence yourself. BCFSA's free [Find a Professional search](https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/find-professional) shows a licensee's licence category, contact details, any licence conditions and recent discipline history.

## What it doesn't cover

To set expectations clearly, a relocation agent finding you a rental isn't the same as a moving company, an immigration consultant or a lawyer. I don't pack or ship your things, give immigration advice or give legal advice. I'm also on your side as the tenant, so I'm not managing the building or collecting rent for the landlord.

The paperwork that protects you still comes from BC tenancy law. The Province says landlords must prepare a [written tenancy agreement](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/starting-a-tenancy/tenancy-agreements) for every tenancy and give the tenant a copy within 21 days of signing. It also says the security deposit and the pet damage deposit can't each be more than half of one month's rent. For a deeper look at deposits and scams, see my post on [renting in Vancouver before you move](/blog/2026-10-10-how-to-rent-in-vancouver-before-you-move.html).

## Checklist: working with a relocation agent from out of town

If you're planning a move to Port Moody or anywhere in the Tri-Cities, BC, here's what I'd do:

1. **Reach out 4-6 weeks before your move.** That's usually enough time to line up homes that will actually be free on your move-in date.
2. **Check the licence on BCFSA's Find a Professional search** before you sign anything or pay a fee.
3. **Send your must-haves in writing:** budget, bedrooms, pets, parking and your commute. If you'll take the SkyTrain or West Coast Express, say so.
4. **Ask for a live video walkthrough** rather than relying only on listing photos.
5. **Read the tenancy agreement before you sign,** and keep a copy.
6. **Plan the move-in inspection.** The Province says the [move-in condition inspection](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/starting-a-tenancy/move-in-condition-inspection) should happen on the day you get the keys unless you agree on another time, and the landlord must give you a copy of the report within 7 days. If you can't attend, you can send someone in your place, but you must tell the landlord their name in writing first.
