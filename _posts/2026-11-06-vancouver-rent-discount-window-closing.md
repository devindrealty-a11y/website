Vancouver rents are going up again. 📈🏠

This started as a post on my social channels yesterday. Here's that version, with a bit more room for the numbers.

## The first yearly bump after 34 months

After almost 3 years of falling rents, Vancouver apartment and condo asking rents rose 0.2% compared with a year ago, to an average of $2,735. That's the first yearly increase after 34 straight months of declines, according to the Rentals.ca and Urbanation National Rent Report for October 2026 (released November 5).

It's a small rise, not a spike. Month over month, Vancouver actually slipped 0.2%. The story is the turn, not a sudden jump.

Urbanation's Shaun Hildebrand put it plainly: "The window for cheaper rents is closing in Toronto and Vancouver."

## Where other cities landed

Year over year for apartments and condos:

- Toronto: +1.6% (first annual gain after 32 months)
- Ottawa: +0.3%
- Vancouver: +0.2%
- Montreal: −0.3%
- Calgary: −2.4%
- Canada: −2.5%

Canada overall is still down year over year. This is a big-city shift.

## What it means in the Tri-Cities, BC and Metro Van

**Renters** who've been waiting — if the right place shows up, moving sooner may beat hoping for another soft year. Soft new-build incentives (like free months) can still pop up briefly, but I wouldn't bank on 2027 looking like the last two soft years.

**Landlords** — finally a signal the long slide may be easing.

One tiny bump isn't a crisis. Direction matters more than the size of the move.
