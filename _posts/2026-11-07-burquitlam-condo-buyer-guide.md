If you're buying a condo in Burquitlam, start with three things: how you'll use Burquitlam Station on the Millennium Line, what the City of Coquitlam's neighbourhood plan allows near that station, and what the strata documents say about the building—not just how the suite looks. Burquitlam is the southwest Coquitlam neighbourhood on the Burnaby border where SkyTrain access and ongoing redevelopment meet, so those checks matter more here than a generic "Coquitlam condo" search.

I'm often asked by buyers and newcomers looking for a realtor in Burquitlam what to know before they write an offer. This is the short version. For more detail, see my [Burquitlam neighbourhood guide](/burquitlam-neighbourhood-guide.html) and [Coquitlam condo buyer page](/coquitlam-condo-buyer.html).

## SkyTrain is why most buyers look here

Burquitlam Station sits on Clarke Road on the Millennium Line. It opened on December 2, 2016, with TransLink's Evergreen Extension, linking Lougheed Town Centre through Port Moody to Coquitlam City Centre. One stop west at Lougheed Town Centre you can transfer to the Expo Line toward Downtown Vancouver. That commute pattern is why many Burquitlam condo buyers care about distance to the station more than finishes.

The City plans Burquitlam with Lougheed as the Burquitlam-Lougheed neighbourhood—about 560 hectares along the North Road / Clarke Road corridor, between Burnaby, Port Moody, the Trans-Canada Highway, and Austin Heights / Maillardville. Since SkyTrain arrived at Lougheed in 2002 and at Burquitlam in 2016, the plan has aimed growth at transit-supportive housing near the stations.

## The neighbourhood plan and transit-oriented rules

Coquitlam's [Burquitlam-Lougheed Neighbourhood Plan](https://coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/17458/Official-Community-Plan---Schedule-J-PDF) (Schedule J-2 of the Official Community Plan, consolidated May 27, 2026) puts the tallest, densest buildings next to SkyTrain stations, the neighbourhood centres and along North Road, then steps down farther out. It calls for a mix of highrise and low-rise apartments, townhouses and small-scale homes, plus shops and services in the centres.

Provincial transit-oriented area rules add another layer. The City lists Burquitlam Station among eight SkyTrain stations where land within **800 metres** is a Transit-Oriented Area, with minimum heights and densities set by the Province and most residential parking minimums removed (except for people with disabilities). On May 27, 2026, Council also adopted updated land-use plans for the Southwest Shoulders and Corridors around Burquitlam and Lougheed Town Centre, opening more room for medium-density housing and townhouses beside the higher-density station areas.

**What that means for a buyer:** a quiet side street of older houses can sit on land designated for future towers or townhouses. Before you remove subjects, check the address on the City's QtheMap tool or ask planning staff (DevInfo@coquitlam.ca), and have your agent flag nearby redevelopment.

## Prices are citywide—the building file is local

Greater Vancouver REALTORS® does not publish a Burquitlam-only benchmark. For context, the October 2026 MLS® Home Price Index apartment benchmark for **Coquitlam as a whole** was **$624,200**, down 9.1% from a year earlier. That sat below the Metro Vancouver apartment benchmark of **$673,400** (down 5.9% year over year) in the same month's market report. Coquitlam recorded **82** apartment sales that month. Those citywide numbers are only a starting point—two towers a block apart can trade very differently once strata fees, parking and upcoming repairs enter the picture.

Before you remove subjects, get the [Form B Information Certificate](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/paperwork-for-buyers-and-sellers/form-b-information-certificate). The Province says the strata must provide it within seven days of a request. It shows monthly strata fees, special levies, the contingency reserve fund, parking and locker allocations, and a summary of insurance, and it must attach the rules, current budget and most recent depreciation report. I also want the last two years of council, AGM and special general meeting minutes, plus the bylaws. That file tells you more about living costs than the listing photos do.

## What this means for you

If you're a first-time or newcomer buyer comparing Burquitlam with City Centre or Northeast Coquitlam, decide the commute first, then read the strata file and the land-use designation.
