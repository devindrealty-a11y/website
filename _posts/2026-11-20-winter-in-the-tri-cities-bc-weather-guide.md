Winter in the Tri-Cities, BC is mild and wet. At Environment and Climate Change Canada's Port Moody Glenayre station, the 1971–2000 climate normals put a typical January day at a daily average of 3.0°C, a daily maximum of 5.5°C and a daily minimum of 0.6°C, and 1,896.9 mm of the year's 1,955.3 mm of precipitation falls as rain.

## What winter in the Tri-Cities, BC feels like

Glenayre is in Port Moody, at 129.5 metres. An average year there brings 58.4 cm of snow. November is the wettest month, at 297.0 mm of precipitation. December is 275.6 mm, January is 249.6 mm, and July is 67.9 mm. There are 176.7 days a year with at least 0.2 mm of precipitation, and 9.6 days with at least 0.2 cm of snow. Snow depth at the end of January averages 1 cm. The overnight low is at or below 0°C on 13.7 January days.

Coquitlam Como Lake Avenue, at 160 metres, tells the same story in the 1981–2010 normals: 1,922.8 mm of precipitation a year, 1,855.5 mm of it rain and 67.3 cm of snow. November is 308.5 mm, and average January snow depth is 3 cm. These are long-term averages, not a forecast for this winter. Neither station describes Burke Mountain or Westwood Plateau, so ask about the last snowfall on a hillside street. See my [Coquitlam guide](/coquitlam.html).

## Winter tires on the highway, and city streets

On many Lower Mainland city streets, the [Province of British Columbia](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/transportation/driving-and-cycling/traveller-information/seasonal/winter-driving/winter-tire-and-chain-up-routes) does not require winter tires. You are still responsible for the conditions you drive in, and the province notes that many drivers use M+S tires year-round.

On a signed highway, winter tires or chains are required on most routes from October 1 to April 30. On select highways outside mountain passes and high-snowfall areas, that ends March 31. A passenger vehicle without the required winter tires draws a $121 fine under section 125 of the Motor Vehicle Act ([enforcement page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/transportation/driving-and-cycling/traveller-information/seasonal/winter-driving/enforcement)).

A legal winter tire needs at least 3.5 mm of tread and a sidewall marked M+S or with the three-peaked mountain and snowflake ([passenger-vehicle page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/transportation/driving-and-cycling/traveller-information/seasonal/winter-driving/about-winter-tires)). Chains on summer tires are not an acceptable substitute on a signed highway. Before the Coquihalla, the Fraser Canyon or the Sea-to-Sky, check the sign and [DriveBC](https://www.drivebc.ca/).

## What this means for you: a newcomer checklist

- **Rain kit.** A waterproof jacket and shoes for November through January, plus one warm layer for nights at or below freezing.
- **Highway tires.** Legal winter tires before October 1 if you will drive a signed route. Many Lower Mainland city streets have no such rule.
- **Sidewalks.** [Coquitlam](https://www.coquitlam.ca/180/Boulevard-Maintenance) requires snow and ice off the sidewalk within 10 hours after a snowfall ends or ice stops forming. [Winter Wise](https://www.coquitlam.ca/354/Winter-Wise-Program) says pile snow at the back of the driveway, and clear the catch basin so rain can drain. [Port Moody](https://www.portmoody.ca/resident-services/roads-traffic-and-construction/snow-and-ice-removal/) gives residents, stratas and businesses 24 hours after a snowfall or storm, or until 10 a.m. on a Priority Pedestrian Route, with fines of $100, or $100 to $500 on a priority route. [Port Coquitlam](https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/services/roads-transportation/snow-removal-ice-control) gives owners 24 hours after it stops snowing. Check the lease for who shovels.
- **Damp on a viewing.** [Fraser Health](https://www.fraserhealth.ca/health-topics-a-to-z/air-quality/indoor-air-quality) says mould grows in damp homes. Run bathroom and kitchen fans, and fix leaks quickly. On a viewing I check for a musty smell, stained sills, and a fan that runs.
- **Tenant insurance.** [PreparedBC](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/safety/emergency-management/preparedbc/make-your-plan/insurance) says a landlord's policy does not cover your belongings or extra living costs if you have to leave. Ask a broker whether overland water is included.
- **Outages.** [BC Hydro](https://www.bchydro.com/safety-outages/power-outages/prepare-for-outages/prepare-your-home.html) asks for a 72-hour kit with water, food, light, a radio and medications. A full fridge keeps food cold for about four hours, a full freezer up to 48 hours, and a half-full freezer about 24 hours, if the doors stay shut ([BC Hydro](https://www.bchydro.com/news/conservation/2026/storm-season-is-coming.html)). Stay at least 10 metres from a downed line and call 911 ([BC Hydro](https://www.bchydro.com/news/press_centre/news_releases/2026/storm-response-continues.html)). Report an outage at [bchydro.com/outages](https://www.bchydro.com/outages) or 1-800-224-9376. Renters without an account number can stay on the line.
