Buying a presale condo in Port Moody or Coquitlam means you sign a contract today for a home the developer will finish later, and you pay deposits that must be held in trust by a lawyer, notary or real estate brokerage. Under B.C.'s Real Estate Development Marketing Act (REDMA), you get the developer's disclosure statement before you sign and a seven-day window to cancel in writing with no penalty.

That's the short answer. Here's how it works in practice, and what I'd look at before signing anything in Burquitlam, Moody Centre or anywhere else in the Tri-Cities, BC.

## How a presale purchase works in B.C.

The [BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA)](https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/consumer-resources/consumer-guide-pre-sale-real-estate-purchases) describes a presale as buying "the right to receive a completed unit at some point in the future." You usually pay an initial deposit when you sign, sometimes more deposits on a schedule, and the balance when the developer says the unit is ready to move in.

Three protections matter most:

- **The disclosure statement.** The developer has to give you this document, including a Summary of Pre-sale Risks and Buyer Rights form, before selling you a unit. It covers estimated construction dates, estimated strata fees, bylaws, parking and your cancellation rights.
- **The seven-day right to cancel.** You can cancel by written notice within seven days after the later of signing the contract or acknowledging you had a chance to read the disclosure statement, according to [BCFSA's REDMA FAQs](https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/real-estate-developer-resources/real-estate-development-marketing-act-faqs). If you cancel properly, the deposit goes back to you.
- **Deposits in trust.** BCFSA says developers must place deposits with a brokerage, lawyer or notary who holds them in a B.C. trust account.

One thing that surprises people: B.C.'s three-business-day Home Buyer Rescission Period, which applies to resale homes, does **not** apply to presales covered by section 21 of REDMA, per [BCFSA's rescission period guide](https://www.bcfsa.ca/public-resources/real-estate/consumer-resources/home-buyer-rescission-period-consumer-guide). It also doesn't apply when you buy an assignment.

## The risks BCFSA tells buyers to weigh

BCFSA's consumer guide is refreshingly blunt. Before you commit, it says to think about:

- **Delays, or a project that never finishes.** If the developer can't complete, it has to return your deposit but isn't required to pay you anything extra. A delay can also mean extending your lease or finding short-term housing.
- **Layout and finish changes.** Square footage can shrink, and contracts often let developers substitute materials of "a similar nature and quality."
- **Appraisal gaps.** If the unit appraises lower at completion, your lender may lend less, and you may need more cash to close.
- **Not being able to complete.** You risk losing your deposit, and the developer may take legal action for the difference if the unit resells for less.
- **Assignments aren't automatic.** The developer may refuse consent or charge an assignment fee, and you typically stay liable if the person you assign to doesn't complete.
- **Higher costs after you move in.** Strata fees, insurance and property taxes usually rise over time.

## What this means for you

If you're looking at a presale in Port Moody, Coquitlam or Port Coquitlam, here's how I'd approach it:

1. **Read the disclosure statement before the clock starts.** Ask for it early and go through construction dates, strata fee estimates, parking and the cancellation section with a lawyer or notary.
2. **Use the seven days.** Arrange your mortgage pre-approval and legal review so you can make a calm decision inside that window.
3. **Look up the developer.** BCFSA suggests checking past projects, whether they finished on time and what earlier buyers say.
4. **Plan for the gap.** If you're a newcomer still learning the area, completion could be a while away, so you'll need a place to live in the meantime.
