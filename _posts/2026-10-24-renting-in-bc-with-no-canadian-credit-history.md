Yes, you can rent in BC with no Canadian credit history. A landlord can judge you on your ID, references from past landlords and proof of income, and BC's privacy commissioner says a credit check should only be run near the end of the selection, on the few people the landlord is seriously considering.

I get this question from almost every newcomer I help, whether they are coming from another country or just never had a credit card here. Here is what the rules say, and how I would put an application together.

## Why you have no credit score here yet

The [Financial Consumer Agency of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-reports-score/credit-report-score-basics.html) says Canada has two main credit bureaus, Equifax and TransUnion, and that they "only collect information about your credit activity in Canada." So a strong record back home does not show up on a Canadian report. The agency also notes that your credit report can affect your ability to "rent a home," which is why landlords ask about it.

Being new does not mean being a risk, though. It just means the landlord needs other ways to get comfortable with you.

## What a BC landlord can ask for

In BC, private landlords have to follow the Personal Information Protection Act. The [Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner for BC](https://www.oipc.bc.ca/guidance-documents/2332) has a plain guide for landlords and tenants. In short:

- **ID.** A landlord can look at your government photo ID, but should hand it back and not copy or keep it.
- **References.** A landlord can ask about where you rented before and for how long, and can contact past landlords with your consent.
- **Income or employment.** If you cannot give adequate references, it may be reasonable for a landlord to ask for proof of income or employment to show you can pay rent.
- **Credit check.** A landlord can ask for consent as part of a standard application, but should only run it near the end of the selection.
- **SIN.** The guide says a full name and date of birth are often enough for a credit check, so landlords should avoid asking every applicant for a social insurance number or should make it optional.

The commissioner's earlier [2018 tenant screening report](https://www.oipc.bc.ca/documents/investigation-reports/2035) also points out that tenants are not really in a credit relationship with a landlord, because most rentals are paid one month in advance. The 2019 guide is the one that applies where the two differ.

BC's Human Rights Code also stops a landlord from refusing to rent to you because of things like your place of origin, race or lawful source of income, as the commissioner's guide lists.

## How to build a strong application without Canadian credit

This is what I put together with my clients who are new to Canada:

1. **A reference letter from your current or last landlord**, with their phone number and email. A reference from a professional landlord about on-time rent carries a lot of weight in the commissioner's own reasoning.
2. **A job offer or employer letter** showing your role, start date and pay. If you are bringing savings, a bank letter can help, and the 2018 report says you may black out other details on a statement.
3. **Your credit report from home, if you have one.** The federal agency notes that some lenders may look at your history from another country, so it does no harm to have it ready, translated if needed.
4. **Your free Canadian report once you have one.** The [federal agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-reports-score/order-credit-report.html) says both bureaus offer free reports online, and checking your own report will not affect your rating.

One more rule that matters here: under the [Province of British Columbia's deposit rules](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/starting-a-tenancy/deposits-fees), a security deposit can be no more than half of one month's rent, and landlords cannot charge a fee to review or process an application. Having no credit history does not change either limit.

## What this means for you

If you are a newcomer with no Canadian credit, lead with what you do have: ID, references and proof of income, all in one tidy package. Be ready to consent to a credit check late in the process, and know that your SIN is usually optional.
