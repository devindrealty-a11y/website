What should you check before renting a basement suite in the Tri-Cities, BC? Start with whether the suite is fully self-contained, because if you share a kitchen or bathroom with the owner, BC's Residential Tenancy Act doesn't cover you. After that, get the utility split in writing, look for basic safety features, and do a proper move-in inspection.

I'm Devin Desaulniers, a Real Estate Associate with Axford Real Estate in Port Moody. I've been licensed since 2020, I'm a lifelong Coquitlam local, and I've lived in Greater Vancouver for 28 years. Suites in houses are a big part of the rental market here. The City of Coquitlam's [secondary suites brochure](https://www.coquitlam.ca/DocumentCenter/View/4833/Secondary-Suite-Brochure-PDF) (June 2021) says about 70% of single-family homes in the city have one. So if you're a newcomer, there's a good chance you'll tour a few. Here's what I'd check.

## Is a basement suite in the Tri-Cities, BC covered by tenancy law?

Usually, yes. The Province's [types of rental housing page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/calculators-and-resources/tenancy-laws-rules/tenancies-types) explains that the Residential Tenancy Act doesn't apply when the tenant shares bathroom or kitchen facilities with the owner. In that case, disputes may have to go to the Civil Resolution Tribunal or the courts instead of the Residential Tenancy Branch.

That's why "self-contained" matters. A suite with its own kitchen and bathroom is generally covered, even if you share a laundry room. Ask directly: does the owner live upstairs, and is anything other than laundry shared?

What about suites that aren't approved by the city? The Tenant Resource & Advisory Centre (TRAC) [says](https://tenants.bc.ca/your-tenancy/am-i-covered-by-the-law/) unauthorized suites are still covered by the Residential Tenancy Act. TRAC also notes that if a city finds an illegal suite, it may shut it down, and the tenant can get a one month notice to end tenancy. Coquitlam's brochure explains that a suite is unauthorized unless it has building and plumbing permits and a final City inspection. It's fair to ask the landlord whether the suite is permitted, and to factor the answer into your decision.

## Utilities: get the split in writing

In a house with an upstairs and a downstairs, hydro and other utilities are often on one bill. The Province's [paying rent page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/rent-rtb/paying-rent) says the tenancy agreement should clearly state how utilities are paid and who pays them.

The Residential Tenancy Branch's [Policy Guideline 1](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/housing-and-tenancy/residential-tenancies/policy-guidelines/gl01.pdf) adds that a term requiring a tenant to put the electricity, gas or other utility bill in their name for space they don't occupy is likely to be found unconscionable. So before you sign, ask:

- Which utilities are included in the rent, and which aren't?
- If they're shared, what percentage is yours, and is that written into the agreement?
- Whose name will the bills be in?

## Safety and comfort: what to look for on a tour

The BC Building Code sets safety rules for secondary suites, and the City of Port Moody's [secondary suite guide](https://www.portmoody.ca/media/rdtbhokg/a_guide_to_secondary_suites.pdf) gives a plain-language summary of key items. It lists:

- A window in each bedroom that opens to the outside without special tools, as a second way out in a fire. Security bars aren't permitted.
- Smoke alarms in each bedroom, outside the bedrooms and on each floor, interconnected within the suite.
- An exit door that opens to the outside.
- Winter heating in every room, and, in general, no forced-air heating system shared with the main house (existing shared systems need extra measures such as fire dampers and smoke alarms).

You don't need to be an inspector. Just open the bedroom window, look for smoke alarms and ask how the suite is heated. Also check ceiling height, natural light, noise from upstairs, parking and storage, since those affect daily life. If you're comparing cities, [Port Coquitlam's secondary suites page](https://www.portcoquitlam.ca/business-development/property-development-building/building-permits/secondary-suites) and my [Coquitlam](/coquitlam.html), [Port Moody](/port-moody.html) and [Port Coquitlam](/port-coquitlam.html) area pages are a good start.

## What this means for you: a basement suite checklist

1. Confirm the suite has its own kitchen and bathroom, and ask what (if anything) is shared.
2. Ask whether the suite is permitted with the city.
3. Get the rent, what's included and any utility split in a written tenancy agreement.
4. Expect a security deposit of no more than half a month's rent, per the Province's [deposits and fees page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/starting-a-tenancy/deposits-and-fees).
5. Check bedroom windows, smoke alarms, heat and the exit door on your tour.
6. Do the [move-in condition inspection](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/starting-a-tenancy/move-in-condition-inspection) with the landlord and take photos. A landlord who skips it can lose the right to claim against your deposit for damage.
