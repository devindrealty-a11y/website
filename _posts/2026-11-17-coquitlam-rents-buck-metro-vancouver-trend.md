If you've been reading rental headlines this fall, you've mostly heard one thing: rents in Metro Vancouver are lower than a year ago. That's still true on average. But the latest monthly report from rental platform liv.rent, published November 13, shows Coquitlam heading the other way, and that matters if you're planning a move to the Tri-Cities, BC this winter.

## What the November rent report found

According to [liv.rent's November 2026 Metro Vancouver rent report](https://liv.rent/blog/rent-reports/november-2026-metro-vancouver-rent-report/), the average asking rent for a newly listed, unfurnished one-bedroom across Metro Vancouver was $2,095. That's up 0.58% from October but still 3.2% (about $69) below November 2025.

Seven of the nine cities liv.rent tracks were below last year, with declines ranging from 0.23% in North Vancouver to 8.64% in Richmond. Only two cities were higher than a year ago: West Vancouver (up 0.45%) and Coquitlam (up 0.50%).

Coquitlam also stood out month to month. liv.rent says it was the only city where furnished and unfurnished rents went up for one-, two- and three-bedroom units alike. A few of the numbers:

- Unfurnished one-bedroom: $2,057, up 2.66% from October
- Unfurnished two-bedroom: $2,503, up 6.18%
- Unfurnished three-bedroom: $3,264, up 6.58%
- Furnished one-bedroom: $2,056, up 6.67%

For comparison, [Vancouver Is Awesome's write-up of the same report](https://www.vancouverisawesome.com/local-news/vancouver-rent-report-housing-nov-2026-13023870) lists Burnaby's unfurnished one-bedroom average at $2,109 and the City of Vancouver's at $2,263.

## How I read it

I'd keep this in perspective. One month of asking rents isn't a new trend, and these are prices on new listings, not what people already in place are paying. Coquitlam is still a little cheaper than the Metro Vancouver average for a one-bedroom. What it does tell me is that the "rents are falling everywhere" story doesn't fit every city, and it doesn't fit every unit size.

From what I see helping renters around the Tri-Cities, BC, the bigger jump in two- and three-bedroom asking rents makes sense. Families and couples who want space near SkyTrain, schools and parks are looking at the same limited supply of larger units. If you need a family-sized rental in Coquitlam, I wouldn't count on the regional averages to give you much room to negotiate.

The report also notes that one-bedrooms made up 46.63% of active listings and two-bedrooms 42.74%, while three-bedrooms were only 10.63%. That's another reason larger homes can feel competitive.

## What this means for you

**If you're renting in Coquitlam now:** your current rent is protected by B.C.'s annual cap, which liv.rent notes is 2.3% for 2026 and 2.2% for 2027, effective January 1, 2027. Moving only makes sense if the new place is a real upgrade, because you'll be paying today's asking rents.

**If you're a newcomer moving to the Tri-Cities, BC:** budget from current listings in the exact city and unit size you want, not from regional headlines. Compare Coquitlam with nearby options too. Port Moody, Port Coquitlam and Burnaby can each look different depending on whether you need one bedroom or three.
