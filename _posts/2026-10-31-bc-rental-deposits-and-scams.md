In BC, a landlord can charge no more than half of one month's rent as a security deposit, plus up to another half month as a pet damage deposit if pets are allowed. Once you move out and give your forwarding address in writing, the landlord has 15 days to return the deposit or take the proper steps to keep part of it, and a real landlord will never ask you to send money for a place you can't confirm exists.

I covered the basics of signing a lease from out of province in [how to rent in Vancouver before you move](/blog/2026-10-10-how-to-rent-in-vancouver-before-you-move.html). This post goes one step further: how a newcomer keeps a deposit safe from the day they pay it to the day they get it back, and how to tell a real listing from a fake one.

## How much a BC landlord can ask for

The [Province of British Columbia's deposit rules](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/residential-tenancies/starting-a-tenancy/deposits-fees) are short and clear:

- **Security deposit:** up to half of the first month's rent. On $1,500 rent, that is $750 at most. The amount is based on the rent in your original agreement, even if the rent goes up later.
- **Pet damage deposit:** also up to half a month's rent, no matter how many pets. Together, the two deposits can equal one full month. There is no monthly pet fee on top, and no pet deposit for a certified guide or service dog.
- **No application fees.** A landlord can't charge you to accept, review or process an application.
- **Paid too much?** The province says you can tell the landlord in writing and hold back the extra from your next rent, or ask the Residential Tenancy Branch (RTB) for help.

Once you pay the deposit, the tenancy is considered established and the landlord can't rent the unit to someone else. Keep the e-transfer confirmation or a receipt.

## How to get your deposit back

This is where missing paperwork can cost you.

1. **Do the condition inspection.** The province says landlords and tenants should complete a Condition Inspection Report (Form RTB-27) together, and comparing the move-in and move-out reports shows who is responsible for damage. A tenant who doesn't take part in the move-out inspection can lose their right to the deposit. Take your own dated photos too.
2. **Give your forwarding address in writing.** You can do this up to one year after the tenancy ends, but sooner is better.
3. **Watch the 15-day clock.** After the landlord has your address, they have 15 days to return the deposit with any interest owed. They can only keep money with your written permission or an RTB order.
4. **If nothing happens,** you can apply to the RTB, and the landlord may be ordered to pay double the deposit.

One small detail: the province lists deposit interest at 0% for 2026, so don't expect much interest on a newer deposit.

## Rental scam red flags

The [RCMP in BC](https://rcmp.ca/en/bc/safety-tips/frauds-and-scams/rental-scams) describes a common scam where someone advertises a place, says it is "really high in demand," and asks for a deposit before you've seen it. Once the money arrives, the messages stop. The [Competition Bureau](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2018/08/rental-scam-no-room-for-error.html) adds that scammers often copy photos from old listings, homes for sale or short-term rental sites, and may claim to be abroad.

Be suspicious if:

- The rent is lower than similar places.
- You're asked for a deposit with no written tenancy agreement.
- You're asked to send money out of the country.
- The landlord won't meet you or show the unit, or their name isn't on the documents.

The RCMP suggests checking the address yourself, doing a reverse image search on the photos, asking the landlord for photo ID, and contacting the builder if it's a new building. If you're moving from far away, have someone you trust see the place in person.

If you do get scammed, the [Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/fraud-and-scams/tips-and-advice/how-report-fraud-and-scams-canada) says to keep every message and receipt, call your local police, and report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501.

## What this means for you

If you're a newcomer, treat the deposit like the start of a paper trail: written agreement, receipt, move-in inspection, photos, forwarding address. And never send a deposit for a unit nobody you trust has seen.
