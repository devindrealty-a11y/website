I get this question a lot from people thinking about a move: how much do I actually need to earn to rent a 2-bedroom?

I ran the standard 30% affordability test against [CMHC’s latest average 2-bedroom rents](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/housing-data/data-tables/rental-market/rental-market-report-data-tables) (October 2025). A full-time worker — 40 hours a week, 52 weeks a year — needs $29.81 an hour to afford the typical Canadian 2-bed.

I posted this on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:share:7531133197561233409/), [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/photo/?fbid=1766294408832072) and [X](https://x.com/LeasingAgentVan/status/2125368530090778965) on November 24. Same numbers here.

## Where the wage lands

In B.C., that jumps to $39.71 an hour. That’s more than double our [$18.25 minimum wage](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage). In Metro Vancouver, it’s $45.44. Victoria and Kelowna aren’t far behind, at $40.77 and $40.73.

For comparison: Montréal is $25.88, Edmonton $30.83, Calgary $36.81, Toronto $39.35. The lowest provincial figure is Newfoundland and Labrador, at $23.23.

Vancouver’s $45.44 is the Vancouver CMA. The B.C. figure, $39.71, is centres of 10,000 or more, so it mixes the expensive metros with smaller cities. The rents behind those wages: Vancouver $2,363, B.C. $2,065, Canada $1,550.

## How the hourly wage is calculated

This is the same method as the [Canadian Centre for Policy Alternatives’ rental wage](https://www.policyalternatives.ca/news-research/making-rent-the-ccpas-rental-wage-update-2024/). Take the average monthly 2-bedroom rent, multiply by 12, divide by 0.30, then divide by 2,080 hours (40 a week times 52 weeks). It simplifies to the monthly rent divided by 52, rounded to the nearest cent.

CCPA’s latest update, *Making Rent* (Lee and Macdonald, September 4, 2025), still uses October 2024 rents. I did not use their dollar figures. I used October 2025 rents from CMHC’s Table 1.0 and the same 30% test.

## What these averages leave out

One important caveat: these are purpose-built apartment averages, so they include long-term tenants. A newly listed unit is often higher. [CMHC’s December 11, 2025 news release](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/media-newsroom/news-releases/2025/canadas-vacancy-rate-rises-amid-historically-high-rental-construction) says the national purpose-built vacancy rate rose to 3.1%, from 2.2% in 2024, with historically high rental completions and slower population and economic growth. Even so, “the average rent paid by all tenants for 2-bedroom units rose 5.1%, driven in part by units being repriced higher at turnover, when a unit is newly leased.” B.C.’s two-bedroom average rose 3.6% over that same year. The 5.1% is the Canada figure.

- These rents are purpose-built apartments in buildings of three or more units. Not condos, houses, basement suites, or the asking rent on a new listing.
- The 30% rule is a pre-tax benchmark for one full-time earner. Many households have two incomes. Many others earn less than full-time.
- B.C.’s $18.25 minimum wage took effect June 1, 2026 (it was $17.85). I checked it November 24, 2026. The rents are October 2025, so the two dates do not match.
- Yukon, the Northwest Territories and Nunavut are n/a on the map. Yellowknife is not an N.W.T. average.

## What this means if you’re moving to or renting in Greater Vancouver

If you’re a newcomer lining up a 2-bedroom in Metro Vancouver, start from $45.44 an hour, not the Canada number and not the B.C. number. That is one full-time earner, before tax, with rent at 30% of income, on the average purpose-built 2-bed. A second income changes the math. A new lease is often higher, because long-term tenants pull the average down.

The Tri-Cities, BC sit inside the Vancouver CMA, so $45.44 is the regional benchmark I’d use while you compare Port Moody, Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam with a place in the city. It is not a quote for a specific unit.
