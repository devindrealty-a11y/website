Newcomers can find free settlement services in the Tri-Cities, BC at two main organizations in Coquitlam: S.U.C.C.E.S.S. and the Immigrant Services Society of BC (ISSofBC), both of which serve Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam and Port Moody. The key thing to know first is that which program you can use depends on your immigration status, so it's worth checking that before you book an appointment.

I've lived in Coquitlam my whole life, and when I help people relocate here, settlement questions come up almost as often as rental questions. Here's the plain-language version of how it works.

## Who qualifies for free settlement services in the Tri-Cities, BC?

There are two big programs, and they're designed to cover different people.

**Federal newcomer services (IRCC).** According to [Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada](https://ircc.canada.ca/english/newcomers/services/), you're eligible if you're a permanent resident (including a resettled refugee), a permanent residence applicant who's been approved in principle and has a letter from IRCC, a protected person with a positive decision from the Immigration and Refugee Board, or a temporary resident in certain programs.

There's a newer time limit to know about. The [Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/settle-canada/newcomer-services/eligibility.html) says that starting April 1, 2026, economic class permanent residents (for example, people who came through Express Entry or the Provincial Nominee Program) can use these services for up to 6 years from the date they became a PR, and that drops to 5 years starting April 1, 2027. Your "Became P.R. on" date is on your Confirmation of Permanent Residence. Canadian citizens aren't eligible for the federal services.

**The BC Newcomer Services Program (BC NSP).** The province fills the gap. [WelcomeBC](https://www.welcomebc.ca/start-your-life-in-b-c/find-newcomer-services/services-for-newcomers-on-a-temporary-work-or-study-permit-and-new-citizens%C2%A0(bc-nsp)) lists temporary workers with a valid work permit, international post-secondary students (if their school doesn't offer these services), provincial nominees waiting on a PR decision, naturalized Canadian citizens, displaced Ukrainians with valid CUAET visas, and refugee claimants as eligible. Services include an introduction to life in BC, English practice, connections to school and health care, job search help and counselling.

If you're approved for permanent residence and still outside Canada, the Government of Canada also offers [free pre-arrival services](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/application-approved/outside-canada.html), so you can get started before you land.

## Where the offices are

**S.U.C.C.E.S.S.** runs its Tri-Cities, BC service centre at #2058 - 1163 Pinetree Way in Coquitlam, and the phone number is 604-468-6000 ([S.U.C.C.E.S.S.](https://successbc.ca/contact-us/)). WelcomeBC lists S.U.C.C.E.S.S. as a BC NSP provider for Coquitlam.

**ISSofBC** has settlement and career services in Unit 258 and free LINC English classes in Unit 136 of the Lincoln Centre, 3030 & 3020 Lincoln Ave in Coquitlam. Its [Coquitlam location page](https://issbc.org/locations/tricities/) says the office supports newcomers living in Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam and Port Moody, is open Monday to Friday 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., and recommends booking an appointment rather than walking in. It also notes its North Road location closed permanently in April 2026, so don't rely on older listings. ISSofBC is clear that staff can't fill out immigration forms for you or advise on visa decisions; that's IRCC's job.

For everything else, the [community resources directory](https://www.tricitieslip.ca/resource-library/community-resources) from the local immigration partnership for the Tri-Cities, BC gathers programs from public libraries, settlement organizations and community agencies in the area. Many programs have their own eligibility rules, so read the criteria first.

## What this means for you: a quick checklist

1. **Confirm your status.** PR or approved in principle? Start with IRCC-funded services. Work permit, study permit, provincial nominee or new citizen? Look at the BC NSP.
2. **Economic class PR?** Find your "Became P.R. on" date and note when your access window ends.
3. **Approved but not here yet?** Sign up for pre-arrival services.
4. **Book an appointment** with S.U.C.C.E.S.S. or ISSofBC once you know where you'll be living.
5. **Bring your documents** (passport, PR confirmation or permit) to your first meeting.
6. **Browse the local immigration partnership directory** for library programs, youth and seniors' services.
