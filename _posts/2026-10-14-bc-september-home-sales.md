The British Columbia Real Estate Association published its September sales figures today. Across B.C., 5,254 homes sold on the Multiple Listing Service, down 8.9% from September 2025.

## What today's release says

Those 5,254 sales were 25.8% below the 10-year average for September. The total dollar value of the sales was $4.9 billion, down 11% from a year earlier.

The average MLS price in B.C. was $924,279. That is 2.4% lower than $946,767 in September 2025. On its [housing monitor](https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/economics/housing-monitor-dashboard/), BCREA explains the average as total sales dollars divided by the number of sales. It also says the mix of homes can move that number. In the association's example, if buyers shift toward apartments rather than single-family homes, the average price falls when everything else stays the same. I am not saying that mix is what happened in September. I am saying a provincial average is a blunt figure.

Brendon Ogmundson, BCREA's chief economist, said:

> "Home sales dipped in September after an encouraging summer as spiking oil prices prompted speculation of impending Bank of Canada rate hikes."

He added, "However, we are also seeing inventory levels decline on a monthly basis, which should help markets remain in balance." The release gives that comment and does not give a count of active listings, so I will not invent one.

[The Canadian Press](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/real-estate/2026/10/14/bc-home-sales-fall-9-in-september-amid-interest-rate-speculation/), reporting the same release, said September sales were lower than a year earlier in every region other than the South Peace River and the Kootenays.

For 2026 so far, BCREA counts 51,310 residential sales, down 5.9% from the same period in 2025. Sales dollar volume for the year to date is $48.11 billion, down 7%. The year-to-date average price is $937,644, down 1.1%.

## October 28 is the next rate announcement

Ogmundson tied the September dip to speculation that the Bank of Canada might raise rates after oil prices jumped. The decision itself is still ahead.

The [Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/core-functions/monetary-policy/key-interest-rate/) target for the overnight rate is 2.25%. The September 2, 2026 announcement left it unchanged. The next announcement is Wednesday, October 28, together with the Monetary Policy Report.

I will leave the guesswork there. A lender qualifies you on your income, your debts, and the rate on your file. If you are buying, ask what you qualify for before you write an offer, and keep a financing condition. When a lender offers a rate hold, you can keep shopping while you wait for October 28.
