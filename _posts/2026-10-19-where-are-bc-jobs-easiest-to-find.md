I mapped the latest Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey numbers for every B.C. metro area (September 2026, three-month average).

This started as a post on my social channels. Here's that version.

## Kamloops and Kelowna

The surprise winner: Kamloops. Unemployment there is 4.4%, down from 9.2% a year ago. At the other end, Kelowna sits at 9.3%, up from 7.1%.

## Closer to home

Closer to home, Metro Vancouver is at 6.5%, right in line with Canada overall (6.5%) and B.C. (6.4%). The Fraser Valley is quietly improving too: Chilliwack dropped to 5.2% and Abbotsford-Mission to 6.8%.

These are three-month averages, so they differ from the single-month 7.1% Vancouver-area figure in [Vancouver-area unemployment rose to 7.1% in September](/blog/2026-10-11-vancouver-area-unemployment-september.html).

## How to read the map

On the map, each city's colour shows its unemployment rate: green is under 5%, yellow is 5–6%, orange is 6–7% and red is 7% or higher. In the table, a green arrow means the rate fell over the past year and a red arrow means it rose.

## Why it matters for real estate

Jobs decide where people move, and where people move decides where homes and rentals get snapped up. When I help families [relocating to Greater Vancouver](/relocating-to-greater-vancouver.html), the job question usually comes before the neighbourhood question.

One honest caveat: smaller cities' numbers swing a lot month to month, so treat this as a snapshot, not a verdict.

My take? Metro Vancouver is still B.C.'s biggest job market by a mile, and the Tri-Cities, BC, sit right in the middle of it, with SkyTrain into Vancouver and the Fraser Valley next door.
