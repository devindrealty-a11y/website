The BC Real Estate Association (BCREA) released its fourth-quarter housing forecast this morning, and the short version for our area is "a soft finish to 2026, then a modest pickup in 2027." BCREA expects Greater Vancouver MLS® home sales to dip about 3% this year to roughly 23,000, then rise 8.7% to about 25,000 in 2027. Here's how I read it, and what I'd keep in mind if you're buying, selling or moving to the Tri-Cities, BC as a newcomer.

## What BCREA is forecasting

Province-wide, BCREA expects 67,025 MLS® sales in 2026, down 4.5%, followed by a 7.2% rise to 71,840 in 2027. It forecasts the average BC price to slip 1.2% this year to $941,200, and it says that drop "largely reflects disproportionate weakness in more expensive markets in the Lower Mainland."

For the Greater Vancouver REALTORS® area, which includes Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam and Port Moody, the [full BCREA forecast report](https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026-11-10-housing-forecast.pdf) breaks it down like this:

- **Sales:** 23,702 in 2025, about 23,000 forecast for 2026 (−3%) and about 25,000 for 2027 (+8.7%).
- **Average price:** forecast at $1,223,000 for 2026 (−1.4%) and $1,250,000 for 2027 (+2.2%).
- **By home type in 2026:** detached sales are forecast to be nearly flat (−0.8%), while apartment sales are forecast to fall 4.6% and townhouse sales 3.7%.

Keep in mind these are forecasts, not promises. BCREA itself calls its 2027 view cautious and says it "hinges on" stability in the economy and lower fixed mortgage rates.

## Why the market is still hesitant

BCREA's chief economist Brendon Ogmundson summed it up as "mounting headwinds" fuelling "hesitancy among prospective buyers." The report points to a few specific things: five-year fixed mortgage rates have climbed above 5%, the Bank of Canada raised its policy rate in October, US tariffs are expected to hit BC harder than Canada as a whole, and population growth is expected to stay subdued through 2027.

The report also says Metro Vancouver's economy has been held back all year by a weak job market with rising unemployment, and that Lower Mainland sales through the third quarter were 6.4% below the same period last year. On the supply side, BC active listings are near their highest level since 2015, and there's extra pressure from unsold new homes.

That lines up with what I see day to day in Coquitlam and Port Moody: plenty of choice, buyers taking their time, and sellers who price realistically getting the most attention.

## What this means for you

**If you're buying:** A slower market usually means more listings to compare and more room to negotiate on price and conditions like financing and inspection. The trade-off is rates. With fixed rates above 5%, I'd get a pre-approval with a rate hold before you shop seriously, so you know your real budget. **If you're selling:** BCREA expects prices to keep drifting lower this year before a small rise in 2027. Nobody can promise where prices go, so I'd focus on what you can control: pricing against current competition, preparing the home well and being ready for buyers who want conditions.
