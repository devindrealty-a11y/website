BC Property Transfer Tax and the First-Time Buyer Exemption: Tri-Cities, BC Examples

In B.C., property transfer tax is 1% of the fair market value up to $200,000, 2% from $200,000 to $2,000,000, and 3% above $2,000,000, with a further 2% on residential value over $3,000,000. Qualifying first-time buyers pay no tax on the first $500,000 when the home is worth $835,000 or less (a saving of up to $8,000), and get a smaller exemption between $835,000 and $860,000. Qualifying buyers of a newly built home can be fully exempt up to $1,100,000, with a partial exemption up to $1,150,000. On a $600,000 condo in Tri-Cities, BC, that's $2,000 in PTT for an eligible first-time buyer instead of $10,000.

The tax

What is BC property transfer tax?

Property transfer tax (PTT) is a one-time tax you pay when your purchase is registered at the Land Title Office. It's based on the property's fair market value on the day it's registered. It's not the same as the property tax your city bills you every year. Most of the time, your lawyer or notary files the PTT return and pays the tax for you as part of closing. Exemption

The first-time home buyers' exemption

Who may qualify

You may qualify if all of these are true (first time home buyers' program):

  • You're a Canadian citizen or permanent resident.
  • You've lived in B.C. for the 12 months right before the home is registered, or you've filed two B.C. income tax returns in the last six years.
  • You've never owned a principal residence anywhere in the world.
  • You've never received this exemption or a first-time buyer refund before.

Which homes qualify

  • It has to be your principal residence.
  • The fair market value is $835,000 or less for the full exemption.
  • The property is 0.5 hectares or smaller (larger lots get a reduced exemption).
  • Only residential improvements count.

How much you could save

If the home is worth $835,000 or less, there's no tax on the first $500,000, which works out to a saving of up to $8,000. Between $835,000 and $860,000 the exemption gets smaller. It's $8,000 × ($860,000 − fair market value) ÷ $25,000. At $860,000 and above there's no first-time buyer exemption. (exemption amounts table)

Buying with someone who doesn't qualify

Only the share of the home owned by eligible buyers counts. If you buy 50/50 with a partner who has owned a home before, you get 50% of the exemption.

After you close

You need to move in within 92 days of registration and keep living there until the first anniversary. If you don't, you may have to pay back part of the exemption. There are also penalties for false declarations.

Forgot to claim it?

You can ask for a refund with form FIN 265, starting at the first anniversary of registration and up to 18 months after it, as long as you met all the conditions.

New homes

The newly built home exemption

You don't have to be a first-time buyer for this one. A newly built home can be fully exempt if its fair market value is $1,100,000 or less, with a partial exemption from $1,100,000 to $1,150,000. You need to be a citizen or permanent resident. It has to be the first registration of the home with a completed improvement, it has to be your principal residence, and the lot has to be 0.5 hectares or smaller. The same 92-day move-in and one-year occupancy rules apply, and the exemption code on the return is 49. For how the partial exemption is worked out, see the Province's newly built home exemption page.

Budget

Budgeting PTT into your closing costs

PTT is due when your purchase is registered, so plan for it with your other closing costs, not your down payment. FAQ

FAQ

How much is property transfer tax on a $700,000 home in BC?

$12,000 before any exemption. An eligible first-time buyer would pay $4,000.

What is the first-time buyer exemption limit in BC in 2026?

The full exemption applies to homes worth $835,000 or less. A partial exemption applies above $835,000 and under $860,000. (The older $500,000 and $525,000 limits applied before April 1, 2024.)

Do I pay PTT on a new condo in Coquitlam?

Possibly not. If the condo is newly built, worth $1,100,000 or less, and you meet the other conditions, the newly built home exemption may cover the full tax.

Can I use both exemptions?

You claim one exemption on the PTT return. If you applied under the first-time buyers' program and think the newly built home exemption fits better, you can contact the Ministry of Finance about switching.

My spouse owned a home before. Do I still qualify?

Your share can still qualify. The exemption is reduced to the percentage owned by eligible buyers.

I just moved to B.C. Can I claim it?

Only if you've lived in B.C. for the 12 months before registration (or filed two B.C. tax returns in the last six years) and you're a citizen or permanent resident. If you become a permanent resident within a year of registering, you may be able to apply for a refund.

When is PTT paid, and who handles it?

It's paid when the home is registered at the Land Title Office, and your lawyer or notary usually handles it.

What if I move out within the first year?

You may have to repay part of the exemption.

I forgot to claim it. Can I get a refund?

Yes, if you met the conditions. Apply with form FIN 265 between the first anniversary of registration and 18 months after it.
