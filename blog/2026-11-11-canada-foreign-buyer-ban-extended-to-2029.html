Canada's foreign buyer ban is staying, with a twist.

I shared this on my social channels last week. Here's that version for the blog, with a little more room for what it means if you're buying, selling or renting in Greater Vancouver.

What Ottawa just proposed

In Budget 2026 (tabled Nov 5), the Carney government proposed extending the ban on foreign purchases of EXISTING residential properties to January 1, 2029, while exempting NEW residential properties.

What that means in plain English:

  • Resale homes: still largely off-limits to non-Canadians until 2029.
  • New builds: foreign capital would be allowed back in (details and timing still coming via legislation).

Ottawa's stated reason: keep the vast majority of the market available for Canadians, while unlocking more investment in new housing supply. In the budget's words, the change "would help protect housing affordability by keeping the vast majority of the market available for Canadians, while unlocking additional investment in new housing supply."

Why it matters: a full lift in 2027 is off the table

The ban, under the Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, has been in force since January 1, 2023. It was extended in February 2024 and was scheduled to expire on January 1, 2027.

A lot of people were betting the whole ban would just expire on January 1, 2027. That full lift just got ruled out. Keeping the ban on existing homes can keep downward pressure on resale prices, especially where speculation had priced in a clean end to the ban. New construction is the door they want to reopen.

This is still a Budget proposal. Legislation and regulations have to follow, and the detailed rules and start date for the new-home exemption haven't been published yet.

What this means for you

  • If you're buying a resale home: the rules you're competing under today stay in place for longer, if this passes as proposed.
  • If you're looking at new construction or a pre-sale: watch for the legislation. Once the exemption's details are out, the buyer pool for new builds could look different.
  • If you're a newcomer to Canada: whether the ban applies to you depends on your status and the exceptions in the Act, so get advice on your own situation before you shop.
