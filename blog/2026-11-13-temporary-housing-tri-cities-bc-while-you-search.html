Temporary housing in the Tri-Cities, BC: where to stay while you search

Need temporary housing in the Tri-Cities, BC while you look for a rental? In Port Moody, Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam, most legal short-term rentals are rooms in the host's own home, so plan your stay early. Here's what newcomers should know about the rules, registration numbers and keeping the stay short.

If you need temporary housing in the Tri-Cities, BC while you look for a rental, plan for a hotel or a room in someone's home rather than a whole vacation apartment. In Port Moody, Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam, the city rules mean most legal short-term rentals are rooms in a host's own house, so the more of your rental search you finish before you arrive, the shorter that stay can be.

I'm Devin Desaulniers, a Real Estate Associate with Axford Real Estate in Port Moody. I grew up in Coquitlam and I've lived in Greater Vancouver for 28 years. A lot of the newcomers I work with ask me where to stay for the first couple of weeks, so here's what I tell them.

How short-term rentals work in the Tri-Cities, BC

The Province of BC defines a short-term rental as accommodation offered to the public for money for less than 90 consecutive days. Hotels and motels don't count. Every host has to register with the provincial registry each year and show a provincial registration number on their online listings, plus a local business licence number where the city requires one.

Port Moody, Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Anmore and Belcarra are all on the Province's list of communities with the principal residence requirement. That limits short-term rentals to the host's own home plus one secondary suite or accessory dwelling unit (such as a laneway home) on the same property. Local bylaws can be stricter, and in the Tri-Cities, BC they are:

  • Port Moody: since February 4, 2025, short-term rental hosts can only operate out of a bed and breakfast or boarding business, with a City licence (City of Port Moody).
  • Coquitlam: a permanent resident has to live in the home and run the rental, with a maximum of two guests or one family at a time and no separate kitchen in the rental space (City of Coquitlam).
  • Port Coquitlam: up to two bedrooms without cooking facilities and up to four guests, and the operator has to live in the home as their principal residence (City of Port Coquitlam).

In plain terms: if you're picturing a whole furnished condo with its own kitchen for a few weeks, you'll find fewer legal options in the Tri-Cities, BC than you might expect. A private room in a host's home or a hotel is the more realistic plan, especially for a family.

What temporary housing in the Tri-Cities, BC does (and doesn't) give you

Two things surprise newcomers here.

First, BC's Residential Tenancy Act doesn't apply to living accommodation occupied as vacation or travel accommodation. A nightly or weekly stay is a booking, not a tenancy, so refunds and cancellations follow the host's and platform's policy, as the Province points out. Read those terms before you pay.

Second, the registration number is your quick check. The Province says only registered short-term rentals can be advertised and booked, and guests aren't fined for staying somewhere that turns out to be non-compliant. The responsibility sits with the host and platform. Still, a listing with no registration number is a reason to move on, and I'd be cautious with anyone who asks you to pay outside the platform.

What this means for you: a temporary housing checklist

  1. Shrink the gap first. Start your rental search 4 to 6 weeks before your move so you need days in temporary housing, not weeks.
  2. Book refundable if you can. Move-in dates can shift, so flexible cancellation terms are worth a lot.
  3. Check every listing for a provincial registration number and, where the city requires one, a business licence number.
  4. Stay near transit if you'll be viewing homes. A base near a SkyTrain station makes it easier to see places across Port Moody and Coquitlam. My post on living near Inlet Centre Station shows what's within walking distance of one.
  5. Know the deposit rules for your real rental. Once you sign a regular tenancy, a BC landlord can ask for a security deposit of no more than half a month's rent, plus up to half a month as a pet damage deposit if pets are allowed (Province of BC).
  6. Pick your area before you land. My Port Moody, Coquitlam and Port Coquitlam guides, plus my newcomer's guide to relocating to Port Moody, can help you narrow it down.
