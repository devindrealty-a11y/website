How do you find a family doctor in the Tri-Cities, BC?

To find a family doctor in the Tri-Cities, BC, register for the province's Health Connect Registry once you have your Personal Health Number. While you wait, urgent and primary care centres in Port Moody and Port Coquitlam, pharmacists and 8-1-1 can help.

The way to find a family doctor in the Tri-Cities, BC is to sign up for the province's Health Connect Registry as soon as you have your Personal Health Number. There's no need to phone clinic after clinic, and while you wait you can still get care at urgent and primary care centres in Port Moody and Port Coquitlam, at a pharmacy, or by calling 8-1-1.

Here's how it works, in plain language.

Step one: MSP and your Personal Health Number

Almost everything in BC's health system runs on your Personal Health Number (PHN), which appears on your BC Services Card or BC driver's licence. You get it by enrolling in the Medical Services Plan (MSP).

New and returning residents have a wait period before MSP coverage starts: the rest of the month you become a resident, plus two more months. The Province says to apply as soon as you arrive rather than waiting until the end of that period, and each family member has their own wait period. My first 30 days in Coquitlam checklist puts MSP next to your other first-month tasks, and if you're coming from the States, my US to BC guide covers health coverage during the gap.

How to find a family doctor in the Tri-Cities, BC with the Health Connect Registry

According to HealthLink BC, registering puts you on a list of people in your community who need a family doctor or nurse practitioner. That list is shared with attachment coordinators who match people with providers taking new patients. A few details worth knowing:

  • It's quick. Registration takes less than five minutes. You'll need your PHN, home address, email and phone number.
  • Register the whole household. You can sign up yourself, your family members or a person in your care.
  • Other languages are covered. Call 8-1-1 to register in a language other than English; translation is available in over 130 languages.
  • Old clinic waitlists have moved over. Clinic waitlists in BC have moved to the registry, and you keep your original waitlist date.
  • Keep it current. When a provider becomes available, the local team contacts you, so update your address and phone number if they change.

Where to get care while you wait

Being on the registry doesn't mean going without care:

  • Port Moody Urgent and Primary Care Centre, 3105 Murray Street, near Moody Centre Station. Fraser Health lists urgent care hours of 9 a.m. to 8 p.m., seven days a week, including statutory holidays.
  • Port Coquitlam Urgent and Primary Care Centre, 150-820 Village Drive, with the same 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. daily hours.

These centres are for non-life-threatening concerns that need attention within 12 to 24 hours, such as cuts that need stitches, infections or new pain. You register first with your BC Services Card, by phone or in person, and an urgent care nurse calls you back. Fraser Health says most people get that call within two hours, and showing up in person doesn't get you seen faster.

Pharmacists can assess and prescribe for 21 minor ailments, such as uncomplicated urinary tract infections, allergies, pink eye and dermatitis, and can renew or change some prescriptions. HealthLink BC says the minor-ailment assessment is free for BC residents when it's done in person at a pharmacy.

8-1-1 is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. A health service navigator can connect you with a registered nurse, dietitian or pharmacist.

Emergencies: call 9-1-1 or go to an emergency department for things like chest pain, head injuries or a broken bone. Eagle Ridge Hospital at 475 Guildford Way in Port Moody has a 24/7 emergency department.

What this means for you: a newcomer health checklist

  1. Apply for MSP as soon as you arrive, and plan ahead for coverage during the wait period.
  2. Once you have your PHN, register every household member on the Health Connect Registry.
  3. Save the numbers for the Port Moody and Port Coquitlam urgent and primary care centres in your phone.
  4. Find a pharmacy near your new place for prescriptions and minor ailments.
  5. Update your registry details whenever your address or phone number changes.
