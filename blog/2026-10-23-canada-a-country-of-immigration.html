Canada, a country of immigration: where we're born and where we go

Canada really is a country of immigration.

This started as a post on my social channels. Here's that version.

Where people in Canada were born

About 8.8 million people living in Canada were born somewhere else, according to the UN's latest migrant stock estimates (mid-2024).

The top three birthplaces:

  • India: about 1.0 million
  • Philippines: about 836,000
  • China: about 772,000

The rest of the top 10 on the graphic: the United Kingdom (about 428,000), Pakistan (267,000), the United States (257,000), Iran (205,000), Hong Kong (205,000), Italy (183,000) and Vietnam (180,000).

Now flip it around

About 1.3 million Canadian-born people live abroad, and 7 in 10 of them are in the U.S. The U.K. and Australia come next, a long way back (about 82,000 and 57,000).

So we bring the world in, and when we leave, most of us just go south.

What this means for you if you're moving here

Here in Greater Vancouver, I help a lot of people relocating here, whether they're brand new to Canada or Canadians coming home after years away. Everyone's "where are you from?" story is a little different, and that mix is a big part of what makes this place special.
