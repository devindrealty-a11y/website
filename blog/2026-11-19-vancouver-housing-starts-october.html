Vancouver's housing-start trend rose 2% in October

CMHC's October 2026 report shows the Vancouver housing-start trend up 2% from September, to 26,586, while B.C.'s trend fell 3%. Actual October starts jumped on multi-unit homes, but the year-to-date total is only 1% above 2025. What that means in the Tri-Cities, BC.

By Devin Desaulniers, Real Estate Associate, Axford Real EstatePublished November 19, 2026 · Greater Vancouver & Tri-Cities, BC

Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation released its October housing-starts numbers on November 17. A housing start is the moment construction on a new home begins, not the day it is finished or listed for sale. CMHC asks readers to watch the six-month trend, a smoothed annual rate, because one big apartment project can make a single month look enormous.

Nationally, that trend fell 1.7%, to 246,231. In the Vancouver census metropolitan area it rose 2% from September, to 26,586. That area is bigger than the City of Vancouver. Statistics Canada's 2021 census profile lists Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam and Port Moody among the communities inside it, along with Surrey, Burnaby, Richmond and the rest of the region. If you live in the Tri-Cities, BC, you are inside this figure.

A strong October, a flat year

CMHC counted 2,474 housing starts in the Vancouver area in October, up from 1,628 in October 2025. That is the 52% in its table. Single-detached starts fell from 244 to 170, down 30%. Every other home type, which the table calls "all others" and the release calls multi-unit, rose from 1,384 to 2,304, up 66%. That group includes apartments and townhomes. The report does not split rentals from homes for sale.

CMHC says the multi-unit segment can vary a lot from one month to the next, which is why the trend exists. From January through October, Vancouver-area starts totalled 22,263, only 1% above the same months of 2025. Over those ten months, single-detached starts were down 11% and multi-unit starts were up 2%.

The smoothed trend is the calmer version of the same story. Vancouver's total trend rose 2%. Inside that, the single-detached trend rose 5%, from 1,765 to 1,852, and the multi-unit trend rose 2%, from 24,192 to 24,734. CMHC says the Vancouver increase was due to higher multi-unit starts, which are nearly the whole total.

B.C. as a whole went the other way

British Columbia's six-month trend fell 3%, from 41,954 to 40,504. Kevin Hughes, CMHC's deputy chief economist, said October's results "signal reduced momentum for housing construction, with the national trend decreasing, driven by declines in Ontario, British Columbia, and the Atlantic provinces."

A busy October in the Vancouver area did not pull the province up. Victoria's trend fell 26% from September. Actual October starts in B.C. centres of 10,000 people or more were still up 36% from a year ago, 3,794 versus 2,784. I put more weight on the six-month trend than on one month.

The near-term pipeline in the Vancouver area got a little thinner. Homes with an approved building permit that had not started construction fell 5.1%, from 32,288 in September to 30,639 in October. Hughes said that decline in pending starts "suggests some softness in the near-term construction pipeline and should be monitored carefully." Homes already under construction rose 1.1%, to 62,335. Completions, the homes where construction finished, fell 15.1% from September, to 1,818.
