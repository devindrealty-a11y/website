Selling a townhouse in Coquitlam: what should you know?

Selling a townhouse in Coquitlam right now means pricing against a benchmark of $962,600, down 6.6% from a year ago, and having your strata paperwork ready before buyers ask. Here's what the latest numbers show and how I'd prepare.

By Devin Desaulniers, Real Estate Associate, Axford Real EstatePublished November 16, 2026 · Greater Vancouver & Tri-Cities, BC

Selling a townhouse in Coquitlam right now means pricing against a market that has come down: the Greater Vancouver REALTORS® benchmark price for a Coquitlam townhouse was $962,600 in October 2026, down 6.6% from a year earlier. The sellers who do well are the ones who price to today's numbers from day one and have their strata paperwork ready before a buyer asks for it.

That's the short answer. Here's what the latest data shows, and how I'd prepare if you're thinking about listing a townhouse anywhere in Coquitlam or the wider Tri-Cities, BC.

What the numbers say about Coquitlam townhouses

According to the GVR October 2026 statistics package, the Coquitlam townhouse benchmark was down 1.4% from September and 6.6% from October 2025. Nearby, the townhouse benchmark was $895,800 in Port Moody (down 10.1% year over year) and $854,900 in Port Coquitlam (down 3.3%). Across Greater Vancouver it was $1,006,500, down 5.5%.

There is some good news for townhouse sellers in the bigger picture. GVR's October report showed 468 townhouse sales across the region, down just 1.9% from a year earlier, while apartment sales fell 16.7%. Townhouses also had the strongest sales-to-active listings ratio of the three home types at 16.5%. GVR's historical data suggests prices tend to face downward pressure when that ratio stays below 12% and upward pressure above 20%, so townhouses are sitting in balanced territory.

In plain terms: buyers are still out there for townhouses, especially families who need more space than a condo but can't stretch to a detached home. They just have plenty to compare, and they know prices are lower than last year.

Why pricing right the first time matters

A local analysis by Zealty, reported by The Regional, found that 38% of the 1,516 Coquitlam homes sold in the first nine months of 2026 had been listed at least once before without selling. The median Coquitlam home took 50 days to sell, counted from the first listing attempt.

That tells me a lot of sellers started too high, sat, relisted and then sold anyway. Every extra week on the market gives buyers more reason to ask what's wrong with the home. My approach is to look at recent townhouse sales in your specific complex and neighbourhood, compare them with what's actively listed, and set a price buyers can justify. If you'd like a starting point, my free home evaluation is a good first step.

Get your strata paperwork ready early

Most Coquitlam townhouses are strata properties, so buyers and their lenders will want the strata documents. The big one is the Form B: Information Certificate. According to the Province of B.C., the strata corporation has 7 days to provide it after a request. It can charge a maximum of $35 plus up to 25 cents per page, and some charge a rush fee if you give less than 7 days' notice.

The Form B shows things like your monthly strata fees, any approved special levies, the contingency reserve fund balance and a summary of the strata's insurance. The current budget, rules and most recent depreciation report must be attached. I like to order it, along with recent strata minutes, before we list. If there's a special levy or a big repair coming, it's much better to know and explain it upfront than to have a deal collapse during subjects.

Know the buyer's three-day rescission period

Townhouses are covered by B.C.'s Home Buyer Rescission Period. The BC Financial Services Authority explains that a buyer can rescind an accepted offer up to three business days afterwards by paying the seller 0.25% of the purchase price, and this right can't be waived. So an accepted offer isn't fully firm on day one. I tell sellers not to make moving plans until the rescission period has passed and subjects are removed.

What this means for you

If you're selling: Price to the October numbers, not last year's, and have your Form B and strata documents ready. Good photos, a clean and neutral home and a realistic price will do more than waiting for the market to come back.

If you're selling and buying your next home: Lower prices cut both ways, so you may be buying in a softer market too. My buyer resources page walks through the steps and has a mortgage calculator.

If you're leaving the area or you're a newcomer who bought here a few years ago: Selling from out of town is very doable with the right paperwork in place. If you're moving within the region instead, my guide to relocating to Greater Vancouver covers neighbourhoods across the Tri-Cities, BC and beyond.
