Pet-friendly rentals in the Tri-Cities, BC: a newcomer's guide

Looking for pet-friendly rentals in the Tri-Cities, BC? In BC a landlord can say no to pets, so the key is confirming the lease and any strata bylaws allow your pet before you sign. Here's what newcomers should know about pet deposits, strata limits and dog licences in Coquitlam, Port Moody and Port Coquitlam.

Can you find pet-friendly rentals in the Tri-Cities, BC? Yes, but in BC a landlord is allowed to say no to pets, so the real job is confirming, in writing, that both the tenancy agreement and (in a condo or townhouse) the strata bylaws allow your pet before you sign. Once that's settled, the money side is capped by law: a pet damage deposit can be no more than half of one month's rent.

Newcomers moving with a dog or cat ask me about this a lot, so here's a plain-language guide for Coquitlam, Port Moody and Port Coquitlam.

What BC law says about pets in rentals

The Province's pets and tenancy page is clear that landlords don't have to allow pets. A tenancy agreement can include a no-pets clause, or it can limit the size, type or number of pets. If a landlord agrees to change that later, the change should be in writing.

If pets are allowed, here's how the deposit works, per the Province's deposits and fees page:

  • A pet damage deposit can be at most half of one month's rent, and a landlord can only ask for one, whatever the number of pets.
  • Landlords can't charge other pet fees, such as a monthly pet fee.
  • Guide and service dogs aren't considered pets. They can live in rental units, and no pet damage deposit can be charged for them.

I've covered security deposits and scams in more detail in BC rental deposits and scams, so I won't repeat that here.

Condos and townhouses: check the strata bylaws too

A lot of Tri-Cities, BC rentals are strata units, and that adds a second layer. The Province's landlords and strata corporations page says tenants must follow the strata's bylaws and rules, those bylaws can restrict or ban pets, and the landlord must give tenants a copy.

According to the Province's strata pet bylaws page, stratas can ban pets, limit the number or kind (for example, no dogs over 20 kilograms), or require pets to be registered with the strata council. If a building uses the government's Standard Bylaws, the default is one dog or one cat, plus a reasonable number of fish or small caged mammals and up to two caged birds. Pets must be leashed or secured on common property.

So even if the owner says "pets are fine," ask to see the current bylaws. An owner can't approve something the strata doesn't allow.

Dog licences and off-leash areas in the Tri-Cities, BC

Each city has its own dog licence rules, so plan for this in your first month:

  • Coquitlam: all dogs over six months old need a City licence every year, and licences expire December 31 (City of Coquitlam). If your dog is already licensed somewhere else, the bylaw gives you 30 days after moving in to apply, and with proof of that licence the City issues one for the rest of the year at no charge. Off-leash spots include Bramble, Glen, Leigh, Miller, Mundy and Ridge parks (City of Coquitlam).
  • Port Moody: dogs four months or older need a licence, renewed every year (City of Port Moody). Off-leash areas include Chines Park, a wide gravel trail in Bert Flinn Park, the north end of Westhill Park and the fenced dog run at Rocky Point Park (City of Port Moody).
  • Port Coquitlam: dogs six months or older need a licence that expires December 31 (City of Port Coquitlam), and the bylaw asks you to apply within 31 days of becoming a resident. The City lists six off-leash areas, including fenced dog parks on Maple Street, at Lions Park and on Shaughnessy Street (City of Port Coquitlam).

If you're still deciding which city suits you, my Coquitlam, Port Moody and Port Coquitlam area pages are a good start.

What this means for you: a checklist for pet-friendly rentals in the Tri-Cities, BC

  1. List your pets honestly in every inquiry: type, size, number and age.
  2. Ask whether the unit is in a strata, and request the current bylaws before you commit.
  3. Get the pet permission written into the tenancy agreement.
  4. Expect at most half a month's rent as a pet damage deposit, and no monthly pet fee.
  5. Do a move-in condition inspection so pet-related damage claims are fair later.
  6. Licence your dog with your new city soon after you move.
