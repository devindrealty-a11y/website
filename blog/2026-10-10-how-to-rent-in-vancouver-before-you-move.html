How do I rent in Vancouver before I move?

Yes. You can line up a Greater Vancouver rental before you arrive: prepare your papers, sign a written tenancy agreement, and pay no more than half a month's rent as a security deposit.

By Devin Desaulniers, Real Estate Associate, Axford Real EstatePublished October 10, 2026 · Greater Vancouver & Tri-Cities, BC

Yes, you can rent in Vancouver before you move. If you are still in another province or another country, line up a written tenancy agreement and pay only the deposits British Columbia allows.

Get the paperwork ready early

WelcomeBC, the Province of British Columbia's site for newcomers, says British Columbia has one of Canada's largest and most expensive housing markets, and that there are generally more people than homes. It says finding a place can be challenging, and that "a bit of preparation goes a long way."

I ask renters to reach out 4 to 6 weeks before the move, which is when I can search, book showings, and go through a lease with you. Before that, set your budget, who will live there, pets, and how you will get to work or school.

WelcomeBC says English is the most widely spoken language in B.C., and that you may be asked for official translations of documents that are not already in English. For an application, I ask clients for government identification, proof of income such as a job offer or employer letter, and past landlords or employers we can reach.

Sign in writing, then pay only what is allowed

The Province of British Columbia says a tenancy agreement is a contract. Landlords must prepare a written agreement for every tenancy, and both of you must sign and date it. It needs the standard terms in the Residential Tenancy Act. It should name both of you, give the rental address, say when the tenancy starts, and say whether it is month to month or a fixed term. The province says fixed terms are usually one year.

Read it before you sign. A term that ignores the Residential Tenancy Act cannot be enforced. The landlord must give you a copy within 21 days, so photograph the signed pages that day.

The province's deposit page says a landlord can charge up to half of one month's rent as a security deposit, also called a damage deposit. The amount follows the rent in the original agreement. In the province's example, rent of $1,500 means a security deposit of no more than $750. If pets are allowed, a pet damage deposit can also be no more than half a month's rent, whatever the number of pets, so the two deposits together can equal one full month. A landlord cannot add a monthly pet fee on top of that, and cannot charge a fee just to accept, review, or process an application.

Once the security deposit is paid, the province says the tenancy is established and the landlord cannot rent the unit to someone else. You have 30 days from signing to pay it. Keep the receipt.

Check the home before any money moves

WelcomeBC says newcomers are often targets of fraud and scams, before the move as well as after. The Royal Canadian Mounted Police describes a common version: a person advertises a home, says it is in high demand, and demands a deposit without a viewing, sometimes with an electronic transfer and no meeting. After the money arrives, the messages stop.

Be suspicious, the RCMP says, if the rent is lower than similar places, if there is no formal agreement, if you are asked to send money outside the country, or if the landlord will not meet you and is not named on the papers. Walk through the unit, or join a live video call while someone is standing in it, and check that the photos are not copied from another ad. Do not send money or credit card details until you know what you are paying for.

What this means for you

You can sign before you land. Set the budget and commute first, then use that 4-to-6-week window on real listings, with the agreement in writing and the security deposit at half a month's rent.
