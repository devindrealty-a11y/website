Moving to BC from overseas: first steps in the Tri-Cities, BC

Moving to BC from overseas? Your first steps in the Tri-Cities, BC: arrange private health insurance for the MSP wait, list your belongings for the border, get a SIN, open a bank account, switch your licence within 90 days, and know BC's half-month deposit limit.

What are the first steps when moving to BC from overseas? Arrange private health insurance before you fly, because BC's Medical Services Plan (MSP) has a wait period, and bring a two-part list of your belongings for the border officer. In your first weeks in the Tri-Cities, BC, apply for MSP, get your Social Insurance Number (SIN), open a bank account, and plan to switch to a BC driver's licence within 90 days.

I'm Devin Desaulniers, a Real Estate Associate with Axford Real Estate in Port Moody. I've been licensed since 2020, I'm a lifelong Coquitlam local, and I've lived in Greater Vancouver for 28 years. I've already written guides for people moving from Ontario and moving from the US. This one uses government sources, so confirm details for your own situation, because rules change.

Before you fly: health insurance and a goods list

Health coverage. The Province of BC says new residents wait the rest of the month they become a BC resident, plus two more months, before MSP starts. If you're coming from outside Canada, the Province says to arrange private health insurance before you arrive and keep it until MSP begins. Most private plans don't cover pre-existing conditions, including pregnancy, and you pay the full cost of care during the wait unless you're insured. Apply for MSP as soon as you arrive, and note that each family member's wait is based on their own arrival date.

Your belongings. The Canada Border Services Agency asks you to prepare two copies of a list of everything you're bringing, with value, make, model and serial number where it applies. Split it into goods arriving with you and goods to follow. Give the list to the officer at your first point of entry, even if nothing comes with you. The officer completes Form BSF186 and gives you a copy, which you'll need when your shipment arrives. Goods that aren't on your original list don't qualify for duty- and tax-free entry later.

Your first weeks after moving to BC from overseas

SIN. Service Canada describes the SIN as a 9-digit number you need to work in Canada or to access government programs and benefits. There's no fee, and you can apply online, by mail or in person.

Bank account. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada confirms you can open an account if you're not a Canadian citizen. You need original ID, for example one document with your name and address plus one with your name and date of birth, and a foreign passport is on the accepted list. You may need to visit a branch in person.

Driver's licence. ICBC says you can drive on your valid licence for up to 90 days after moving here. Licences from some places can be exchanged without testing (ICBC lists them on its "licence exchange jurisdictions" tab). Others need a knowledge test and a road test. You'll surrender your current licence when you apply and need proof of at least two years of non-learner driving to skip BC's Graduated Licensing Program. ICBC can credit up to 15 years of driving experience toward your insurance if you bring an acceptable driving record, so ask for one from your home licensing office before you leave.

Settlement help. Settlement agencies in the Tri-Cities, BC can help eligible newcomers with steps like these. My post on settlement services in the Tri-Cities, BC lists who to call. If you have children, my SD43 school registration guide explains why a lease usually comes before school registration.

Renting your first home in the Tri-Cities, BC

Many newcomers rent first, and you may be doing it with no Canadian credit history. I cover that in renting in BC with no Canadian credit history. Two rules from the Province of BC are worth knowing before you send money:

  • A landlord can take no more than half of one month's rent as a security deposit, plus up to half a month's rent as a pet damage deposit if pets are allowed.
  • Landlords can't charge a fee to accept, review or process a rental application.

If anyone asks for a bigger deposit or an application fee, slow down and check before paying. My tenant resources page and the relocation guide to Greater Vancouver have more on renting from a distance, and the Coquitlam area page helps compare neighbourhoods.

What this means for you: an overseas checklist

  • Buy private health insurance that starts the day you land.
  • Make two copies of your goods list, split into "with me" and "to follow."
  • Request a driving record or licence history letter from home.
  • Apply for MSP, your SIN and a bank account in your first weeks.
  • Book ICBC well before day 90.
  • Never pay more than half a month's rent as a security deposit, and never pay an application fee.
