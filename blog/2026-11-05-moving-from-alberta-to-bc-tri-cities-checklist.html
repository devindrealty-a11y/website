Moving from Alberta to BC: a Tri-Cities, BC newcomer checklist

Moving from Alberta to BC? Here's what changes when you land in the Tri-Cities, BC: your 90-day licence switch, the 30-day vehicle deadline, health coverage timing, BC's 7% PST, and rental rules that differ from Alberta's.

If you're moving from Alberta to BC and landing in Coquitlam, Port Moody or Port Coquitlam, the move itself is the easy part. The paperwork has a few deadlines that start the day you arrive, and some rules you're used to in Alberta work differently here, from sales tax to how much a landlord can ask for as a deposit.

I'm Devin Desaulniers, a Real Estate Associate with Axford Real Estate in Port Moody. I'm a lifelong Coquitlam local, I've lived in Greater Vancouver for 28 years, and I've been licensed since 2020. Here's the checklist I'd want in your hands before moving day. If you want the bigger picture of the region first, start with my guide to relocating to Greater Vancouver.

Moving from Alberta to BC: your driver's licence and vehicle

ICBC gives new residents 90 days to switch to a BC driver's licence, and you can keep driving on your valid Alberta licence in the meantime. Your vehicle is on a shorter clock: you have 30 days after arriving to register, license and insure it in BC (commercial vehicles have to be done immediately).

A few details worth knowing before you book:

  • Bring proof of driving experience. ICBC says it will credit up to 15 years of experience once it has a driver's abstract showing when you were first licensed, and that premiums for new BC residents are adjusted for the first three years of driving here. Proof of insurance is no longer required (ICBC).
  • You may skip the BC safety inspection. Most out-of-province vehicles need a passed inspection from a BC designated facility before registration. But under the New West Partnership, some privately owned or leased vehicles from Alberta can be exempt. ICBC lists conditions, including the model year (four years old or less, or five years or more with a passed Alberta out-of-province inspection from the last 90 days) and the same owner as in Alberta (ICBC). Ask your Autoplan broker before you book anything.

Health coverage: line up Alberta and MSP

This one lines up nicely if you act early. The Government of Alberta says that when you move to another province, AHCIP covers you for the month you leave and the next 2 months. BC's Medical Services Plan has a wait period of the rest of the month you arrive plus two months, and the province tells people moving from another province to keep their former plan's coverage during that wait.

So apply for MSP as soon as you've moved, and cancel your Alberta coverage with your forwarding address, as Alberta asks. One time-saver: most adults finish MSP enrolment and get their BC Services Card at an ICBC driver licensing office, so you can often handle it on the same visit as your licence.

Money and rentals: what's different from Alberta

  • Sales tax. Alberta has no provincial sales tax. BC charges 7% PST on top of the 5% GST (Canada Revenue Agency). The Province says 7% is the general rate, with some exceptions. Budget for it on furniture and the other things you buy after the move.
  • Deposits. In Alberta, a security deposit can be up to one month's rent. In BC, it's capped at half a month's rent, plus up to another half month as a pet damage deposit if pets are allowed. If a Tri-Cities, BC landlord asks for a full month as a security deposit alone, that's a red flag. My post on BC rental deposits and scams covers this in more detail.
  • Rent increases. BC sets a yearly limit for existing tenants: 2.3% in 2026 and 2.2% in 2027, with three full months' written notice and no more than one increase every 12 months (Province of BC). The limit applies once you're in the home. The starting rent on a new lease is whatever you and the landlord agree to.

What this means for you: a moving-week checklist

  1. Book an ICBC appointment for your licence (and BC Services Card) within your first few weeks.
  2. Check whether your Alberta vehicle qualifies for the inspection exemption, then register and insure it within 30 days.
  3. Apply for MSP right after you arrive, and tell AHCIP you've moved.
  4. Add 7% PST to your setup budget.
  5. Expect a deposit of no more than half a month's rent (plus a pet deposit if you have pets), and get everything in writing.
  6. Pick your city based on your commute. My posts on moving to Coquitlam from another province and commuting from Port Moody to Vancouver help with that, and my Coquitlam, Port Moody and Port Coquitlam pages give you a feel for each area.
