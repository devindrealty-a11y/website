Moving to Greater Vancouver? Your first-week checklist

Just arrived in Greater Vancouver or the Tri-Cities, BC? A plain day-by-day first-week checklist: MSP, ICBC, BC Hydro and FortisBC, Canada Post forwarding, a Compass Card, SD43 school registration and a doctor.

The boxes are in, the rain has started, and you can't remember which box the kettle is in. Don't worry. Everyone's first week in B.C. looks a bit like this. Here's a day-by-day plan to get the important stuff done early.

Day 1 to Day 3: health, licence and power

Day 1: Apply for MSP. All B.C. residents enrol in the Medical Services Plan. The Province of B.C. says coverage starts after a wait: the rest of the month you become a resident, plus two more months. Apply right away. If you're moving from another province, keep your old health plan during the wait.

Day 2: Visit ICBC. ICBC gives you 90 days to switch to a B.C. driver's licence, and 30 days to register, license and insure your vehicle (commercial vehicles right away). Book an appointment at a driver licensing office. On the same visit, many people have the photo taken for a BC Services Card, which helps finish MSP enrolment.

Day 3: Turn on the utilities. Open a BC Hydro account for electricity (new residential accounts carry a $13.50 + GST setup charge on the first bill, and some need a security deposit). If your home has natural gas, open a FortisBC account. While you're at it, book your internet installation. Coming from Alberta? See my Alberta to BC Tri-Cities, BC checklist.

Day 4 to Day 7: mail, transit, school and a walk

Day 4: Protect your stuff and your mail. Ask your landlord whether they require tenant insurance, and get a policy that fits. Set up Canada Post Mail Forwarding from your old Canadian address (the service needs a Canadian original address). Lettermail, Registered Mail and magazines are forwarded; parcels are not, so tell parcel senders your new address yourself. Buy the service a few days before you need it.

Day 5: Get a Compass Card. TransLink uses a reloadable Compass Card to tap on for SkyTrain, buses and SeaBus. Buy one online, at a station vending machine or at select retailers, load Stored Value or a pass, and tap in (and out on SkyTrain and SeaBus). In the Tri-Cities, BC, the Millennium Line (the Evergreen Extension) gets you downtown without driving. For Coquitlam detail, see my first 30 days in Coquitlam checklist.

Day 6: Sort out school and a doctor. Families in Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Port Moody, Anmore and Belcarra register with School District No. 43 (Coquitlam). Eligible Canadian citizens, permanent residents and refugees who are ordinarily resident in B.C. usually register at their catchment school; work-permit and study-permit situations go through International Education. Once you have your Personal Health Number, sign up for a family doctor or nurse practitioner through the Health Connect Registry, or call 8-1-1.

Day 7: Buy a real rain jacket. Then go explore. Try Rocky Point Park in Port Moody, Lafarge Lake at Coquitlam Town Centre Park, or the Traboulay PoCo Trail in Port Coquitlam. You live here now.
