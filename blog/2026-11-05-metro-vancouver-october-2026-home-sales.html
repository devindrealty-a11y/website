Metro Vancouver home sales fell 10% in October. What it means

Greater Vancouver REALTORS® reports 2,024 Metro Vancouver home sales in October 2026, down 10.2% from a year ago, as a rate hike and a snap election kept buyers and sellers on the sidelines. My take for buyers, sellers and newcomers in the Tri-Cities, BC.

By Devin Desaulniers, Real Estate Associate, Axford Real EstatePublished November 5, 2026 · Greater Vancouver & Tri-Cities, BC

Short version: October was a quiet month for Metro Vancouver real estate. According to the Greater Vancouver REALTORS® October 2026 market report, 2,024 homes sold across the region last month, down 10.2% from October 2025 and 22.5% below the 10-year average for October. Fewer people listed, too. Here's what stood out to me and what it could mean if you're buying, selling or moving here as a newcomer.

Why the market paused

GVR's chief economist Andrew Lis pointed to "a few suspenseful distractions" in October, mainly the Bank of Canada's rate hike and the snap provincial election, alongside municipal elections. His read was that people "took a step back in October to recalibrate." That matches what I've been hearing. When rates move up unexpectedly and there's a new government talking about changing housing rules, a lot of people decide to wait a month and see.

Sellers stepped back as well. New listings came in at 4,805, down 11.6% from a year earlier, though that's still 1% above the 10-year average for the month. Total homes for sale sat at 15,688, which is 4.3% lower than last October but 24% above the 10-year average. So there's still plenty to choose from.

Prices and the balance between buyers and sellers

The benchmark price for all home types in Metro Vancouver was $1,068,600, down 5.2% from a year ago and 0.7% from September. By type:

  • Detached: $1,775,200 benchmark, down 7% year over year. 643 sales, down 7.2%.
  • Townhouses: $1,006,500 benchmark, down 5.5%. 468 sales, down just 1.9%.
  • Apartments: $673,400 benchmark, down 5.9%. 892 sales, down 16.7%.

GVR also tracks the sales-to-active listings ratio, which is sales divided by homes for sale. Its historical data suggests prices tend to face downward pressure when that ratio stays below 12% and upward pressure when it stays above 20%. In October it was 13.4% overall: 11.6% for detached homes, 16.5% for townhouses and 13.5% for apartments. In plain terms, detached buyers have the most room to negotiate right now, while townhouses were the steadiest part of the market.

What this means for you

If you're buying: Prices are lower than a year ago, but higher borrowing costs have eaten into some of that. Lis noted that financial markets are pricing in "a slight chance" of another quarter-point hike in December, and that some buyers may act to use rate holds before they expire. If you have a pre-approval with a rate hold, check its expiry date now. My buyer resources page has a mortgage calculator and the steps I walk buyers through.

If you're selling: With inventory well above normal, pricing matters more than ever, especially for detached homes. Buyers can compare a lot of options, so a realistic list price and good presentation go a long way. Looking for a realtor in Coquitlam or Port Moody to talk through pricing? I'm happy to run the numbers with you.

If you're a newcomer or renting: A slower sales month doesn't change much for renters directly, but it's useful context if you plan to rent first and buy later. Many of my relocation clients do exactly that. My relocation rental service for newcomers helps you lock in a rental before you arrive, and my guide to relocating to Greater Vancouver covers neighbourhoods across the Tri-Cities, BC and beyond.

My take

October looks like a pause rather than a turning point. Lis described 2026 as "one step forward, one step back," and November and December will show which way the year ends. If you're thinking about a move in the Tri-Cities, BC, a quieter market can mean less competition and more time to make a careful decision.
