How to use this

The rain is the whole list.

I am Devin Desaulniers, a Real Estate Associate with Axford Real Estate in Port Moody. This guide is for people who own a home in Greater Vancouver, with the most detail for a detached house in the Tri-Cities, BC. I did not put a rainfall total in it. If a number is here, it is copied from the source named beside it.

Detached

You look after the roof, the gutters, the slope of the ground, the furnace or heat pump, and the water heater.

Townhouse

The strata often looks after the roof and the walls. You still look after filters, alarms, and what your bylaws leave with you.

Condo

Much of the outside is common property. Your list is shorter: alarms, a heater if it is yours, and the steps inside the unit.

The Province of British Columbia says repair duties come from the strata plan and the bylaws, not from a guess. Under the standard bylaws in the Strata Property Act, the strata maintains the exterior, the structure, and things attached outside, including balconies. Owners maintain their strata lot except where the bylaws give that job to the strata.

Fall and spring

Get the water off the roof.

BC Housing’s Maintenance Matters No. 2 says steep-slope roofs, the usual roof on a detached house or a townhouse, shed water into eavestroughs. Low-slope roofs, common on apartment buildings, use a membrane and drains, and they have to stay watertight.

What to have checked

  • Twice a year: spring, and fall before winter rain.
  • Again after high wind, heavy rain, or hail.
  • Gutters and downspouts full of leaves. Overflow wets the walls.
  • Missing flashing, damaged downpipes, and failed sealant.
  • Curled, cracked, or missing shingles, shakes, or tiles.
  • A brown stain on a ceiling. Act the day you see it.

Moss

  • An accumulation of moss holds moisture on the roof.
  • That blocks drainage and speeds up wear on shakes, shingles, and some membranes.
  • Have a roofing professional do the cleaning and the inspection.
  • Do not treat a wet, mossy roof as a weekend walk.

BC Housing says a roof’s life can range from 10 years to over 30, depending on design, exposure, construction, and materials. That is their range, not a replacement year for your house. In a strata, roof work is often the corporation’s job. Read the bylaws first.

Source: BC Housing, Maintenance Matters No. 2, Maintaining Your Roof, February 2018. bchousing.org

Where the water goes next

Away from the foundation.

CMHC’s moisture guide lists the same pattern again and again: ground that slopes toward the house, a blocked footing drain, and a sump that does not run.

Outside

  • Slope lawns, walks, and beds away from the walls.
  • Extend downspouts so the water does not dump at the foundation.
  • Keep soil and mulch off the siding.
  • If a perimeter drain is blocked, have it looked at. That is not a hose-down.

Sump and crawl space

  • If you can reach the float, confirm the pump runs.
  • Put a tight cover on an open sump pit.
  • An earth crawl-space floor: cover it with polyethylene, joints sealed, or with a concrete floor and polyethylene underneath.
  • Wood touching soil is a flag a home inspector is asked to report.

Attic and window condensation

Condensation needs three things at once: too much moisture in the air, too little air exchange, and a cold surface. CMHC says the usual response to a wet attic is to add vents, and that this can pull more moist house air up and make it worse. Stop the leaks from the house into the attic. Bathroom and kitchen fans should duct outside. Do not wrap the insulation in plastic. A black stain on attic wood can mean blocked ventilation or air leaking up from the rooms. That needs a closer look, not a coat of paint.

Sources: CMHC, Moisture and Air, and About Your House on attic moisture (the guide says computer models found attics in damp coastal climates can be drier with less ventilation). InterNACHI Standards of Practice, October 2022, on crawl spaces, wood near soil, and sump pumps with a float the inspector can reach. BC Housing Maintenance Matters No. 2, on black staining in the attic.

Inside the house

Filters, the tank, the alarms.

Gas furnace

  • Check the filter at least every three months. Clean or replace it if it is dirty.
  • Pets or smokers: check it more often.
  • Shut the power off at the furnace switch and the breaker before you open it.
  • FortisBC says Technical Safety BC generally recommends a yearly visit by a licensed gas contractor.
  • Keep solvents, propane, and gasoline-powered tools out of the furnace room.

Heat pump

  • Clean indoor filters several times a year. Replace them once a year, and follow the manual.
  • BC Hydro says cleaning a filter can improve airflow by as much as 30%.
  • Keep furniture off the indoor unit. Clear snow, leaves, and gear off the outdoor unit at the start of each season.
  • Book maintenance every one to two years.
  • Use heat in winter and cool in summer. Hold a steady temperature.

Hot water

  • Health Canada: set the tank to 60°C (140°F) to limit Legionella.
  • Use a mixing valve so water at the tap is 49°C (120°F), to cut scalds.
  • Strap the tank snug to the wall. FortisBC says the B.C. building and plumbing codes now require it.
  • A home inspector looks at the temperature-and-pressure relief valve and the seismic bracing.

Smoke and CO

  • Test smoke alarms every month, with the test button.
  • Replace a 9-volt battery at least once a year, or when the maker says. Replace the alarm at 10 years, or when the maker says.
  • Put one outside each sleeping area and on every level, including the basement.
  • The provincial fire package says working smoke alarms can cut the risk of death in a home fire by 50%.
  • FortisBC: a CO alarm on each level, especially near bedrooms. CO has no smell. The B.C. Fire Code says to maintain CO alarms the way the maker instructs.
If you smell rotten eggs, or someone has a headache and flu-like symptoms that might be carbon monoxide: turn the appliances off, get everyone out, including pets, and call 911 or FortisBC at 1-800-663-9911.

Sources: FortisBC, appliance maintenance and carbon monoxide pages. BC Hydro, heat pump use and the heat-pump maintenance checklist. Health Canada, water temperature and scalds. Province of British Columbia, smoke alarm toolkit, updated 2 September 2026, and the Get to Know Fire package. B.C. Fire Code 2018, Division B, 6.7.1.1.

The outside of the walls

Joints, decks, and damp wood.

Decks and balconies

BC Housing: keep drains clear of leaves, and do not cover a floor drain with a pot or a rug. A hot barbecue, gasoline, or a solvent can melt a vinyl membrane. Punctures from heels and chair legs let rain into the wood. Their usual figures: a liquid-applied membrane often about ten years, sheet vinyl often ten to fifteen, depending on sun and wear. A guardrail bolted through the membrane is a leak point. Ponding is a reason to call someone.

Siding and sealant

Sealant fills the joint so water stays out. BC Housing says it typically lasts somewhere between two and fifty years, depending on the product, the weather, and how much the joint moves. Review cladding and sealant once a year. Most water gets in at windows, doors, vents, and pipes, not through the middle of a board. Leave weep holes open. Replacing the bead is a contractor’s job when the joint is wide or the old product has to come off clean.

Carpenter ants

  • They do not eat wood. They tunnel in it.
  • Nests like moist, decaying wood, and also drier walls, eaves, attics, and crawl spaces.
  • Repair wet wood, clear gutters, store firewood up and away from the house, prune branches that touch the walls, and keep siding off the soil.
  • The province says more than 10 to 12 ants indoors in an evening is a reason to find out if the nest is in the house.

Outdoor water

  • Before a freeze, take hoses and splitters off the outdoor taps.
  • If there is an indoor shut-off for that tap, close it, open the outside tap to drain, then close the outside tap again.
  • In-ground sprinklers: have a contractor clear the lines with air before a freezing night.
  • I did not find a Metro Vancouver or Tri-Cities, BC page that sets a blowout date or a price, so neither is printed here.

Sources: BC Housing Maintenance Matters No. 5 (sealants), No. 6 (decks and balconies), and No. 8 (cladding). Province of British Columbia, Managing Carpenter Ant Pests, updated 5 August 2020.

A year on one page

Check it, then date it.

Write the month in the margin when you do it. Strata homes: skip any line the bylaws give to the corporation, and still do the lines inside your own place.

Fall
  • Gutters and downspouts clear, and the water leaves the house.
  • Roofer looks at shingles, flashing, and moss.
  • Hoses off. Outdoor taps drained. Sprinkler lines cleared with air.
  • Furnace or heat pump serviced if it was not done this year. Filter checked.
Winter
  • Smoke alarms tested once a month. CO alarms the same.
  • After a snowfall, the outdoor heat pump is clear.
  • Watch the windows for condensation that does not clear.
  • No stored paint, propane, or fuel next to the furnace or the water heater.
Spring
  • Roof and gutters again, after the wet months.
  • Ground still slopes away. Downspout extensions still in place.
  • Sump runs, if you can reach it. Crawl-space ground cover still sealed.
  • Deck drains clear. Sealant at windows and vents still stuck, weep holes open.
Any month
  • Tank at 60°C, tap at 49°C through a mixing valve, tank strapped.
  • Bathroom fans actually blow outside.
  • Firewood off the soil and away from the wall. Branches off the siding.
  • You can find the water shut-off, the electrical panel, and the gas meter in the dark.

What this page is not

It is not a repair quote, and it is not a list of work a buyer will require. A buyer in B.C. can still write an offer that is subject to a home inspection. The next page is what that visit usually covers.

A different job from this guide

A look, on that day.

In B.C., home inspectors are licensed through Consumer Protection BC. The Home Inspectors Association BC, which used to be CAHPI (BC), describes the visit as a limited visual examination of what can be seen that day. It is not a code inspection, and it does not promise how the home will perform later. InterNACHI’s Standards of Practice, revised October 2022, are one published list of what a visual inspection includes. Your contract can add or leave out items. This is that list, in plain language, not a script for the visit.

Roof and walls

  • Roof covering, gutters, downspouts, flashing, vents, and the chimney, from the ground or the eaves. Walking the roof is not required.
  • Wall coverings, a sample of windows, doors, decks, balconies, railings, and the grading where water could reach the structure.

Below the floor

  • Foundation, basement, and crawl space, if they can be entered safely.
  • Wood in contact with or near soil, and signs of water getting in now.
  • A sump, and they run it when the float is reachable.

Heat, water, power

  • Heating and cooling equipment that is visible.
  • Main water shut-off and main fuel shut-off.
  • Water heater, including venting, the relief valve, and seismic bracing.
  • The electrical panel, a sample of outlets, and GFCI buttons where they can test them.

Alarms and the attic

  • Whether smoke and carbon monoxide detectors are present. The standard does not require the inspector to test them.
  • Insulation and ventilation in an unfinished attic or crawl space, without moving the insulation.
  • Kitchen, bathroom, and laundry fans.

A maintenance checklist is not this visit. An inspector reports what is wrong and visible today. They are not required to predict how long a roof will last, to see through walls, or to inspect underground drains and sprinkler lines. In a strata, the unit inspection is separate from reading the minutes, the depreciation report, and the bylaws.

Sources: Consumer Protection BC, home inspections. Home Inspectors Association BC, scope of inspection (the association was CAHPI (BC) until 2016). InterNACHI, Home Inspection Standards of Practice, last revised October 2022. CAHPI National Standards of Practice, 7 December 2024, which also say a visual inspection is not a check against the building code.

Before the ground moves

Know the shut-offs. Then stop.

  • Drop, Cover, and Hold On. If you are inside, stay inside.
  • Strap tall bookcases to the studs. Move heavy frames off the wall above the bed.
  • Latch cupboard doors. Strap the fridge, the freezer, and the laundry machines.
  • Strap the water heater. A licensed gas fitter installs a flexible gas line if you need one.
  • Store food and water for at least two weeks. PreparedBC’s guide says you may be on your own for several days, and services may take weeks.
  • Find the electrical panel, the water shut-off, and the gas meter before you need them. Keep the path clear.
  • Do not shut the gas off only because you are leaving. FortisBC says they will shut it off if emergency officials ask.
  • If you smell rotten eggs or hear hissing: go outside, then call 1-800-663-9911 or 911.
  • If the meter is off, do not turn it back on. Only a licensed gas contractor does that.
  • Damaged appliances stay off until a licensed gas contractor has looked at them.
