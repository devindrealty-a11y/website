The climate does the damage

Rain runs the list.

Detached

Roof, gutters, the slope of the ground, the furnace or heat pump, and the water heater.

Townhouse

The strata often has the roof and the walls. You still have filters, alarms, and what the bylaws leave you.

Condo

Much of the outside is common property. Your list is alarms, a heater if it is yours, and the steps inside.

The province says the strata plan and the bylaws decide who repairs what. Under the standard bylaws, the strata maintains the exterior, the structure, and things attached outside, including balconies. Owners maintain their strata lot except where the bylaws give that job to the strata.

BC Housing · roofs

Get the water off.

Look twice a year

  • Spring, and fall before winter rain.
  • Again after high wind, heavy rain, or hail.
  • Steep roofs shed into gutters. Low-slope roofs, common on apartments, use a membrane and drains and must stay watertight.
  • A roofer does the inspection. Do not walk a wet, mossy roof.

What moss and debris do

  • Moss holds moisture, blocks drainage, and speeds wear on shakes, shingles, and some membranes.
  • Leaves in the gutters send overflow against the walls.
  • Fix missing flashing, damaged downpipes, and failed sealant.
  • Curled, cracked, or missing shingles, shakes, or tiles.
  • A brown ceiling stain: act that day.

BC Housing, Maintenance Matters No. 2 (February 2018): a roof’s life can range from 10 years to over 30, depending on design, exposure, construction, and materials. That is their range, not a date for your house. In a strata, roof work is often the corporation’s. Read the bylaws.

CMHC · under the house

Send water away.

Outside

  • Slope lawns, walks, and beds away from the walls.
  • Extend downspouts so water does not dump at the foundation.
  • Keep soil and mulch off the siding.
  • A blocked perimeter drain needs a look. It is not a hose-down.

Sump and crawl space

  • If you can reach the float, confirm the pump runs.
  • Put a tight cover on an open sump pit.
  • Earth crawl floor: sealed polyethylene, or concrete with polyethylene underneath.
  • Wood touching soil is a flag an inspector reports.

Condensation needs three things at once: too much moisture, too little air exchange, and a cold surface. CMHC says adding vents to a wet attic can pull more moist house air up. Stop the leaks from the rooms. Bathroom and kitchen fans duct outside. Do not wrap the insulation in plastic. A black stain on attic wood can mean blocked vents or air leaking up. Their models found attics in damp coastal climates can be drier with less ventilation. InterNACHI Standards of Practice, October 2022.

FortisBC · BC Hydro · Health Canada

Filters, the tank, alarms.

Heat

  • Gas furnace filter: at least every three months. More often with pets or smokers. Power off at the switch and the breaker first.
  • Technical Safety BC, as FortisBC reports it, generally wants a yearly visit by a licensed gas contractor. Keep solvents, propane, and gasoline tools out of that room.
  • Heat pump: clean indoor filters several times a year and replace them once a year. Follow the manual. BC Hydro says cleaning a filter can improve airflow by as much as 30%.
  • Furniture off the indoor unit. Snow, leaves, and gear off the outdoor unit at the start of each season. Service every one to two years. Heat in winter, cool in summer, at a steady temperature.

Water and alarms

  • Health Canada: tank at 60°C (140°F) to limit Legionella. Mixing valve so the tap is 49°C (120°F).
  • Strap the tank. FortisBC says the B.C. codes now require it. An inspector looks at the relief valve and the seismic bracing.
  • Test smoke alarms every month. Replace a 9-volt battery at least yearly, or when the maker says. Replace the alarm at 10 years, or when the maker says.
  • One outside each sleeping area and on every level, including the basement. The provincial fire package says working alarms can cut the risk of death in a home fire by 50%.
  • CO alarm on each level, especially near bedrooms. CO has no smell. Maintain it the way the maker says (B.C. Fire Code).
Rotten-egg smell, or a headache and flu-like symptoms that might be carbon monoxide: appliances off, everyone out, including pets. Call 911 or FortisBC at 1-800-663-9911.

Walls, decks, pests, taps

Joints and damp wood.

Decks and sealant

  • Keep drains clear. Do not cover a floor drain with a pot or a rug.
  • A hot barbecue, gasoline, or a solvent can melt vinyl. Heels and chair legs puncture it.
  • BC Housing’s usual figures: liquid-applied membrane often about ten years; sheet vinyl often ten to fifteen, depending on sun and wear.
  • A guardrail bolted through the membrane leaks. Ponding is a reason to call.
  • Sealant typically lasts somewhere between two and fifty years, depending on the product, the weather, and how much the joint moves. Review cladding and sealant once a year.
  • Most water gets in at windows, doors, vents, and pipes. Leave weep holes open. Wide joints are a contractor’s job.

Ants and outdoor water

  • Carpenter ants do not eat wood. They tunnel in it. Nests like moist, decaying wood, and also drier walls, eaves, attics, and crawl spaces.
  • Repair wet wood, clear gutters, store firewood up and away, prune branches off the walls, keep siding off the soil.
  • More than 10 to 12 ants indoors in an evening: find out if the nest is in the house.
  • Before a freeze, take hoses and splitters off the taps. If there is an indoor shut-off, close it, drain the outside tap, then close that tap.
  • In-ground sprinklers: a contractor clears the lines with air before a freezing night. No Metro Vancouver or Tri-Cities, BC page found that sets a date or a price, so neither is printed.

BC Housing Maintenance Matters No. 5 (sealants), No. 6 (decks), No. 8 (cladding). Province of British Columbia, carpenter ants, updated 5 August 2020.

One page for the year

Check it. Date it.

Fall

  • Gutters clear. Water leaves the house.
  • Roofer looks at shingles, flashing, and moss.
  • Hoses off. Taps drained. Sprinkler lines cleared with air.
  • Furnace or heat pump serviced if not done this year. Filter checked.

Winter

  • Smoke alarms tested once a month. CO alarms the same.
  • After snow, the outdoor heat pump is clear.
  • Windows: condensation that does not clear.
  • No paint, propane, or fuel next to the furnace or the tank.

Spring

  • Roof and gutters again after the wet months.
  • Ground still slopes away. Downspout extensions still on.
  • Sump runs if you can reach it. Crawl-space cover still sealed.
  • Deck drains clear. Sealant stuck. Weep holes open.

Any month

  • Tank 60°C. Tap 49°C through a mixing valve. Tank strapped.
  • Bathroom fans blow outside.
  • Firewood off the soil and away from the wall. Branches off the siding.
  • You can find the water shut-off, the panel, and the gas meter in the dark.

Strata homes: skip a line the bylaws give the corporation, and still do the lines inside your place. This is not a repair quote, and not a list a buyer must require. A buyer in B.C. can still write an offer subject to a home inspection.

A different job from this guide

A look, that day.

In B.C., home inspectors are licensed through Consumer Protection BC. The Home Inspectors Association BC was CAHPI (BC) until 2016. The visit is a limited look at what can be seen that day. It is not a code inspection, and it does not promise later performance. InterNACHI’s Standards of Practice (October 2022) are one published list. Your contract can add or leave out items.

Outside and below

  • Roof covering, gutters, downspouts, flashing, vents, chimney, from the ground or the eaves. Walking the roof is not required.
  • Walls, a sample of windows and doors, decks, balconies, railings, and grading where water could reach the structure.
  • Foundation, basement, and crawl space if they can be entered safely. Wood near soil. Water getting in now. A sump, run when the float is reachable.

Inside the systems

  • Heating and cooling that is visible. Main water shut-off and main fuel shut-off.
  • Water heater: venting, relief valve, seismic bracing. Panel, a sample of outlets, GFCI buttons where they can test them.
  • Whether smoke and CO detectors are present. The standard does not require a test of the alarms.
  • Insulation and ventilation in an unfinished attic or crawl space, without moving the insulation. Kitchen, bathroom, and laundry fans.

Not required: predict how long a roof lasts, see through walls, or inspect underground drains and sprinkler lines. In a strata, the unit visit is separate from the minutes, the depreciation report, and the bylaws. CAHPI National Standards of Practice, 7 December 2024, also say a visual inspection is not a check against the building code.

PreparedBC · FortisBC

Know the shut-offs.

Before

  • Drop, Cover, and Hold On. If you are inside, stay inside.
  • Strap bookcases to the studs. Heavy frames off the wall above the bed. Latch cupboards. Strap the fridge, freezer, and laundry machines.
  • Strap the water heater. A licensed gas fitter adds a flexible gas line if you need one.
  • Food and water for at least two weeks. PreparedBC: you may be on your own for several days, and services may take weeks.

After

  • Find the panel, the water shut-off, and the gas meter beforehand. Keep the path clear.
  • Do not shut the gas off only because you are leaving. FortisBC shuts it off if emergency officials ask.
  • Rotten eggs or a hiss: go outside, then call 1-800-663-9911 or 911.
  • If the meter is off, do not turn it back on. Only a licensed gas contractor does that. Damaged appliances stay off until one has looked.
