Federal GST · in force

Up to $50,000 back on a new home

$50,000$1 million or less: up to 100% of the GST, capped here.
$25,000CRA’s example at $1.25 million. Half the maximum.
$0$1.5 million or more. No rebate.
  • Law since March 12, 2026 (Bill C-4, Royal Assent). CRA has taken claims since March 17, 2026. Not a proposal.
  • British Columbia charges 5% GST on a new home. No provincial HST on that purchase.
  • New or substantially renovated, and your primary residence. A resale home does not qualify.
  • Between $1 million and $1.5 million the maximum shrinks: the lesser of $50,000 and the GST paid, times ($1,500,000 minus the price) divided by $500,000.
  • The builder agreement must be dated on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. The agreement date counts, not the day conditions are waived. An assignment of a pre-March 20, 2025 contract does not reset that date.

Who can claim the GST rebate

The tests, in short

  • 18 or older. Canadian citizen or permanent resident.
  • You are the first to live in the new or renovated home, and it will be your primary residence.
  • You, and your spouse or common-law partner, did not live in a home either of you owned in that year or the four calendar years before it. A home sold in 2020 can still leave you eligible in 2026. That is the CRA’s own example.
  • If several people are on title, only one claims it, and that person must be a first-time buyer under this test. A parent on title who already owns a home does not, by itself, block the claim.

The older new-housing rebate

36%Of the GST, maximum $6,300, when the home is $350,000 or less.
ShrinksBetween $350,000 and $450,000.
$0At $450,000 or more. Most new homes in the Tri-Cities, BC are over this.

At $450,000 or less the two rebates together can return all of the GST. You do not get the $6,300 as well as a full GST refund.

Two different exemptions

First-time buyers, and new homes

First Time Home Buyers' Program

  • Resale or new. Citizen or permanent resident.
  • Lived in B.C. for a year before registration, or filed 2 B.C. returns in the last 6 years.
  • Never owned a principal residence anywhere. This home is your principal residence, on 0.5 hectares or less.
  • $500,000 or less: the full tax can be exempt.
  • Over $500,000 up to $835,000: the exemption is $8,000. You still pay the rest.
  • Over $835,000 and under $860,000: that $8,000 shrinks. At $860,000 it is gone.
  • Move in within 92 days and stay through the first year.

Newly built home exemption

  • You do not have to be a first-time buyer.
  • New, not yet lived in, and the first registration after it was completed.
  • Citizen or permanent resident. Principal residence. 0.5 hectares or less.
  • $1,100,000 or less: the full tax is exempt. At $1,100,000 that tax is $20,000, so you pay $0.
  • Between $1,100,000 and $1,150,000 it shrinks: tax times ($1,150,000 minus the price) divided by $50,000.
  • $1,150,000: no exemption. The tax is $21,000.
  • Same 92-day move-in rule.

When both could apply, the new-home exemption removes more. A larger lot, a partial share, or a foreign buyer can change the bill.

Saving before you buy

FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan

$8,000FHSA room in the year you open it. Contributions and RRSP transfers share that room. Lifetime cap $40,000.
$60,000Most you can withdraw from your RRSPs under the Home Buyers' Plan.

First Home Savings Account

  • Contributions can be deducted. A qualifying withdrawal is tax-free.
  • Unused room can carry forward. The exact later-year room is in your CRA account.
  • More than one FHSA is allowed. The yearly room is shared across all of them.

Home Buyers' Plan

  • Repay over 15 years.
  • A first withdrawal from January 1, 2026 through December 31, 2028 starts repayment in the fifth year.
  • A 2026 withdrawal starts repayment in 2031.
  • You can use both an FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan for the same home.

The path

Steps to buy in B.C.

  1. Know the cash. Talk with a lender, then read the tax pages in this guide before you fall for a price.
  2. Sign with your agent. In B.C. that includes a disclosure of representation, and a written agreement about how the agent is paid.
  3. Tour homes in the Tri-Cities, BC or elsewhere in Greater Vancouver. On a strata home, read the Form B, minutes, bylaws, and the depreciation report.
  4. Write an offer with subjects such as financing, inspection, and a document review. The deposit is negotiated and held in trust.
  5. Remove subjects in writing only when you are satisfied. After that, walking away can cost the deposit and more.
  6. Your lawyer or notary searches title and calculates the transfer tax. Completion is when money and title move. Possession is when you get the keys. Those dates are negotiated.

B.C. has a home-buyer rescission period on many residential purchases. Confirm the window, the cost, and the exceptions with your lawyer and the BCFSA guide. Subjects are still the usual protection.
