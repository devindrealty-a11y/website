Page 2 · Federal GST

The GST rebate for a new first home is law

Bill C-4 received Royal Assent on March 12, 2026. On March 17, 2026 the Canada Revenue Agency said it is accepting the First-Time Home Buyers' GST/HST rebate. This is not a proposal.

In British Columbia a new home is charged 5% GST. The rebate returns that GST, or the federal part of the HST in provinces that charge HST. A resale home is not charged GST in the usual sale, so this rebate does not apply to a resale.

Up to $50,000Price of $1 million or less: up to 100% of the GST, capped at $50,000.
$25,000The CRA's example at $1.25 million: half of the $50,000 maximum.
$0At $1.5 million or more, this rebate is not available.

Between $1 million and $1.5 million the maximum shrinks. The memorandum's formula for a purchase from a builder is: the lesser of $50,000 and the GST paid, multiplied by ($1,500,000 minus the price) divided by $500,000.

Page 3 · Who qualifies

The tests for the GST rebate

  • You are 18 or older, and a Canadian citizen or permanent resident.
  • The home is newly built or substantially renovated, it will be your primary residence, and you are the first person to live in it after that work.
  • You, and your spouse or common-law partner if you have one, did not live in a home that either of you owned in the year of the purchase or the four calendar years before it. A home you sold in 2020 can still leave you eligible in 2026. The CRA's own example says so.
  • For a home from a builder, the purchase agreement is dated on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031. The date you both agreed is the one that counts, not a later date when conditions are waived.
  • If more than one person is on title, only one person claims it, and that person must meet the first-time test. A parent on title who already owns a home does not, by itself, block the claim.

The older new-housing rebate

It is 36% of the GST, to a maximum of $6,300, when the home is $350,000 or less. It shrinks between $350,000 and $450,000, and it is $0 at $450,000 or more. On a home of $450,000 or less, the two rebates together can return all of the GST. You do not receive the $6,300 as well as a full GST refund. Many new homes in the Tri-Cities, BC are over $450,000, so the older rebate is often $0.

An assignment of a contract signed before March 20, 2025 does not create a new agreement date. Ask your lawyer to read the contract if the date is close.

Page 4 · Property transfer tax

The B.C. rates, then an $800,000 resale

Property transfer tax is paid once, when the home is registered. It is not the yearly city tax bill. General rates from the Province:

1%First $200,000.
2%From $200,000 to $2 million.
3%Above $2 million.

Residential value above $3 million is charged a further 2%. A fully residential home at $4.5 million is the Province's own example: $143,000 of tax, including that further 2%.

Resale home at $800,000

$2,0001% of the first $200,000.
$12,0002% of the next $600,000.
$14,000Tax before any exemption. The Province's table shows the same figure.

A qualifying first-time buyer at this price gets an $8,000 exemption and pays $6,000. The Province's table lists that $6,000 as well.

Page 5 · Two exemptions

First-time buyers, and newly built homes

First Time Home Buyers' Program

Resale or new. You must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, have lived in B.C. for a year before registration (or filed 2 B.C. tax returns in the last 6 years), never have owned a principal residence anywhere, and use this home as your principal residence on 0.5 hectares or less.

  • $500,000 or less: the full tax can be exempt.
  • Over $500,000 up to $835,000: the exemption is $8,000. You still pay tax on the rest.
  • Over $835,000 and under $860,000: that $8,000 shrinks to zero.
  • $860,000: no exemption.

Move in within 92 days and stay through the first year.

Newly built home exemption

You do not have to be a first-time buyer. The home must be new, not yet lived in, and this must be the first registration after it was completed. Citizen or permanent resident, principal residence, 0.5 hectares or less.

  • $1,100,000 or less: the full tax is exempt. At $1,100,000 that tax is $20,000, so you pay $0.
  • Between $1,100,000 and $1,150,000 the exemption shrinks. The Province's formula is the tax times ($1,150,000 minus the price) divided by $50,000.
  • $1,150,000: no exemption. The tax is $21,000.

Same 92-day move-in rule.

On a new home of $800,000, the newly built exemption can bring the tax to $0. The first-time program at that price would leave $6,000. When both could apply, the new-home exemption removes more.

Page 6 · Saving before you buy

FHSA and the RRSP Home Buyers' Plan

First Home Savings Account

In the year you open your first FHSA, you can contribute or transfer from an RRSP up to $8,000 in total. The lifetime cap on contributions is $40,000. Contributions can be deducted on your tax return. A qualifying withdrawal to buy your first home is tax-free. Unused room can carry forward. The exact room in a later year is in your CRA account, because excess amounts change the math. You can hold more than one FHSA, but the yearly room is shared across all of them.

Home Buyers' Plan

You can withdraw up to $60,000 from your RRSPs. You repay it over 15 years. For a first withdrawal from January 1, 2026 through December 31, 2028, repayment starts in the fifth year after the year you withdrew. A 2026 withdrawal starts repayment in 2031. You can use an FHSA withdrawal and a Home Buyers' Plan withdrawal for the same home if you meet both sets of rules.

Page 7 · The lender's test, and cash at closing

Stress test, then a closing checklist

For an uninsured mortgage from a federally regulated lender, OSFI's minimum qualifying rate is the greater of your contract rate plus 2 percentage points, or 5.25%. OSFI reviews that floor and that buffer at least once a year. It does not require that set rate when you switch an uninsured mortgage to another federally regulated lender at renewal, if the loan amount and the amortization do not increase. Insured mortgages follow federal mortgage-insurance rules. Your lender applies the test that fits the loan. This is not an approval.

Cash besides the down payment

  • Property transfer tax, or an exemption if you qualify.
  • GST on a new home, and any rebate you are allowed to claim.
  • Legal or notary fees, and title insurance if you choose it.
  • A home inspection, and an appraisal if the lender asks for one.
  • Adjustments for property tax, utilities, and strata fees already paid.
  • Strata documents, including the Form B, on a condo or townhouse.
  • Home insurance in place before completion, and the cost of the move.

This list does not put a dollar amount on legal fees or inspections. Those vary. Ask for the quotes.

OSFI, “Minimum qualifying rate for uninsured mortgages.” Checked November 28, 2026.

Page 8 · The path

Steps to buy a home in B.C.

  1. Know the cash. Talk with a lender about what you can borrow. Read the tax pages in this guide before you fall for a price.
  2. Sign the paperwork with your agent. In B.C. that includes a disclosure of representation before services start, and a written agreement about how the agent is paid.
  3. Tour homes in the Tri-Cities, BC or elsewhere in Greater Vancouver. On a condo or townhouse, read the Form B, minutes, bylaws, and the depreciation report.
  4. Write an offer with subjects such as financing, inspection, and a review of strata documents and title. The deposit amount is negotiated. It is held in trust.
  5. Do the homework, then remove subjects in writing only when you are satisfied. After that, walking away can cost the deposit and more.
  6. Your lawyer or notary searches title, prepares the transfer, and calculates property transfer tax. Completion is the day money and title move. Possession is the day you get the keys. Those dates are negotiated and are not always the same day.

B.C. has a home-buyer rescission period on many residential purchases. Confirm the current window, the cost, and the exceptions with your lawyer and the BCFSA consumer guide. Subjects are still the usual way to protect yourself.
