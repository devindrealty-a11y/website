FAQ

Questions clients ask about these rates

Are these the rates I will get?

No. These rates are illustrative only. Ratehub.ca published them for the Big 5 banks on October 28, 2026, 9:14 p.m. They are not the rate you will be offered. A licensed mortgage professional or your bank sets that from your credit, income, down payment, the property, and whether the mortgage is insured.

No. I am not a mortgage broker. I am Devin Desaulniers, a Real Estate Associate with Axford Real Estate. I do not hold a mortgage licence. I can introduce you to a licensed mortgage professional. I do not take the application, and I do not approve a mortgage.

What does illustrative mean on this page?

It means the rate is an example only, copied from a public table. It is not a quote and not an approval. The 5-year variable, 5-year fixed, and 3-year fixed figures come from Ratehub's Big 5 comparison table. The 1-year fixed figures come from Ratehub's separate 1-year fixed table, checked at the same time. Open rates on that site are not shown here. The rates can change after the date on this page.

What is the difference between the term and the amortization?

The term is how long the rate agreement lasts, such as 1 year, 3 years, or 5 years. The amortization is how long it would take to pay the mortgage off, often 25 years. A 5-year term can sit inside a 25-year amortization. At the end of the term you renew, refinance, or pay the balance. The example payment on this page asks for both.

What does prime minus a number mean?

On a variable rate, Ratehub prints a percent and a spread such as Prime -0.85%. This page shows both, as labeled. The percent is illustrative only. If the lender's prime changes during the term, a variable rate can change with it. A fixed rate stays the same for the term. This page does not calculate prime on its own, and it is not a recommendation to choose variable or fixed.
