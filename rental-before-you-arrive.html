Timing

An 8-week checklist

Settle the big decisions early. The homes themselves usually show up close to the move-in date.

  1. 8 weeks out

    Decide the brief

    • Set the budget (rent, utilities and deposits), bedrooms, parking, pets, and who will live there.
    • Write down the commute you actually need: a walk to SkyTrain, the West Coast Express, or driving across a bridge.
    • Shortlist two or three areas, for example Vancouver, Burnaby and the Tri-Cities, BC. The Greater Vancouver relocating guide and the Coquitlam, Port Moody and Port Coquitlam guides are the place to start.
  2. 6 weeks out

    Build the application pack

    Landlords move fast. Have these ready as PDFs:

    • Photo ID: a clear, unexpired government-issued ID.
    • Proof of income: two or three recent pay stubs, an employment or offer letter, or a contract. If you're self-employed, recent bank statements and/or a Notice of Assessment.
    • Landlord and employer references, with a phone number or email.
    • Optional: a credit report you pull yourself (Equifax or TransUnion), which can speed an application up.
    • A short renter profile: who will live there, the move-in date, pets and must-haves.

    New to Canada, with no Canadian credit file yet? Read the newcomer rental FAQs.

  3. 4 to 6 weeks out

    Start the active search

    Most Greater Vancouver rentals are listed only a few weeks before they're available. Inside this window, search Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, other rental sites and Axford Real Estate's own rentals. Book a showing the day a fit appears. If you're still out of province, ask for a live video walkthrough and a floor plan rather than relying on photos.

  4. 2 to 4 weeks out

    View, compare and apply

    Confirm the unit, and confirm who you're dealing with (the landlord, a licensed agent or a property manager), before any money moves. Compare the commute, noise, light, parking, laundry and the lease term, not just the photos. When you want the place, send a complete application the same day.

  5. Offer week

    Sign in writing, and pay only what B.C. allows

    • Get a written tenancy agreement. Both parties sign and date it, and you keep a copy. Photograph the pages that day.
    • A security deposit can be at most half of one month's rent.
    • A pet damage deposit, if pets are allowed, can be at most another half month, however many pets you have. Together the two deposits can equal one month's rent. That is not "first and last," plus extras.
    • A landlord can't charge a fee just to accept or process an application.
    • Do a move-in condition inspection and keep the report.

    General information, not legal advice. Confirm it on the Province of B.C. deposit page and the tenancy agreement page before you rely on a number.

  6. Arrival week

    Keys, utilities and paperwork

    • Do the final walk-through and collect the keys.
    • Set up utilities and internet if they aren't included.
    • Keep the deposit receipts and the signed agreement.
Before you send money

Three signs to walk away

The rent is far below similar homes

If a place is priced well under everything else in that neighbourhood, treat that as a question, not a bargain.

No viewing, in person or on live video

A landlord who will not show you the home, even on a live video call, is asking you to pay for something you have not seen.

Wire, gift card or crypto to "hold" it

Don't send a deposit to hold a unit before the home and the person are verified.

My rule: never send a deposit before the unit and the landlord (or their licensed agent or property manager) are verified. I can view in person, or on live video with you, before you commit. FAQ

Questions before you arrive

Can I rent in Vancouver before I move?

Yes. Prepare your application pack, start the active search about 4 to 6 weeks before move-in, view homes in person or on live video, sign a written tenancy agreement, and pay only the deposits B.C. allows. A security deposit is capped at half a month's rent.

Who can help me find a rental in Vancouver if I'm still out of province?

I can. I'm Devin Desaulniers, a Real Estate Associate with Axford Real Estate in Port Moody. I search Greater Vancouver on the tenant side, including the Tri-Cities, BC, arrange showings or live video walkthroughs with floor plans, negotiate, and review the lease with you before you sign. The fee is $1,500 + GST, paid by the tenant in two parts.

How far ahead should I start?

Settle the budget, the areas and the documents early. The active search usually starts about 4 to 6 weeks before move-in, because most listings appear close to the date the home is available.

What deposit can a landlord ask for in B.C.?

At most half of one month's rent as a security deposit, and at most another half month as a pet damage deposit if pets are allowed. Landlords can't require "first and last month's rent" the way some other provinces do, and they can't charge a fee just to accept or process an application. Confirm the current rules with the B.C. Residential Tenancy Branch. This is general information, not legal advice.

Are rents in Vancouver going up or down in 2026?

Mostly flat to a little lower, depending on the city and the size. Vancouver's average asking rent for purpose-built and condo apartments was $2,735 in October 2026, up 0.2% from a year earlier. That was the first yearly increase after 34 months of declines, in the Rentals.ca and Urbanation National Rent Report published November 5, 2026. The same report's city table, which also includes houses and townhouses, shows a Vancouver one-bedroom at $2,377 (down 3.0%) and a two-bedroom at $3,270 (unchanged). Coquitlam one-bedrooms averaged $2,051, down 5.2% on the year.

Is it cheaper to rent in Coquitlam than Vancouver?

Yes, on the October 2026 averages. A one-bedroom averaged $2,051 in Coquitlam and $2,377 in Vancouver, which is $326 a month less in Coquitlam. A two-bedroom averaged $2,648 in Coquitlam and $3,270 in Vancouver, $622 less. Several Coquitlam stations are on the Millennium Line, including the Evergreen Extension, with SkyTrain into Burnaby and Vancouver. Those are average asking rents from the Rentals.ca city table, not rents on signed leases.
