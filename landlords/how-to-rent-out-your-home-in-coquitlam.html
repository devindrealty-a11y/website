Secondary suites

Secondary suites in Coquitlam

Many Coquitlam owners rent out a suite in the house they live in, or rent the whole house with its suite. The City's secondary suite FAQ sets out the basics:

  • What counts as a suite. A separate living area inside a single-family dwelling, with its own kitchen, bathroom and at least one bedroom, and at least one door that opens directly to the outside.
  • It isn't a separate title. A suite isn't a separate housing unit with its own Certificate of Title, and it can't be stratified.
  • One suite per house. A single-family dwelling may not contain more than one secondary suite.
  • A building permit makes it legal. The City says all secondary suites need building permits to be legal.
  • Parking. The City notes that one parking space for the suite is a requirement.

If you're not sure whether an existing suite was built with permits, ask the City's Building Division before you advertise it. The City's Secondary Suites page has the application details.

Business licences

Business licences and renting a whole house

I won't guess at whether your rental needs a City business licence. Before you rent out a house, a suite or a condo in Coquitlam, confirm with City of Coquitlam business licensing whether a licence applies to your situation.

This page is about long-term rentals. If you're thinking about short-term rentals, confirm the City's rules first.

Flood areas

Check whether the property is in a flood zone

The City's secondary suite brochure (PDF) reminds owners that specific areas of Coquitlam are within potential flood zones of the Coquitlam River, the Fraser and Pitt Rivers and several streams. It suggests checking your Certificate of Title for restrictions or asking City staff.

Check the specific property before you plan a suite or sign a tenancy, and talk to your insurer about what your policy covers once the home is rented.

Strata

Renting out a strata condo or townhouse in Coquitlam

Since 24 November 2022, a strata corporation in BC must not have a bylaw that restricts rentals. Bylaws that restrict short-term rentals are still allowed.

  • Other bylaws still apply to your tenant. A strata can still have bylaws about pets, smoking and similar matters, and your tenant has to follow them.
  • Age restrictions can still exist. A building with a 55+ age-restriction bylaw can keep it, which affects who you can rent to.
  • Paperwork before the tenancy. Before you rent the unit, give your tenant the strata's current bylaws and rules and a Notice of Tenant's Responsibilities, as section 146 of the Strata Property Act requires.

Don't assume every building allows every use. Read the current bylaws and rules for the specific building before you advertise. The Province explains the details on its pages about landlords and strata corporations and changes to strata legislation.

Province-wide rules

The BC tenancy rules still apply

City rules add to the provincial ones; they don't replace them. Wherever the home is, most residential tenancies in BC fall under the Residential Tenancy Act. Before you rent, read through:

  • a written tenancy agreement, using the Province's Residential Tenancy Agreement (form RTB-1);
  • deposit limits: a security deposit of no more than half of one month's rent, and a pet damage deposit of no more than half of one month's rent;
  • rent increases: once every 12 months, with at least three full months' written notice, within the yearly limit set by the Residential Tenancy Branch (check the current amount);
  • a move-in condition inspection with your tenant, recorded on a condition inspection report.

I've put these together in plain language on my BC landlord basics page. Renting in another part of the Tri-Cities, BC? See my guides for Port Moody and Port Coquitlam.

Does a secondary suite in Coquitlam need a permit?

Yes. The City's secondary suite FAQ says all secondary suites need building permits to be legal, a single-family dwelling may not contain more than one secondary suite, and one parking space is a requirement.

Do I need a business licence to rent out my home in Coquitlam?

Confirm that with City of Coquitlam business licensing before you rent. Ask about your specific situation, whether that's a whole house, a suite or a condo.

Is my Coquitlam home in a flood area?

Other bylaws, such as rules on pets and smoking, still apply to your tenant, and 55+ age-restriction bylaws can still exist.
