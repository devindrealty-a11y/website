The law

The Residential Tenancy Act and the RTB

In BC, the Residential Tenancy Act sets the rules for most residential tenancies. The Residential Tenancy Branch (RTB) publishes the forms and guidance and resolves disputes between landlords and tenants.

The Act doesn't cover every living arrangement. Some roommate situations and some strata hotel arrangements, for example, fall outside it. Before you set anything up, check whether the Act applies to your situation, starting at the Province's residential tenancies hub.

Tenancy agreement

Put the tenancy in writing

Use a written tenancy agreement. The standard terms belong in every agreement, and the Province's form already includes them. That form is the Residential Tenancy Agreement, form RTB-1 (PDF).

Write down the rent, the deposits, what's included (parking, storage, utilities, laundry), the start date and whether the tenancy is fixed-term or month-to-month. The Province's tenancy agreements page has more.

Deposits

Security and pet damage deposits

  • Security deposit: no more than half of one month's rent.
  • Pet damage deposit: if you allow pets, a separate deposit of no more than half of one month's rent.
  • One of each. You can take one security deposit and one pet damage deposit, not several.

The RTB-1 form states that the landlord keeps the deposits during the tenancy, that interest is dealt with in the regulation, and that repayment timing is set in the Act, which gives 15 days in the circumstances the form describes. The Province's page also notes that landlords can't charge a fee for accepting, reviewing or processing a rental application.

Keeping or returning deposits has its own rules. Read the Province's tenancy deposits and fees page rather than relying on a summary.

Rent increases

Raising the rent

To raise the rent for an existing tenant:

  • Wait 12 months. It must have been at least 12 months since the rent was set at the start of the tenancy or last legally increased.
  • Give three full months' notice in writing. Use the approved Notice of Rent Increase, form RTB-7 (PDF).
  • Stay within the yearly limit. The dollar amount must stay within the current limit published by the Residential Tenancy Branch. The limit changes over time, so check the current amount on the Province's rent increases page before you calculate the increase.

An increase above the yearly limit needs a different legal path, either the tenant's written agreement or an order from the RTB. The same rent increases page explains when that applies.

Move-in inspection

Do the move-in condition inspection

Before the tenant moves in, the landlord and tenant inspect the unit and record its condition together. The Province's form is the Condition Inspection Report, form RTB-27 (PDF).

Give the tenant a copy of the signed report promptly, and within 7 days of the move-in inspection, as the Residential Tenancy Regulation requires. The same report is used again at move-out, which is why it's worth doing carefully. The Province's move-in condition inspection page walks through it.

Ending a tenancy

Ending a tenancy has strict rules

Ending a tenancy, whoever starts it, has strict notice rules and approved forms. I don't give eviction advice, and this page isn't legal advice.

Use the Province's ending a tenancy guidance, and contact the Residential Tenancy Branch if you have questions about your situation.

Strata

Renting out a strata condo or townhouse

Since 24 November 2022, a strata corporation in BC must not have a bylaw that restricts rentals. Bylaws that restrict short-term rentals are still allowed.

  • Other bylaws still apply to your tenant. A strata can still have bylaws about pets, smoking and similar matters, and your tenant has to follow them.
  • Age restrictions can still exist. A building with a 55+ age-restriction bylaw can keep it, which affects who you can rent to.
  • Paperwork before the tenancy. Before you rent the unit, give your tenant the strata's current bylaws and rules and a Notice of Tenant's Responsibilities, as section 146 of the Strata Property Act requires.

Don't assume every building allows every use. Read the current bylaws and rules for the specific building before you advertise. The Province explains the details on its pages about landlords and strata corporations and changes to strata legislation.

Managing the rental

Manage it yourself, or hire a licensed manager

You can manage a home you own yourself. BCFSA's licensing information page explains that a person doesn't need a licence to provide real estate services to themselves, such as renting out their own property.

Managing a rental for someone else is different. In BC, that's a licensed real estate service under the Real Estate Services Act, overseen by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA). A REALTOR® licence for buying and selling is not the same thing as being the property manager.

If you'd rather hand the day-to-day work to someone licensed, I connect owners with Axford Property Management, my brokerage's licensed property management team. Their public site describes tenant placement, tenant screening, lease preparation, rent collection, property maintenance and financial reporting. Any management agreement, including any management fee, is between you and Axford Property Management. I make the introduction; I don't take over management.

FAQ

BC landlord basics: FAQ

Does the Residential Tenancy Act apply to every rental in BC?

No. It applies to most residential tenancies, but not to every living arrangement, for example some roommate situations and some strata hotel arrangements. Check whether the Act applies to your situation on the Province's residential tenancies pages.

Do I need a written tenancy agreement?

Yes. Put the tenancy in writing, with the standard terms included. The Province's form is the Residential Tenancy Agreement, form RTB-1.

How much can I take as a deposit?

A security deposit of no more than half of one month's rent, and, if you allow pets, a separate pet damage deposit of no more than half of one month's rent. One of each.

How much can I raise the rent?

Within the yearly limit set by the Residential Tenancy Branch, which changes over time. Check the current amount on the Province's rent increases page. It must have been at least 12 months since the rent was set or last legally increased, and you must give at least three full months' written notice on form RTB-7.

When do I give the tenant the move-in inspection report?

Promptly, and within 7 days of the move-in inspection, as the Residential Tenancy Regulation requires. The landlord and tenant record the condition of the unit together on the report.

Can you help me end a tenancy?

No. I don't give eviction advice, and this page isn't legal advice. Use the Province's ending a tenancy guidance and contact the Residential Tenancy Branch with questions.
