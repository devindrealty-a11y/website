Business licences

Business licences for rentals in Port Coquitlam

Port Coquitlam's Business Bylaw No. 3725 (PDF), section 3(d), says no licence is required for the rental of a single-family dwelling to five or fewer unrelated persons, or for the rental of fewer than five suites in a multi-family building.

A rental apartment business with five or more suites is treated differently. The City's rental housing page explains that its rules for renovations in rental apartment buildings of five or more suites don't apply to a homeowner renting out a secondary suite or coach house, or to a condo owner renting out their suite.

Bylaws change, so confirm your situation with the City before you rent.

Secondary suites

Secondary suites in Port Coquitlam

The City says secondary suites are permitted in the RS-1, RS-2, RS-3 and RS-4 zones, and a suite needs a building permit.

Check your property's zoning with the City before you plan or advertise a suite. If you're not sure an existing suite was permitted, ask before you sign a tenancy.

Floodplain

Check the floodplain and your insurance

The City's Official Community Plan (PDF) says about 45% of Port Coquitlam is in the floodplain of the Pitt, Fraser and Coquitlam Rivers, protected by a system of dikes and pump stations. My Port Coquitlam guide on the main site covers this for buyers too.

Check the specific property, and ask your insurer what your policy covers once the home is rented.

Transit

Describe transit accurately to tenants

There is no SkyTrain station inside Port Coquitlam. Port Coquitlam Station is a West Coast Express commuter rail station. The nearest SkyTrain stations are Coquitlam Central and Lincoln, both in Coquitlam.

Tenants moving from elsewhere often assume otherwise, so say plainly in your listing how a tenant would get to SkyTrain or the West Coast Express. Schedules change, so point them to TransLink rather than quoting train times.

Strata

Renting out a strata condo or townhouse in Port Coquitlam

Since 24 November 2022, a strata corporation in BC must not have a bylaw that restricts rentals. Bylaws that restrict short-term rentals are still allowed.

  • Other bylaws still apply to your tenant. A strata can still have bylaws about pets, smoking and similar matters, and your tenant has to follow them.
  • Age restrictions can still exist. A building with a 55+ age-restriction bylaw can keep it, which affects who you can rent to.
  • Paperwork before the tenancy. Before you rent the unit, give your tenant the strata's current bylaws and rules and a Notice of Tenant's Responsibilities, as section 146 of the Strata Property Act requires.

Don't assume every building allows every use. Read the current bylaws and rules for the specific building before you advertise. The Province explains the details on its pages about landlords and strata corporations and changes to strata legislation.

Province-wide rules

The BC tenancy rules still apply

City rules add to the provincial ones; they don't replace them. Wherever the home is, most residential tenancies in BC fall under the Residential Tenancy Act. Before you rent, read through:

  • a written tenancy agreement, using the Province's Residential Tenancy Agreement (form RTB-1);
  • deposit limits: a security deposit of no more than half of one month's rent, and a pet damage deposit of no more than half of one month's rent;
  • rent increases: once every 12 months, with at least three full months' written notice, within the yearly limit set by the Residential Tenancy Branch (check the current amount);
  • a move-in condition inspection with your tenant, recorded on a condition inspection report.

I've put these together in plain language on my BC landlord basics page. Renting in another part of the Tri-Cities, BC? See my guides for Coquitlam and Port Moody.
