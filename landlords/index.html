How renting out works

Renting out your home, step by step

Every home is different, but most owners work through the same decisions in roughly this order.

  • Decide whether renting is the right move. Think about how long you plan to rent, whether you might move back in or sell later, and how rent would sit against your carrying costs: mortgage, strata fees, property tax and insurance. Ask your lender, your insurer and your accountant what changes when the home becomes a rental.
  • Learn the BC rules. Most residential tenancies in BC fall under the Residential Tenancy Act, and the Residential Tenancy Branch publishes the forms and guidance. My BC landlord basics page is a plain-language starting point.
  • Choose who will manage it. You can manage a home you own yourself. Or you can hire a licensed property manager to handle the day-to-day work. Compare the time it takes, how close you live, how comfortable you are with the tenancy rules, and who will answer the call when something breaks.
  • Use a written tenancy agreement. The Province's form is the Residential Tenancy Agreement, form RTB-1. Put the rent, the deposits, what's included and the start date in writing before the tenant moves in.
  • Respect the deposit and rent-increase rules. A security deposit and a pet damage deposit are each capped at half of one month's rent. Rent can go up once every 12 months, with three full months' written notice, within the yearly limit set by the Residential Tenancy Branch. That limit changes, so check the current amount.
  • Check the property itself. City rules for suites and licences differ across the Tri-Cities, BC, and strata bylaws still matter. My city guides cover Coquitlam, Port Moody and Port Coquitlam.
Licensed management

Why a licensed property manager

In BC, managing a rental property for someone else is a licensed real estate service under the Real Estate Services Act. The BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) oversees that licensing.

An owner can manage a property they own without that licence. BCFSA's licensing information page explains that a person doesn't need a licence to provide real estate services to themselves, such as renting out their own property, and sets out the exemptions that apply in other situations: Licensing Exemptions Information (BCFSA).

A REALTOR® licence for helping people buy and sell is not the same thing as being someone's property manager. I'm licensed to help with buying and selling. I don't manage rentals, collect rent, screen tenants or hold deposits for owners.

Axford Property Management is the property management division of Axford Real Estate, my brokerage, at the same Port Moody office. Axford Real Estate also describes them on its property management page. Ask them directly what is included, what it costs and how their management agreement works.

Any management agreement, including any management fee, is between you and Axford Property Management. My part is the introduction. I don't take over management, and I can't promise how a tenancy will go.

Can I manage the rental myself?

Yes. In BC, an owner doesn't need a real estate licence to rent out and look after a property they own. You'll be the landlord under the Residential Tenancy Act, so read the Residential Tenancy Branch's guidance before you start. If you'd rather have someone licensed handle the day-to-day work, I can connect you with Axford Property Management.

What is the most a landlord can take as a security deposit in BC?

A security deposit can be no more than half of one month's rent. If you allow pets, you can also take a separate pet damage deposit of no more than half of one month's rent. The Province's deposits page has the details.

How much can rent go up each year?

The yearly limit is set by the Residential Tenancy Branch and changes over time, so check the current amount on the Province's rent increases page before you give notice. Rent can go up only once every 12 months, with at least three full months' written notice on the approved form.
