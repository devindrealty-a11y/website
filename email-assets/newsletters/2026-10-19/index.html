Monday’s inflation number

Statistics Canada reported on Monday that the Consumer Price Index rose 3.1% in the year to September, up from 3.0% in August. Gasoline did most of the lifting. Excluding gasoline, inflation was 2.2%. In British Columbia, the yearly pace stayed at 3.0%.

The Bank of Canada policy rate is 2.25%. The next decision is Wednesday, October 28, and the Monetary Policy Report comes out the same morning. The Bank says a change takes effect the following day. If you are lining up an offer, that is the date I would circle.

What the Oct 28 decision means for you

Wednesday’s announcement can go one of three ways. Here is what each one usually means for a payment, and for people buying or selling around here. One decision does not reset prices on its own.

CUT

The policy rate would move below 2.25%. Variable-rate mortgages and many lines of credit usually follow, so those payments can ease a little. Fixed rates follow bond yields, which often move before the announcement. Buyers may qualify for a bit more, and sellers can see a few more showings. Prices do not jump just because the rate did.

HOLD

The rate stays at 2.25%. Variable payments stay where they are. Buyers can plan with the budget they already have. Sellers should price from recent sales, not from a cut that has not happened.

RAISE

Variable payments can go up, and some buyers pause or qualify for less. Showings can slow, especially on condos. Sellers who need to move do better pricing for the buyers who are out now. One increase is not a collapse.
